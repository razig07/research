# Music, Focus and Maladaptive Daydreaming: What Phonk, Hardstyle, Jumpstyle, Rap, Sped-Up, Slowed + Reverb and TikTok Audios Do to Cognitive Work

Scope note: these notes cover only the cognitive and behavioural evidence. The religious ruling on music is out of scope and is not covered. Evidence strength is tagged as **[META]** (meta-analysis or systematic review), **[STUDY]** (a single experiment or survey), **[QUAL]** (qualitative or interview study), or **[CULTURE]** (journalism, music-education blogs or producer commentary, not peer-reviewed). Research current to October 2026, within the limits of what could be retrieved.

---

## 1. Background music and cognitive performance: what do the meta-analyses find?

### Takeaway
Across meta-analyses, the *average* effect of background music on cognition is close to zero, but that average hides task-specific harm. Background music reliably hurts reading comprehension and verbal memory, mostly because of **lyrics**. Instrumental music is roughly neutral on average. The one recent meta-analysis that found a small positive effect for "learning" (d ≈ 0.31) leaned heavily on music played *before* the test and on classical music, so it does not describe lyrical, high-energy music playing *during* verbal work.

### Cited Findings
- **[META] Kämpfe, Sedlmeier & Renkewitz (2011), Psychology of Music.** The overall effect of background music versus no music is essentially null (weighted mean **r = .03**). The authors attribute the null to "averaging out specific effects": background music **disturbs the reading process**, has **small detrimental effects on memory**, but **improves emotional reactions and sports performance**. Comparisons between different music conditions gave r = .17. — [Kämpfe et al. 2011, SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0305735610376261); [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221898291_The_impact_of_background_music_on_adult_listener_A_meta-analysis)
- **[META] Vasilev, Kirkby & Angele (2018), Perspectives on Psychological Science: a Bayesian meta-analysis of 65 studies on auditory distraction during reading.** Background noise, speech and music all have a **small but reliably detrimental** effect on reading. The overall effect of music on reading comprehension was **d ≈ −0.19**. **Lyrical music d ≈ −0.35 versus instrumental ≈ 0.** Meta-regression put the lyrical-versus-instrumental gap at d ≈ −0.19. Lyrical music was **as distracting as intelligible background speech**, and these two were the most distracting sound types. — [Bournemouth eprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/); [data/code repo](https://github.com/martin-vasilev/reading_sounds); summarised in [Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273)
- **[META/systematic review] de la Mora Velasco & Hirumi (2020), Educational Technology Research & Development.** Reviewed 30 studies (2008–2018). Findings were **inconclusive**: benefits for fact memorisation were rare and disruption was more common. The authors blame poor reporting of the music's characteristics for the equivocal results. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-020-09783-4); [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1278005); summarised in [Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273)
- **[META] de la Mora Velasco, Chen, Hirumi & Bai (2023), Psychology of Music: 47 studies, 71 effect sizes, random effects.** Found a **small positive mean effect (d = 0.314)** favouring background music for *learning*, a **medium positive effect when the music was played *before* the learning assessment**, a small positive effect for factual retention, a small advantage for **classical** music over other genres, and age as a possible moderator. — [SAGE abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/03057356231153070); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369490840_The_impact_of_background_music_on_learners_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis)
  - *Conflict to note:* this positive result sits against the null or negative results of Kämpfe 2011 and Vasilev 2018. The difference likely comes from what was pooled (pre-task music, classical music, learning outcomes rather than concurrent reading or verbal tasks). See Inferences.
- **[META/systematic review] Cheah, Wong, Spitzer & Coutinho (2022), Music & Science.** A systematic review of how task, music and population shape background-music effects. The literature's reporting is poor: **94% of studies did not report effect sizes** and only **16% reported exact p-values** for all results. In the review's vote-count analysis of memory tasks, **lyrical music versus silence** showed **12 of 59 tests (20.3%)** counted as "successes", a significant departure from chance (p < .001). **Instrumental music versus silence** showed **50 of 91 (55%)**, which was not significant. — [Cheah et al. 2022, SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20592043221134392); [dataset record](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3166445/1/zenodo.org-Background%20music%20and%20cognitive%20task%20performance%20systematic%20review%20dataset.pdf) (the full text was paywalled, see Gaps)
- **[STUDY, recent, well-powered] Souza & Barbosa (2023), Journal of Cognition, ~113–123 students.** Compared silence, instrumental lo-fi hip-hop and lyrical Portuguese pop on four tasks. Lyrics versus silence: verbal recall **d = −.32**, visual recall **d = −.33**, reading comprehension **d = −.19**, arithmetic **d = −.11**. Instrumental versus silence: −.16, −.23, +.14 and −.05 respectively. Conclusion: **music with lyrics consistently hindered performance; instrumental effects were negligible.** — [Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10162369/)
- **[STUDY] Creativity, Threadgold et al. (2019), Applied Cognitive Psychology.** On compound remote associate tasks (insight or convergent creativity), music with **familiar lyrics, foreign lyrics, and instrumental music all significantly impaired performance compared with quiet**. Experiment 3 showed the impairment held **whether or not the music lifted mood and whether or not participants habitually studied with music**. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532); [open PDF](http://clok.uclan.ac.uk/25989/8/25989%20Threadgold_et_al-2019-Applied_Cognitive_Psychology.pdf); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/02/190227081542.htm)
- **[STUDY] Creativity, Ritter & Ferguson (2017), PLOS ONE.** "Happy" classical music (positive and high-arousal) played *during* a **divergent**-thinking task raised creativity compared with silence. **No effect on convergent creativity.** — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0182210)

### Inferences
- For this user's core work (script writing, reading research, ideation, AI prompting and business thinking, all verbal and semantic), the strongest evidence points against **lyrical** background music: rap, TikTok audios, and sped-up or slowed pop and rap with intelligible vocals. The expected effect is a small-to-moderate drop (d ≈ −0.2 to −0.35) in reading and verbal memory, and convergent or insight creativity is also likely to suffer.
- The positive 2023 meta-analysis is not a licence for high-BPM lyrical music during work. Its gains concentrate on music heard *before* a task (an arousal and mood boost) and on classical music.
- Ritter & Ferguson suggest a narrow possible benefit: upbeat **instrumental** music during *loose brainstorming* (divergent thinking). Threadgold et al. show the opposite for "finding the one right idea" (convergent or insight) tasks. Ideation that needs filtering and selecting is better done in quiet.

### Gaps
- The Cheah et al. (2022) full text was paywalled (HTTP 403). The exact definition of "success" in its binomial vote-count could not be checked. Given the rest of the literature, the pattern (lyrical 20% versus instrumental 55%) most plausibly means lyrical music rarely performs as well as silence. Treat that reading as provisional.
- No meta-analysis dated 2024–2026 that is specific to concurrent background music and adult cognition was found. The newest pooled estimates retrieved are 2023 (de la Mora Velasco et al.) and 2024 (Nigg et al. on noise, see Section 4).
- No study tested phonk, hardstyle, jumpstyle or "edit" audios as background music. Inferences about them are extrapolated from tempo, loudness and lyric findings.

## 2. The irrelevant sound effect, changing-state sound, and why lyrics in a language you understand disrupt writing and reading

### Takeaway
Two separate mechanisms are at work. (1) The **changing-state** irrelevant sound effect: any sound that changes from moment to moment (beats, syllables, melodic jumps) disrupts tasks that need you to hold things *in order* in verbal working memory, whatever the content. (2) **Semantic interference**: when the task is about *meaning* (reading comprehension, writing a script), intelligible words in the background compete for the same meaning-processing system. Rap, with dense, intelligible, fast-changing words in the listener's own language, hits both mechanisms at once. Liking the song does not protect you.

### Cited Findings
- **[STUDY + review in intro] Perham & Currie (2014), Applied Cognitive Psychology.** The irrelevant sound effect (ISE) is "one of the most commonly researched auditory distraction phenomena": poorer performance with background sound than in quiet. Two conditions are needed: the sound must contain **changing-state** information (e.g., "n, r, p…" disrupts but "c, c, c…" does not), and the task must involve **seriation** (keeping order). For plain serial recall, the **content does not matter**: a foreign language disrupts as much as a known one (Jones, Miles & Page 1990). But **when the task requires semantic processing, semantic content in the sound matters**: Martin et al. (1988) for reading comprehension and Marsh et al. (2008, 2009) for category recall found worse performance with lyrical music or English words than with non-lyrical music, reversed speech or quiet. — [Perham & Currie 2014 PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/acp.2994)
- **Perham & Currie (2014), the experiment.** N = 30 native English speakers did SAT reading comprehension under four conditions: quiet, **liked lyrical music** (their own picks, e.g., One Direction, Frank Ocean, Katy Perry), disliked lyrical music (thrash metal), and non-lyrical music, played at **65–75 dB(A)** through headphones.
  - Main effect of sound: **F(3,87) = 8.05, η² = .22**. Quiet beat disliked lyrical music (η² = .37) and beat *liked* lyrical music (η² = .26). **Quiet and non-lyrical music did not differ, and liked and disliked lyrical music did not differ.**
  - Conclusion: liked lyrical music is "equally as disruptive as disliked music" for reading.
  - Participants knew it: they rated liked lyrical music **75/100 distracting** against **10/100 for quiet**.
  - The authors' mechanism: the brain must *inhibit* the plausible-but-wrong semantic candidates (the lyrics), and "this comes at a cost, which is the loss of efficient processing of the semantic content of the text."
  - The study builds on Perham & Vizard (2011), which found that liked and disliked music both impaired serial recall. — [Perham & Currie 2014 PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)
- **[META] Vasilev et al. (2018):** intelligible speech and lyrical music produced the largest reading distraction, and lyrical music was as harmful as intelligible background speech. — [Bournemouth eprint](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/); [summary in Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273)
- **[STUDY] Souza & Barbosa (2023):** lyrics hurt verbal recall (d = −.32) and reading (d = −.19), and also visual recall (d = −.33), so the cost extends beyond purely verbal tasks. — [Journal of Cognition](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273)
- **[STUDY] Threadgold et al. (2019):** creativity impairment occurred with **familiar-language lyrics, foreign lyrics, and instrumental music** alike, compared with quiet. For insight problem-solving, even non-lyrical changing-state sound costs something. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)

### Inferences
- **Script writing is the worst-case task for rap.** Composing sentences uses the same phonological loop (inner voice) and semantic system that rap lyrics occupy. Rap is high in word density and changing state, and is in a language he understands. TikTok audios with speech or vocals and sped-up or slowed songs with intelligible lyrics behave the same way, because speeding up or slowing down does not remove intelligibility unless the vocals become unrecognisable.
- **Phonk** often uses chopped, pitched or distorted Memphis-rap vocal samples, and many drift or gym phonk tracks are mostly instrumental. This likely lowers *semantic* interference relative to rap, but the cowbell patterns, 808 slides and vocal chops are strongly **changing-state**, so phonk still loads the serial and verbal working-memory mechanism (extrapolated; no direct study).
- "But I like it / I'm used to it" is not protective: liked music was as disruptive as disliked music (Perham & Currie), and habitual study-with-music listeners were still impaired on creativity (Threadgold Exp. 3).

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly tests **writing or script composition** (as opposed to reading, recall or creativity tasks) under rap or other lyrical music. The inference to writing rests on shared verbal working-memory mechanisms.
- Studies of first-language versus second-language lyrics exist (e.g., [Ding et al. 2025, Applied Cognitive Psychology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.70104); [2024 study on lyrics language and study habits](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379617587_Impact_of_background_music_on_reading_comprehension_influence_of_lyrics_language_and_study_habits)), but their detailed results were not retrieved.

## 3. Tempo, loudness and arousal: what does high-BPM, bass-heavy, aggressive music do to complex versus simple work?

### Takeaway
Music works partly through **arousal and mood**. A moderate arousal and mood boost can help, especially for dull low-demand tasks or when the music comes *before* the task. Music that is **both fast and loud** drives arousal past the optimum for complex comprehension: in the key experiment, only the **fast + loud** combination pushed reading comprehension significantly below baseline. Phonk (drift or gym ~140–170 BPM), hardstyle (~150–160 BPM), jumpstyle (~140–150 BPM) and nightcore or sped-up edits (~35% faster than the original) all fall in the fast, loud, distorted, high-arousal corner.

### Cited Findings
- **[STUDY] Thompson, Schellenberg & Husain (2001), Psychological Science, the arousal-mood hypothesis.** The "Mozart effect" on spatial tasks appeared only after an energetic, pleasant Mozart sonata, not after a slow, sad Albinoni adagio, and tracked changes in **enjoyment, arousal and mood**. The effect is "an artifact of arousal and mood," not of Mozart. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Arousal,-Mood,-and-The-Mozart-Effect-Thompson-Schellenberg/bae215c23ade46b72bc944257cbdc144e1c23b2e); [Macquarie record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/arousal-mood-and-the-mozart-effect/). Perham & Currie also summarise that the benefits come from "an increase in arousal and mood because of listening to preferred music" *prior to* the task. — [Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)
- **[STUDY] Thompson, Schellenberg & Letnic (2012), Psychology of Music, 40(6), 700–708.** Instrumental background music was crossed for tempo (slow/fast) and intensity (low/high), with 4 minutes to read a passage and 3 minutes for six multiple-choice questions. There was **a significant tempo × intensity interaction, and comprehension in the fast/high condition fell significantly below baseline.** Instrumental background music is "most likely to disrupt reading comprehension when the music is fast and loud." — [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ982853); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230746037_Fast_and_loud_background_music_disrupts_reading_comprehension)
- **[STUDY] Kiss & Linnell (2021) and Kiss, Linnell & Szikora (2024): the counterpoint for simple tasks.** On a **low-demand** sustained-attention (vigilance) task, self-selected or preferred background music **decreased mind-wandering and increased task-focus**.
  - The 2024 pupillometry study (n = 39 students who habitually use background music) found larger pre-trial pupil diameter (higher arousal) with music. It concluded the music-driven **arousal increase** explains the attentional shift and supports an **inverted-U**: task-focus sits at intermediate arousal and mind-wandering at low arousal. Music had **no effect on external distraction**.
  - The authors caution that the findings apply only to people who use background music, and only to a simple task; generalising to complex tasks remains to be done. — [Kiss & Linnell 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343410236_The_effect_of_preferred_background_music_on_task-focus_in_sustained_attention); [Kiss, Linnell & Szikora 2024, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11281972/); [related 2024 paper on mood and arousal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38664478)
- **[STUDY] Kiss & Linnell (2023), Psychology of Music, "Making sense of background music listening habits: An arousal and task-complexity account".** The article frames habitual music choice as a matter of arousal regulation and task complexity: people adapt what they play, or switch it off, depending on the task. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/03057356221089017) (full text 403; framing taken from the title and from the search summary)
- **[STUDY] Heroic music raises arousal.** In Koelsch et al. (2019), heart rate was higher during heroic than sad music, and heroic music made participants feel "more inspired, active, alert, and less afraid." — [Koelsch et al. 2019, Scientific Reports](https://stefan-koelsch.de/papers/koelsch_bashevkin_kristensen_tvedt_jentschke_2019_heroic_music_stimulates_empowering_thoughts_during_mind-wandering_scientific_reports_91_10317.pdf)
- **[CULTURE] Genre tempos and features:**
  - **Hardstyle:** modern hardstyle is typically produced around **150 BPM**, with tempos rising historically from **135–150** to **150–160 BPM**. Its signature is "overdriven and hard-sounding kick drums," an off-beat "reverse bass," and distorted synth leads. — [Wikipedia: Hardstyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardstyle)
  - **Jumpstyle:** typically **140–150 BPM**, with hard, bouncy, gabber-oriented 909 kicks, distorted sounds, four-on-the-floor beats and repetitive melodies. — [MasterClass jumpstyle guide](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/jumpstyle-music-guide); [Soundtrap blog](https://blog.soundtrap.com/what-is-jumpstyle-music/)
  - **Phonk:** built on samples of 1990s Memphis rap, distorted 808 bass and cowbell rhythms. Original Memphis rap ran ~**60–90 BPM**. **Drift phonk ~140–170 BPM** is the most globally popular variant. "**Gym phonk**" runs ~**140–165 BPM** "with maximum aggression for workout motivation," with heavy distortion, rapid cowbells and 808 drops timed for heavy lifts. — [Futureproof Music School](https://futureproofmusicschool.com/blog/what-is-phonk); [Beatstorapon](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/phonk-music/); [Melodics producer guide](https://melodics.com/blog/producers-guide-to-phonk-music) (music-education or production blogs, so lower reliability)
  - **Nightcore / sped-up:** songs are sped up **by roughly 35%**, like a 33⅓-RPM record played at 45 RPM, which raises both tempo and pitch. — [ABC News (Australia)](https://www.abc.net.au/news/103077116)

### Inferences
- **For complex cognitive work (scripting, research reading, strategic thinking, careful prompting),** the user's high-energy genres combine every risk factor in the literature: **fast** (140–170 BPM), **loud and distorted** (overdriven kicks, 808s), **changing-state** (cowbell rolls, vocal chops), and often **lyrical** (rap, sped-up pop). Thompson et al. (2012) found that fast + loud was the *only* combination that dropped reading below baseline, even without lyrics.
- **For simple, repetitive tasks (mechanical editing: cutting silences, syncing B-roll, colour or exporting),** the general-population evidence (Kiss & Linnell) suggests preferred, arousing music can *reduce* mind-wandering by lifting low arousal. This is the one context where high-energy instrumental music has some research support. **For an MDer this may not transfer**: in Somer (2024), music was itself the main daydream trigger (Section 5), so the music that lifts arousal can also launch a fantasy.
- **Using music before work versus during work:** the arousal-mood evidence (Thompson 2001; the 2023 meta-analysis's larger effect for pre-task music) suggests a short burst of energising music *before* a session captures most of the motivational benefit without the concurrent interference. This is an extrapolation and was not tested in MDers.
- **Sped-up edits** take a song the listener already knows and make it ~35% faster and higher-pitched. That raises arousal and the rate of change, and keeps the lyrics intelligible, so it plausibly *adds* interference compared with the original (extrapolated; no direct study).

### Gaps
- The exact tempo (BPM) and dB values used by Thompson et al. (2012) were not retrieved; the abstract says the manipulations were "psychologically equivalent in magnitude".
- No controlled study was found on extreme-tempo electronic genres (≥140 BPM) or heavily distorted bass as concurrent background for verbal tasks. The 2024 systematic review on music, autonomic arousal and attention ([International Journal of Psychophysiology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167876024000321)) was identified but its results were not retrieved.

## 4. Individual differences: introversion and extraversion, working memory, ADHD, and white noise

### Takeaway
People differ. **Introverts** are more impaired by background music than extraverts. Noise helps some people with **ADHD or high inattention** slightly (white or pink noise meta-analysis: g ≈ +0.25), but the same noise **slightly hurts people without ADHD** (g ≈ −0.21). There is no controlled evidence for brown noise. None of this evidence covers music, and none of it covers MD specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **[STUDY series] Furnham and colleagues.**
  - Furnham & Bradley (1997): 10 introverts and 10 extraverts did reading comprehension and memory tasks. **Introverts performed significantly worse with music**, while the two groups did not differ in silence.
  - Furnham & Allass (1999): as music complexity rose, introverts' performance fell while extraverts' rose.
  - Replications include Dobbs, Furnham & McClelland (2011), Kou et al. (2018) with Chinese participants, Lim et al. (2022) cross-cultural, and Avila et al. (2012) with familiar vocal music. — [Furnham & Bradley 1997, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0720(199710)11:5%3C445::AID-ACP472%3E3.0.CO;2-R); [Furnham & Allass 1999](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0984(199901/02)13:1%3C27::AID-PER318%3E3.0.CO;2-R); [Dobbs et al. 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/acp.1692); [Lim et al. 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/03057356211013502); [Avila et al. 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735611422672). Perham & Currie also summarise this finding: "introverts, compared with extraverts, tend to be impaired on tasks accompanied by background music." — [PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)
- **[META] Nigg et al. (2024), Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry: white and pink noise in youth with ADHD or elevated attention problems.**
  - Sample: **13 studies, N = 335**, children and college-age young adults.
  - Result: a **small significant benefit of white or pink noise on task performance, g = 0.249 (95% CI 0.135–0.363)**.
  - **Non-ADHD comparison groups performed *worse* with noise (g = −0.212).**
  - OHSU's release notes a hearing-damage risk if volume isn't monitored, and that the long-term effects of frequent use are unknown. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890856724000741); [OHSU news](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/white-and-pink-noise-show-promise-in-enhancing-attention-in-those-with-adhd/)
- **Brown noise:** the meta-analysis covered only white and pink noise. A secondary source states that no brown-noise ADHD studies met inclusion standards. — [OHSU news](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows); [pinknoisesleep.com (secondary, lower reliability)](https://pinknoisesleep.com/brown-noise-adhd-focus)
- **[THEORY + STUDY] Söderlund's moderate brain arousal model and stochastic resonance.** The theory: random noise can boost weak neural signals, raising under-aroused systems toward an optimum. Söderlund's group's 2024 clinical ADHD study found the benefit of auditory noise is **driven mainly by inattention traits**, while **hyperactivity-impulsivity may be linked to *worse* performance with noise**. — [Sensory white noise in clinical ADHD, PMC 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11585357/); [Frontiers 2023 paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1301771/pdf)
- **[STUDY] Null result:** auditory and visual white noise had **no effect on oculomotor control** in children with ADHD (Jostrup et al. 2024, Journal of Attention Disorders). — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10870547241273249)

### Inferences
- If the user has **no ADHD diagnosis**, the best estimate is that steady noise is neutral to slightly harmful for him, and music, being more changing-state and more emotional than noise, would be more harmful still. If he has marked **inattention** traits (common alongside MD), low-volume white or pink noise is the one sound with meta-analytic support. That support is for noise only, not for music.
- Inverted-U logic (Kiss & Linnell; Söderlund) suggests that people who are *under*-aroused may benefit from moderate stimulation. Fast, loud, aggressive music pushes far past "moderate".

### Gaps
- No study was found on how working memory capacity moderates background music effects for complex tasks. This was not retrieved, though the theory predicts low-capacity individuals are more vulnerable.
- There is no evidence on noise or music specifically in people with MD. MD and ADHD symptoms overlap, but the ADHD noise findings cannot be assumed to apply.

## 5. Music and mind-wandering, daydreaming and maladaptive daydreaming

### Takeaway
Music measurably **increases the vividness of imagination** (about 2.7× compared with silence in a directed-imagery task) and **steers the content and emotional tone** of spontaneous thought. Sad music increases mind-wandering. Heroic or empowering music produces "empowering, motivating" fantasies, with only ~2% of thoughts involving logical reasoning. In MD research, music is formally part of the proposed diagnostic criteria and the scale (two of the 16 MDS-16 items). Interviews show MDers use music as a **trigger, soundtrack, pacing driver and immersion deepener**, and that music **weakens their sense of control** over starting and stopping daydreams.

### Cited Findings
- **[THEORY] Juslin & Västfjäll (2008), extended by Juslin (2013): the BRECVEMA framework.** It names eight mechanisms by which music induces emotion: Brain-stem reflex, Rhythmic entrainment, Evaluative conditioning, Contagion, **Visual imagery**, Episodic memory, Musical expectancy and Aesthetic judgment. In the visual imagery mechanism, music evokes inner images that carry emotion. Imagery, episodic memory and conditioning all link the music to *non-musical* associations. — [Juslin, Towards a unified theory of musical emotions (PDF)](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Readings/Unified%20theory%20of%20musical%20emoitons%20%20Juslin.pdf); [Frontiers 2022 summary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.822264/full)
- **[STUDY] Taruffi, Pehrs, Skouras & Koelsch (2017), Scientific Reports.** Sad music produced **more mind-wandering** than happy music, of a self-referential, emotional kind. Happy music increased **meta-awareness** and focus on the music itself. In the fMRI arm (n = 24, 4-minute blocks with tempo matched, eyes closed), default-mode-network hubs showed greater centrality during sad music. — [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-14849-0); [Durham repository](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1339522/effects-of-sad-and-happy-music-on-mind-wandering-and-the-default-mode-network)
- **[STUDY] Koelsch, Bashevkin, Kristensen, Tvedt & Jentschke (2019), Scientific Reports, "Heroic music stimulates empowering thoughts during mind-wandering".**
  - Design: **n = 62** heard 2-minute heroic and sad excerpts matched in tempo, loudness and orchestration.
  - **Mind-wandering occurred after 73.1% of trials** (heroic 72.6%, sad 73.7%).
  - Heroic music produced thoughts that were more **positive, exciting, constructive and motivating**. Free-response valence differed by **d = 0.96** and arousal by **d = 1.21**.
  - **Only about 2% of reported thoughts involved logical reasoning.** — [PDF](https://stefan-koelsch.de/papers/koelsch_bashevkin_kristensen_tvedt_jentschke_2019_heroic_music_stimulates_empowering_thoughts_during_mind-wandering_scientific_reports_91_10317.pdf)
- **[STUDY] Herff, Cecchetti, Taruffi & Déguernel (2021), Scientific Reports, directed imagery ("imagined journeys").** Every music condition made imagery more vivid than silence: **on average 2.7 times more vivid**. Music also made sentiment more positive and changed the imagined journey's time and space. Music's influence on imagination therefore reaches beyond undirected mind-wandering into goal-directed imagery. — [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-95260-8); [Durham record](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1231758/music-influences-vividness-and-content-of-imagined-journeys-in-a-directed-visual-imagery-task)
- **[STUDY] Follow-ups:** eye closure interacts with music to raise vividness further (Herff et al. 2022). Even **task-irrelevant** music shapes the thematic content of imagery (Taruffi, Ayyildiz & Herff 2023). Music can bias imagery toward **social interactions** compared with silence (Scientific Reports 2025). — [Herff et al. 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20592043221142711); [Taruffi et al. 2023, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620066/); [Solitary silence and social sounds 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-10309-2)
- **[SCALE/CRITERIA] Music in the MD definition.** The original 14-item MDS (2016) had **no** music items. After "consistent guidance from members of the MD community," the **MDS-16** added two:
  - Item 1: "Some people notice that certain music can trigger their daydreaming. To what extent does music activate your daydreaming?"
  - Item 16: "To what extent is your daydreaming dependent on continued listening to music?"
  - The proposed diagnostic criteria (Somer, Soffer-Dudek et al. 2017) require at least one additional feature, such as **"daydreaming triggered, sustained, or intensified by music exposure or stereotypical movements (e.g., pacing, rocking, hand movements)."** — [Somer 2024 PDF](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf); [MDS-16](https://www.somer.co.il/images/MD/Eng-MDS-16.pdf)
- **[REVIEW] Bigelsen et al. (2016) / formulation model.** MD is "often triggered through exposure to vivid imagery, **evocative music** or stereotypical movement which also serve to deepen immersion." — [Cambridge, psychological formulation model of MD](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9); [Bigelsen et al. 2016 record](https://philpapers.org/rec/BIGMDE)
- **[QUAL] Somer (2024), Psychology of Music, "Calling the tune in maladaptive daydreaming": the only dedicated study of music in MD.**
  - Sample: **41 people with probable MD** (MDS-16 ≥ 40; mean **61.4**, SD 12.4), asynchronous email interviews.
  - Demographics: **11 male**, mean age **31.3**, from five continents. **46% were unemployed** and fewer than 15% were in a relationship.
  - Themes: **Music enhances the MD experience** (most data), plus two outlier themes, *Music necessary* and *Music not desired*. The subthemes were:
    - Music avoided in "low energy" scenes.
    - **Music as white noise**, which isolates the person from the external world: "having one of my senses completely occupied … allows me to slip even deeper." One participant said: "If I listen to music on any occasion, I immediately feel a big urge to pace around."
    - Music enhances MD creativity.
    - **Music as an MD trigger compromises the sense of agency**: "The music triggers the daydream. If there's music on, I'll be daydreaming." "I can keep myself from daydreaming so long as I maintain concentration, but in the presence of music, that becomes difficult." "Music is my number one daydream trigger." "It's harder to come out of daydreaming if I'm listening to music. I can continue for hours."
    - **Music as the emotional "soundtrack"**: "Music typically guides the topic, tone, and pace of the daydream … intense music may induce a daydream about two characters having a brawl … characters may hit each other to the beats of the song … may cause me to daydream even when I am trying to maintain focus." Another participant: "I may daydream about running or fighting when I listen to upbeat music or epic movie soundtracks."
  - **Mood-regulation loop:** a participant starts with "extremely sad or angry music," vents through MD, then moves to romantic or devotional music: "Music is like a mood controller."
  - **"Music necessary":** "Without music or movement, it is very irritating. It's like having an itch that you cannot scratch."
  - Somer's conclusion: music plays a **fourfold role**. It distances the person from external reality, triggers complex visual storylines, deepens absorption, and intensifies vividness through emotion. He describes the relationship as concerning, "addictive or dependent".
  - Clinical suggestion: test **stimulus-control** approaches, i.e., controlling exposure to music cues. — [Somer 2024 PDF](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03057356231222290); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/new-psychology-study-sheds-light-on-musics-role-in-maladaptive-daydreaming/)
- **Context:** the Reddit MD community had **over 100,500 members** (July 2023), per Somer. — [Somer 2024 PDF](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)

### Inferences
- **"Using music to amplify daydreams when work gets uncomfortable"** matches Somer's mood-regulation and trigger themes exactly. The music's job in that moment is the opposite of focus: it distances him from external reality and makes the fantasy more vivid (2.7× more vivid imagery in Herff et al.).
- **Phonk, hardstyle and jumpstyle as "power" music** are the closest real-world analogue to Koelsch's "heroic" music. They are likely to produce **empowering, exciting, self-as-protagonist fantasies** (winning, fighting, being admired), the kind Somer's participants describe ("running or fighting" with upbeat music, "characters hit each other to the beats"). This is extrapolated; the genres were not tested.
- **Slowed + reverb** edits sit at the *sad, nostalgic, low-arousal* end. Taruffi 2017 shows this kind of music *increases* mind-wandering and inward, self-referential thought. So both ends of his playlist feed MD by different routes: power fantasies at the fast end, nostalgic emotional fantasies at the slow end.
- **Movement:** high-BPM, beat-driven music invites pacing and rhythmic movement (rhythmic entrainment in BRECVEMA; Somer's "urge to pace"). Kinesthetic activity is a core MD maintenance behaviour.

### Gaps
- **No percentage was found** for how many MDers report music as a trigger in Bigelsen et al. (2016), Bigelsen & Schupak (2011), or the MDS-16 validation. The primary PDFs with item-level statistics were not retrieved. The claim that music is "the most commonly reported MD trigger" could therefore not be checked with a number. It is supported only qualitatively (Somer 2024; MDS-16 item content; the formulation-model review).
- The evidence on music in MD is **qualitative (n = 41)**. Somer himself calls for hypothesis-driven controlled studies. No experiment has yet manipulated music in MDers.
- [van der Walle, Wu, Margulis & Jakubowski (2025), "Thoughtscapes in music … across 17 genres"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03057356251346654) may contain genre-specific thought-type data, but it was not retrieved.

## 6. The genre-specific angle: slowed + reverb, sped-up or nightcore, TikTok loops and phonk's "power" identity

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study was found on slowed + reverb, nightcore, or TikTok audio loops as cognitive stimuli. Credible music journalism describes slowed + reverb as producing **"false nostalgia"** and escapism: a sleepy, sad, spacious sound that listeners use to "transport" themselves to memories and places they never had. That is close to a description of daydream fuel. Sped-up edits are designed for **instant attention capture** in short loops. Phonk is culturally tied to **gym, drift and "edit" content** built around aggression and power.

### Cited Findings
- **[CULTURE] Slowed + reverb origins:** a simplified descendant of DJ Screw's 1990s Houston "chopped and screwed" technique. The modern form was popularised by Houston producer **Slater**, whose slowed + reverb version of Lil Uzi Vert's "20 Minutes" went viral on YouTube in **2017**. The format surged during the 2020–21 lockdowns. — [Clash Magazine, Laura Molloy, 20 Apr 2021](https://clashmusic.com/features/how-slowed-reverb-became-the-soundtrack-to-gen-z-lockdown); [The Root on DJ Screw's legacy](https://theroot.com/dj-screws-legacy-is-being-celebrated-after-tiktok-teens-1844726412)
- **[CULTURE] Why it feels dreamy or nostalgic.**
  - Producer **Mr. Wax** calls it **"false nostalgia"**: "You're not quite sure why it makes you feel like that, but it does. It's a very infectious feeling."
  - Producer **Snowdream**: "There's a sort of sleepy, sad quality that people gravitate to, which is likely exacerbated by poor mental health."
  - Creator **Slowerbed** (140,000+ followers): "People might be very bored or sad … They find their escape in slowed songs which make you relax."
  - Listeners describe mourning "parties they never attended or slow drives through cities they never visited." — [Clash Magazine 2021](https://clashmusic.com/features/how-slowed-reverb-became-the-soundtrack-to-gen-z-lockdown)
  - A student essay (University of Maryland's *Diamondback*) describes it as "a gate to presently living in memories that were cut short," a soundtrack for Gen Z "who felt lost." — [DBK News, Marlia Nash, 4 Nov 2021](https://dbknews.com/2021/11/04/slow-reverb-remixes-nostalgia) (personal essay)
- **[CULTURE] Nightcore and sped-up.** Nightcore speeds songs up by ~**35%**. The concept began in **2002** as a school project by Norwegian students (Thomas S. Nilsen and Steffen Ojala Søderholm) and spread on YouTube from ~2009. On TikTok, **#spedupsongs and #speedupsongs had 11.2 billion and 12.9 billion views**. A social-media manager quoted by ABC said: "you get immediately sucked in … don't have the time to think about it." Sped-up versions helped push Steve Lacy's "Bad Habit" to No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100. — [ABC News (Australia)](https://www.abc.net.au/news/103077116); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/viral/tiktok-sped-up-fast-songs-sounds-rcna79256); [Chartmetric](https://hmc.chartmetric.com/nightcore-slowed-reverb-tiktok-remix/)
- **[CULTURE] Phonk's identity.** Drift phonk is "tied to drift car culture." "Gym phonk" is described as built for "maximum aggression for workout motivation," with 808 drops "timed for heavy lifts." The cowbell over distorted 808 is the genre's instantly recognisable signature. Phonk "surged into global popularity through SoundCloud and TikTok in the early 2020s." — [Futureproof Music School](https://futureproofmusicschool.com/blog/what-is-phonk); [Beatstorapon](https://beatstorapon.com/blog/phonk-music/); [Melodics](https://melodics.com/blog/producers-guide-to-phonk-music) (education or production blogs, low-to-moderate reliability)
- **[STUDY, relevant to loops] Repetition builds earworms and working-memory interference.** See Section 7 (Killingly & Lacherez 2023; Jakubowski et al. 2017: earworm songs tend to be faster with generic, easy-to-remember melodic contours).

### Inferences
- **Slowed + reverb** combines three things the lab evidence links to more mind-wandering and vivid imagery: slow, sad-leaning affect (Taruffi 2017), spacious reverb, and familiar songs (episodic memory and "false nostalgia"). It is plausibly the **most MD-inducing** part of his library, even though it is *less* arousing than phonk and so might seem "calmer" for work. It suits fantasy rather than focus. (Inference; no direct study.)
- **Sped-up edits and TikTok audios** are engineered for "immediate" capture in seconds-long loops. Heard repeatedly, they become very familiar and catchy, which are the strongest predictors of earworms (Section 7). Their attention-grabbing design is the opposite of what a background sound for deep work needs.
- **Phonk and "edit" culture** pairs the music with images of power, physique, cars and dominance. Through evaluative conditioning and visual imagery (BRECVEMA), a phonk track can cue those images and the matching **idealised-self fantasy** even while he sits at a desk. (Inference.)
- **Rap** adds dense, intelligible lyrics (Section 2), the biggest single measured distractor for reading and verbal work.

### Gaps
- **No peer-reviewed research on slowed + reverb or nightcore** as cognitive or emotional stimuli was found. Claims online that slowed music "reduces heart rate" or "reduces overthinking" came from low-quality SEO and generator-tool sites and were excluded.
- No peer-reviewed study on phonk, hardstyle or jumpstyle and cognition or imagery was found.
- No research was found on short-loop (15–60 s) audio exposure and attention specifically.

## 7. Earworms (involuntary musical imagery): music that keeps playing after it stops

### Takeaway
Earworms are near-universal (about 90% of people get them weekly). They are driven most by **recent and repeated exposure**, familiarity, catchiness and the urge to sing along, and the songs that stick tend to be **faster** with simple, generic melodies. Heavy listening to short, repeated, catchy, fast audios is close to a recipe for frequent earworms. These can intrude on verbal working memory even after the music is off. Chewing gum, which occupies the inner voice, is one of the few lab-tested ways to reduce them.

### Cited Findings
- **[STUDY] Prevalence:** Liikkanen (2012) found **~33% of a large internet sample report earworms daily and ~90% at least weekly**. Another dataset gives **89.2% weekly**. People who listen to and sing music a lot get them more often. — [Liikkanen, "Musical activities predispose to INMI" (PhilPapers)](https://philpapers.org/rec/LIIMAP); [Frontiers 2025 review summary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1480633/full); [Liikkanen & Jakubowski 2020 review, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01750-7)
- **[STUDY] Triggers (Williamson et al. 2012):** recent and repeated exposure is the most prevalent trigger. Others are memory triggers (association, recollection, anticipation), affective states (mood, stress, surprise) and **low-attention states such as dreams and mind-wandering**. — [How do earworms start?](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224774285_How_do_earworms_start_Classifying_the_everyday_circumstances_of_Involuntary_Musical_Imagery)
- **[STUDY] Features (Jakubowski et al. 2017, ~3,000 people):** earworm songs are **faster in tempo** than non-earworm songs, with more common, generic melodic contours. — [Goldsmiths news](https://www.gold.ac.uk/news/scientists-find-key-to-writing-catchy-pop-hits/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/555535)
- **[STUDY, experimental] Killingly & Lacherez (2023), "The song that never ends".**
  - Design: N = 44 heard new song choruses 1–4 times on day 1.
  - **68% reported at least one earworm during session 2** and **52% between sessions**.
  - More day-1 exposure produced **more interference with a serial-recall (working-memory) task** on day 2, during *and after* the music (songs heard 4 times: χ² = 9.13, p = .003).
  - Familiarity rose with exposure and plateaued around 4 exposures. Enjoyment did *not* rise, but the urge to sing along did.
  - Earworms were predicted by familiarity, catchiness and desire to sing along. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10585939/)
- **[STUDY] Reduction by chewing gum (Beaman et al. 2015):** chewing gum, which occupies the subvocal articulators (the "inner voice"), **reduced both voluntary and involuntary musical recollections**, i.e., fewer earworms. — [Beaman et al., University of Reading PDF](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/40114/1/Gummed%20up%20earworms3.pdf); [Reading press release](https://archive.reading.ac.uk/news-events/2015/April/pr631000.html)
- **[Expert commentary] Other coping strategies:** listening to the song *to the end* for "closure"; engaging verbal activities (talk, conversation, reading) that use similar resources; and accepting rather than suppressing the tune, since suppression can rebound. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-do-you-solve-a-problem-like-an-earworm/); [Reading, history of the earworm](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/79201/1/History%20of%20the%20earworm%20resubmission.pdf)
- **[STUDY] Earworms as habits:** a 2025 study links INMI with a wide range of habitual behaviours. — [Consciousness and Cognition 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810025000273). Music listening near bedtime is linked to sleep-disrupting earworms (Scullin et al. 2021). — [Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2021/music-listening-near-bedtime-disruptive-sleep-baylor-study-finds); [APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/ear-worm)

### Inferences
- TikTok audios and sped-up hooks are short, catchy, fast and heard many times a day, which maximises the familiarity and catchiness predictors of earworms. The music can then keep loading his phonological loop, and cueing the linked daydream, **during silent work**. This is one reason a switch to silence may at first not *feel* silent.
- Mind-wandering is itself an earworm trigger (Williamson 2012), so MD and earworms can feed each other.
- Practical implication (extrapolated): cutting the number of repeated short loops, especially before work and before bed, should lower earworm load over days to weeks. Gum during writing sessions is a cheap, evidence-based stopgap.

### Gaps
- No study directly measured how long earworm frequency takes to fall after someone cuts their music exposure.
- Detailed results of the 2025 "mental habits" study were not retrieved.

## 8. Habitual and reward-driven listening: dependence, "can't work without music", boredom and switching to silence

### Takeaway
People mainly play music while studying **to relax and to "concentrate"**, yet experiments show that habitual listeners are **not** immune to its costs. Self-rated distraction tracks real impairment, while people still *feel* their liked music helps. The urge to fill silence is strong in general: in Wilson et al. (2014), most people found 6–15 minutes alone with their thoughts unpleasant, and **12 of 18 men shocked themselves** rather than sit quietly. In MD, Somer's participants described music as something whose absence feels like "an itch you cannot scratch". No study was found on an adaptation period after switching to silence.

### Cited Findings
- **[STUDY] Motives:** students mostly listen to music while studying **to relax and to concentrate** (Kotsopoulou & Hallam 2010). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/240525306_The_perceived_impact_of_playing_music_while_studying_Age_and_cultural_differences); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Age-differences-in-listening-to-music-while-Kotsopoulou-Hallam/96361fc0af55c0e655de7a163ceece6f1b62b95a). Newer habit work is [Cheah, Randall & Coutinho 2025, "Help me study! Music listening habits while studying"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03057356251351778) (details not retrieved).
- **[STUDY] Habit does not protect:** Threadgold et al. (2019, Exp. 3) found music impaired creativity **regardless of whether participants typically studied with music**. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)
- **[STUDY] Perceptions versus performance:** in Perham & Currie (2014), participants rated **liked lyrical music as 88/100 familiar and 83/100 likeable** but also **75/100 distracting**. Their estimates of their own performance were lowest for lyrical music, and their actual performance was lower too. — [PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)
- **[STUDY] Habitual users can benefit on boring tasks:** Kiss & Linnell's samples were people who *habitually* use background music, and preferred music reduced mind-wandering on a **low-demand** vigilance task through arousal. The authors stress this has not been generalised to complex tasks. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11281972/)
- **[STUDY] Wilson et al. (2014), Science, "Just think: The challenges of the disengaged mind".**
  - Across **11 studies**, people sat alone for **6–15 minutes**, and **49.3%** rated enjoyment at or below the scale midpoint.
  - In the shock study, **12 of 18 men (67%)** gave themselves at least one electric shock during a 15-minute thinking period; one man pressed it **190 times**.
  - In an at-home version, **32% admitted "cheating" by listening to music or using their phone.**
  - *Caveat:* critics argue the data do not show that thinking is inherently aversive. — [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1250830); [Science news](https://www.science.org/content/article/people-would-rather-be-electrically-shocked-left-alone-their-thoughts); [commentary, Frontiers/PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4260464/)
- **[QUAL] MD-specific dependence (Somer 2024).** Participants described music as **necessary**:
  - "Without music or movement, it is very irritating. It's like having an itch that you cannot scratch."
  - "I can't daydream without music."
  - Somer offers two explanations: music as **soothing coping or emotion regulation** (escaping stress, anxiety and negative emotion), and an **altered state of consciousness** that deepens immersion. He frames MD as a potential behavioural addiction with craving, annoyance when prevented, and failed attempts to cut down.
  - He suggests investigating **stimulus-control therapy**: controlling exposure to the music cues that trigger the behaviour. — [Somer 2024 PDF](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)

### Inferences
- The pattern "work gets uncomfortable, then music, then daydream" fits emotion regulation and escape: music lowers the discomfort and opens the fantasy, which is negatively reinforcing. That makes it habit-forming independently of any effect on focus. (Inference based on Somer's mood-regulation theme and Wilson's evidence that people avoid unstimulated time.)
- When he switches to silence, expect an initial phase of discomfort, boredom and urges ("itch"), plus residual earworms (Section 7). This is a predicted withdrawal-like phase, **not evidence that silence is worse for performance.** The performance evidence favours quiet for verbal work.
- Because people *believe* their liked music helps even when it impairs them (Perham & Currie), self-judgement is unreliable. A short self-experiment (same task type, silence versus music, measured output such as words written or minutes on task) would give him better data than his intuition.

### Gaps
- **No study was found on an adaptation period** (how many days or weeks until silence feels normal) after habitual background-music users switch to silence.
- No direct experimental data were found on "music dependence for starting work" outside MD qualitative reports.

## 9. Non-music alternatives: silence, white, pink and brown noise, and nature sounds

### Takeaway
For **verbal and complex work, quiet is the benchmark** that background sounds are measured against, and it is rarely beaten. **White or pink noise** has meta-analytic support only for people with ADHD or high inattention (small effect), and it slightly *hurts* typical attenders. **Nature sounds** beat **urban** noise for directed attention in a controlled study and support recovery or restoration between work blocks, but evidence that they beat true silence *during* work is thin. **Brown noise is untested.**

### Cited Findings
- **[META] Silence as benchmark:** the main meta-analyses and studies (Vasilev 2018; Souza & Barbosa 2023; Perham & Currie 2014; Threadgold 2019) consistently find quiet equal to or better than lyrical music for reading, verbal memory and creativity. — [Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273); [Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf); [Threadgold 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)
- **[STUDY] Changing-state versus steady-state:** steady-state sound (e.g., "c, c, c…") does not produce the irrelevant sound effect, while changing-state sound does. — [Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf). Constant noise like white, pink or brown is closer to steady-state than music or speech; this is an inference from ISE theory.
- **[META] White and pink noise:** g = +0.249 for youth with ADHD or attention problems (13 studies, N = 335) but **g = −0.212 for non-ADHD controls**. — [Nigg et al. 2024, JAACAP](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890856724000741); [OHSU](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows)
- **[STUDY] Brown noise:** no controlled studies were included in the ADHD meta-analysis. — [OHSU](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows); [secondary](https://pinknoisesleep.com/brown-noise-adhd-focus)
- **[STUDY] Nature sounds (Van Hedger et al. 2019, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review):** participants who heard **natural soundscapes** significantly outperformed those who heard **urban soundscapes** on a directed-attention composite (backward digit span plus dual n-back) after the intervention. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-018-1539-1)
- **[STUDY/REVIEW] Restoration:** Attention Restoration Theory holds that contact with nature replenishes directed attention. Studies report natural sounds outperforming no-sound or control conditions on attention restoration. A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of natural sound exposure and health recovery exists. — [The Influence of Natural Sounds on Attention Restoration](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/306070088_The_Influence_of_Natural_Sounds_on_Attention_Restoration); [Effects of nature sounds on attention and relaxation, Urban Forestry & Urban Greening 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1618866723001589); [Natural sound exposure meta-analysis 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048969724011914)

### Inferences
- **Default for scripting, research, prompting and planning: silence**, using earplugs or noise-cancelling headphones with nothing playing if the room is noisy. This is closest to the best-performing condition across studies.
- **If silence is intolerable at first** (expected during withdrawal), the best-supported "bridge" is **steady, non-musical, non-verbal** sound at low volume: pink or white noise, or nature sounds such as rain or a stream. These lack lyrics, melody and the emotional or imagery cues that trigger MD. That matters especially for an MDer, because music's triggering power runs through emotion and imagery (Section 5), which noise largely lacks. (This MD-specific point is an inference.)
- **Nature sounds** fit best as **break-time restoration** between deep-work blocks, and are better than scrolling TikTok audios, which reload earworms and fantasy cues.
- Volume caution applies to all of these: the OHSU authors flag a hearing-damage risk with headphones.

### Gaps
- Few head-to-head studies compare **silence and nature sounds and white noise** *during* complex verbal work in typical adults. Van Hedger compared nature with urban sound, not with silence.
- There is no evidence on noise or nature sounds in people with MD.

## 10. Practical evidence-based guidance: when, if ever, does background sound help?

### Takeaway
The evidence lines up as a task × sound matrix. **Worst:** lyrical, fast, loud, familiar music (rap, sped-up songs, TikTok audios) during writing or reading. **Also bad for an MDer:** emotionally evocative music of *any* tempo (slowed + reverb, "power" phonk), because it supercharges imagery. **Best for complex verbal work:** silence. **Possibly acceptable:** low-volume steady noise or nature sounds; arousing instrumental music only for mechanical, low-demand tasks, and with caution for an MDer; energising music *before* a session rather than during it.

### Cited Findings (synthesised from sections above)
- Lyrics hurt reading and verbal memory: d ≈ −0.35 for lyrical music in reading ([Vasilev 2018](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/)), and d = −.19 to −.33 across tasks ([Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273)). Liked lyrics are as harmful as disliked ones ([Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)).
- Fast + loud music is the only tempo and intensity combination that dropped reading below baseline. — [Thompson et al. 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ982853)
- Background music of all kinds impaired insight or convergent creativity, regardless of mood boost or study habit. — [Threadgold et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532). Happy instrumental music aided divergent brainstorming. — [Ritter & Ferguson 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0182210)
- Preferred music reduced mind-wandering in a **simple** vigilance task through arousal, among habitual users. — [Kiss et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11281972/)
- Music raises imagery vividness ~2.7× ([Herff et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-95260-8)) and steers spontaneous thought: heroic music makes it empowering ([Koelsch et al. 2019](https://stefan-koelsch.de/papers/koelsch_bashevkin_kristensen_tvedt_jentschke_2019_heroic_music_stimulates_empowering_thoughts_during_mind-wandering_scientific_reports_91_10317.pdf)), and sad music increases mind-wandering ([Taruffi et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-14849-0)). In MDers, music triggers fantasy and weakens control. — [Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)
- Repetition and catchiness drive earworms that interfere with working memory the next day. — [Killingly & Lacherez 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10585939/)
- White or pink noise helps people with ADHD slightly and hurts non-ADHD people slightly. — [Nigg et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890856724000741)

### Inferences: suggested task × sound matrix for this user

These ratings are inferences built on the findings above, not tested prescriptions.

| Task | Rap / TikTok audios / sped-up (lyrical) | Phonk / hardstyle / jumpstyle (fast, loud, mostly instrumental) | Slowed + reverb | Steady noise (pink/white) or nature sounds | Silence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Script writing, outlining | Worst (semantic + changing-state + fast/loud) | Poor (fast/loud + changing-state; power-fantasy cue) | Poor (sad/nostalgic, so more mind-wandering and imagery) | Acceptable bridge | Best |
| Reading research, learning | Worst | Poor | Poor | Acceptable | Best |
| Convergent ideation (choosing *the* hook or angle), AI prompting | Worst | Poor | Poor | Acceptable | Best |
| Loose divergent brainstorming | Poor (lyrics) | Uncertain; any benefit is from arousal, but MD risk | Poor (imagery drift) | Acceptable | Good |
| Mechanical editing (cuts, sync, export) | Moderate risk (lyrics less harmful when the task isn't verbal, but earworms and MD cues remain) | General-population evidence says arousing music *may* help on low-demand tasks; MD risk is the deciding factor | Poor (MD fuel) | Good | Good |
| Pre-work warm-up (2–5 min, then off) | n/a | Plausible arousal or motivation boost (arousal-mood hypothesis; larger effects for pre-task music in the 2023 meta-analysis) | Not advised (sets a nostalgic or fantasy tone) | n/a | n/a |

- **The "discomfort trigger" is the key behavioural target.** The evidence suggests that the moment work feels hard is exactly when music most reliably flips him into MD: it relieves the discomfort and raises imagery and emotion. A stimulus-control rule ("no music at the desk or during work blocks") matches Somer's clinical suggestion.
- **Expect a withdrawal phase.** Initial silence will likely feel aversive (Wilson 2014; Somer's "itch") and earworms will persist for a while (Section 7). This is a predictable transition, not evidence that silence doesn't work.

### Gaps
- No controlled trial has tested any music-reduction intervention in people with MD.
- No studies were found on **video editing** specifically (as opposed to vigilance or arithmetic tasks) with background music, so the "mechanical editing" row is extrapolated.
- No head-to-head study of silence versus steady noise for adult verbal work without ADHD was retrieved, beyond the meta-analytic finding that noise slightly hurts non-ADHD groups.

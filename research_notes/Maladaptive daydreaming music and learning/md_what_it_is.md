# Maladaptive Daydreaming (MD): What It Is, How It Is Measured, and the Competing Scientific Views

Scope note for the report writer: these notes focus on what MD *is*: its definition, the experience from the inside, measurement, prevalence, how it differs from normal daydreaming, and the rival explanations. Neuroscience is deliberately left out. Treatment is mentioned only where it bears on the "is it a distinct disorder?" debate. Most MD research comes from one network of researchers (Eli Somer at the University of Haifa, Nirit Soffer-Dudek at Ben-Gurion University, and collaborators), and much of it uses self-selected online samples. That caveat applies throughout.

---

## 1. How did Somer define MD in 2002, and how has the definition changed (2011–2026)? What are the core features?

### Takeaway
Somer (2002) defined MD as "extensive fantasy activity that replaces human interaction and/or interferes with academic, interpersonal, or vocational functioning." The definition then narrowed, through Bigelsen & Schupak's "compulsive fantasy" (2011), the MDS scale (2016) and the proposed "Daydreaming Disorder" criteria (2017), to the 2025 BJPsych position-paper criteria. Those criteria require compulsive, vivid, absorbing fantasy plus at least two associated features (preference for fantasy over reality, stereotyped movement, short-term relief from bad feelings, annoyance when interrupted, failed attempts to stop). Episodes must last 30 minutes or more, total at least 1 hour a day, persist for 6 months or more, and cause clinically significant distress or impairment. The core features have stayed stable across this history: vivid narrative plots with recurring characters, an idealized self, music, repetitive movement, hours a day, yearning or compulsion, shame, and impairment.

### Cited Findings

**Original definition (2002)**
- Somer (2002) defined MD as "extensive fantasy activity that replaces human interaction and/or interferes with academic, interpersonal, or vocational functioning." The basis was **six patients in a trauma practice**: four had a dissociative disorder and two had narcissistic personality disorder. It was published in *Journal of Contemporary Psychotherapy* 32(2–3). — [Somer 2002, Univ. of Haifa record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/)
- Somer (2002) concluded that MD "seemed to have been preceded by a normal childhood propensity for creative imagination," and that "aversive circumstances were seen to have contributed to the development of MD." — [Somer 2002](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/)

**Bigelsen & Schupak (2011): "compulsive fantasy"**
- The study covered **90 self-identified "non-normative fantasizers"** (75 female, 15 male, ages 18–63), recruited online. Distress came from three factors: "difficulty in controlling the need or desire to engage in fantasizing; concern that the quantity of fantasizing interfered with actual relationships and endeavors; and intense shame." Kinesthetic activity accompanied the fantasies of **79% of participants**. — [Bigelsen & Schupak 2011, Consciousness and Cognition 20(4):1634–1648 (Europe PMC)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21959201)
- Participants reported that fantasizing took up **about 56% of waking hours**. They described their fantasies as "extraordinarily vivid, detailed, and compelling." The authors proposed "compulsive fantasy" as a more precise name. This is a secondary summary in Burrell et al. 2025. — [Burrell, Lawson & Christoff 2025, Behavioral Sciences (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)

**2016: first scale and first controlled comparison**
- **Maladaptive Daydreaming Scale (MDS, 14 items).** Development used **447 English-speaking participants in 45 countries**. A "3-correlated-factors model best presented the underlying dimensions Yearning, Kinesthesia and Impairment." MDS scores correlated with obsessive-compulsive behavior, dissociative absorption and attention deficit. — [Somer, Lehrfeld, Bigelsen & Jopp 2016, Consciousness and Cognition 39:77–91 (Europe PMC)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26707384)
- Bigelsen et al. (2016) compared **340 self-identified MDers with 107 controls** (ages 13–78, 45 countries). MD "differs significantly from normative daydreaming in terms of quantity, content, experience, controllability, distress, and interference with life functioning," and MDers had elevated attention-deficit, obsessive-compulsive and dissociation symptoms. — [Bigelsen, Lehrfeld, Jopp & Somer 2016 (Univ. of Haifa)](https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=33452)

**2017: "Daydreaming Disorder" criteria and the structured interview (SCIMD)**
- Somer, Soffer-Dudek, Ross & Halpern proposed criteria for "Daydreaming Disorder (Maladaptive Daydreaming)." **Criterion A** is persistent, recurrent, vivid, fanciful fantasy, shown by at least two of eight features over 6 months, one of which must be A1:
  - A1: an intense sense of absorption or immersion with visual, auditory or affective properties
  - A2: triggered or maintained by music
  - A3: triggered or maintained by stereotyped movement (pacing, rocking, hand movements)
  - A4: daydreams when distressed or bored
  - A5: daydreams more when alone
  - A6: annoyed when interrupted
  - A7: would rather daydream than do chores, social, academic or professional activities
  - A8: repeated unsuccessful efforts to cut back

  **Criterion B** is clinically significant distress or impairment. **Criterion C** excludes substance or medical causes and cases better explained by autism, ADHD, schizophrenia spectrum, bipolar I, OCD and related disorders, DID, or addictive disorders. Severity is rated mild (distress only), moderate (one area of functioning) or severe (more than one area). — [Somer et al. 2017, Psychology of Consciousness 4(2):176–189 (PDF)](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- The same paper summarizes the phenomenon as "a behavioral addiction for vivid fanciful imagery that can last for hours, and be triggered or maintained either by evocative music or repetitive physical movement, such as pacing or rocking." — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**2020–2025: refinements**
- **MDS-16.** This version adds music items. Its four factors are **Yearning, Impairment, Kinesthesia and Music**, and the structure held across the USA, Italy, Turkey and the UK (N = 1,081). — [Soffer-Dudek, Somer, Abu-Rayya, Metin & Schimmenti, J. Behavioral Addictions ("Different cultures, similar daydream addiction")](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/different-cultures-similar-daydream-addiction-an-examination-of-t/)
- A recent working definition from the Soffer-Dudek lab (2025): MD is "a syndrome involving extensive and compulsive absorption in a vivid fantasized world, with detailed and emotional narratives, replacing interest and investment in real life and resulting in clinical distress and functional impairment. It is often accompanied by stereotypical movements (e.g., pacing, shaking one's hand) or movements embodying the daydream." — [Soffer-Dudek, Aquarone & Somer 2025, J. Psychiatric Research 185:40–45](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-among-patients-with-dissociative-identity/)
- The 2025 meta-analysis defines MD as "elaborate compulsive immersion in intense fanciful fantasy" with intricate set-ups, music and repetitive movements (pacing, gesturing, mouthing). — [Somer, Herscu, Samara & Abu-Rayya 2025, Int. J. Psychology (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)

**Updated 2025 criteria (BJPsych position paper)**
1. Persistent or compulsive vivid, detailed fantasy with intense absorption (visual, auditory or affective).
2. At least two associated features: preference for fantasy over reality, or emotional attachment to daydreaming that exceeds real life; stereotyped movements; short-term relief from stress, anxiety, guilt, shame, low self-worth or loneliness; annoyance when interrupted; repeated failed attempts to control it.
3. Time-consuming: **30 minutes or more per episode, at least 1 hour per day, on most days for the past 6 months**. Single episodes reach **4–5 hours** in some cases.
4. Clinically significant distress or impairment.
5. Not due to a substance or medical condition such as complex partial seizures.
6. Not better explained by OCD, ADHD, GAD, DID, autism, PTSD, acute stress disorder or neurocognitive disorder.

Severity is rated mild, moderate or severe, and "Other Specified MD" covers cases that meet everything except duration. Immersion is "often intensified using evocative music and kinaesthetic stereotypies, including facial gestures, circular pacing, whispering of dialogues or physical movements that enact the fantasy content." — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025, British Journal of Psychiatry 226(4) (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

**Proposals to rename it**
- Burrell, Lawson & Christoff (2025) argue that the label "maladaptive daydreaming" is misleading. In their view these fantasies are "not daydreams, because they are more constrained in their mental dynamics," and they prefer "compulsive fantasy." — [Burrell et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)

### Inferences
- Across 2002–2025 the core picture is stable: a deliberate, story-like, emotionally rewarding inner world, sustained by music, movement and solitude, that crowds out real-life goals. What changed was the formal scaffolding: thresholds, exclusion rules and severity levels.
- The definition moved from "fantasy that replaces human interaction" (a coping and loneliness framing) toward "compulsive, absorptive fantasy causing impairment," which stays neutral about cause. That shift matters for viewpoint (e) below, because trauma is no longer part of the definition.
- The 2025 criteria set a concrete bar: at least 1 hour a day, at least 30 minutes per episode, for 6 months. A reader can hold their own experience against it, but self-diagnosis is not a clinical diagnosis.

### Gaps
- I could not access Schimmenti, Somer & Regis (2019), "Maladaptive daydreaming: Towards a nosological definition" (*Annales Médico-Psychologiques*), so its specific contribution is not summarized here.
- I did not fetch the primary full text of Bigelsen & Schupak (2011). The 56%-of-waking-hours figure comes from a secondary summary in Burrell et al. 2025.

## 2. What does MD feel like from the inside? (qualitative studies and typical content)

### Takeaway
Qualitative studies describe MD as living "parallel lives." People feel a strong sense of presence in a self-made fantasy world, usually started and sustained by solitude, evocative music and pacing or other movement. The fantasies are rewarding and often compensatory: an idealized self, fame or competence, romance, rescue, celebrity relationships, alternate families, or detailed sci-fi and medieval worlds whose characters age over the years. The rewards come with a "yearning" or craving, irritation when interrupted, shame, secrecy, and failed attempts to stop. Most of this evidence comes from small samples of self-identified, online-recruited people.

### Cited Findings

**Somer 2002 (6 patients)**
- Nine themes were grouped into Functions, Themes and Dynamics:
  - **Functions:** "Disengagement from Stress and Pain by Mood Enhancement and Wish Fulfillment Fantasies," and "Companionship, Intimacy, and Soothing."
  - **Recurrent content:** Violence, Idealized Self, Power and Control, Captivity, Rescue and Escape, and Sexual Arousal.
  - **Dynamics:** Onset, and Kinesthetic elements.

  — [Somer 2002](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/)

**Bigelsen & Schupak 2011 (90 participants)**
- Distress came from loss of control, fantasizing displacing real relationships and endeavors, and "intense shame." Kinesthetic movement occurred in 79%. — [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21959201)
- **25%** spontaneously described MD as "a compulsive addiction that engenders irritation, anxiety, and even illness when curbed." Example quotes: "I can't turn it off… I get a bad conscience; maybe like a drinker who promised himself 'this is the last bottle'…", and on being stopped, "I become very anxious, I have intense migraine, and my stomach will hurt." These are quoted in Somer et al. 2017. — [Somer et al. 2017 (PDF)](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**Typical content (Somer et al. 2017, summarizing earlier studies)**
- Compensatory narratives with idealized versions of the self. "Soap-opera-like plots involving alternate families." Complex inner worlds "featuring sci-fi or medieval backdrops." Fantasies that "continued for years with characters aging appropriately over time." "Immensely gratifying fantasies involving such themes as romance, relationships with celebrities, wish fulfillments, and idealized versions of self." Distressed people also "soothe themselves with absorbing compensatory fantasizing." — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**"Parallel lives" (Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016; 21 in-depth interviews)**
- MD is "an excessive form of unwanted daydreaming that produces a rewarding experience based on a created fantasy of a parallel reality associated with a profound sense of presence." Interviewees described how "their natural capacity for vivid daydreaming had developed into a time-consuming habit that resulted in serious dysfunction." Fantasies were "laced with emotionally compensatory themes involving competency, social recognition, and support." **Solitude was necessary** because "social interaction seems to be incompatible with this absorbing mental activity." Kinesthetic activity and/or evocative music "appeared to be essential features." Participants "expressed a substantial need for help." — [Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016, J. Trauma & Dissociation 17(5):561–576](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/parallel-lives-a-phenomenological-study-of-the-lived-experience-o/)

**Childhood antecedents (Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016; grounded theory, 16 online-support seekers)**
- Seven themes emerged:
  1. vivid fantasy as an innate talent
  2. a two-way relationship between daydreaming and social isolation
  3. the role of trauma
  4. rewarding aspects
  5. "compelling yearning"
  6. shame and secrecy
  7. failed treatment attempts

  — [Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016, J. Nerv. Ment. Dis. 204(6):471–478](https://doi.org/10.1097/nmd.0000000000000507)

**Content linked to trauma history (Somer, Abu-Rayya & Brenner 2021; N = 539 probable MDers, multi-country)**
- Emotional neglect and abuse were associated with fantasy used to regulate emotion and with idealized-family imagery. Emotional abuse was associated with suffering, death and violence themes (victim, captor or rescuer roles). Sexual abuse was associated with sexual violence, captivity and rescue. Physical or emotional neglect was associated with revenge daydreams. The authors suggest that for trauma-exposed adults MD may be "a pathological preoccupation with unresolved childhood adversities," not only coping. — [Somer, Abu-Rayya & Brenner 2021, J. Trauma & Dissociation](https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=11619)

**"Double consciousness" (BJPsych 2025)**
- People with MD describe "the simultaneous experience of reality and fantasy" and "leading two parallel lives." Internal events reduce "responsiveness to their surroundings," and episodes can involve "whispering of dialogues" and acting out the fantasy. The fantasy offers "short-term relief from aversive feelings such as stress, anxiety, guilt, shame, low self-worth or loneliness." — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

**The "agency paradox" (theoretical, not empirically tested)**
- The fantasy provides felt agency inside the imagined world while undermining agency in real life. Fantasizers deliberately set up conditions (music, isolation, movement) even while feeling compelled. — [Burrell et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)

**Day-to-day dynamics (daily diary; 77 self-identified MDers over 14 days)**
- Days with more MD had more concurrent symptoms and negative emotion. Obsessive-compulsive symptoms were "the only consistent temporal antecedent of MD." — [Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018, Frontiers in Psychiatry 9:194](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00194/full)
- Depersonalization and derealization increased on high-daydreaming days and the following day, but not the day before. This is summarized in the BJPsych paper. — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

**Related phenomenon: "reality shifting"**
- An online subculture tries to "shift" into fictional universes through relaxation and autosuggestion. One forum has more than 40,000 members, and related social-media content has been viewed billions of times. The authors compare it with hypnosis, dissociation and MD. — [Somer, Cardeña, Catelan & Soffer-Dudek 2021/2023, Current Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-021-02439-3)

### Inferences
- Three things recur on the inside: (1) reward, because the fantasy is pleasurable, compensatory and full of presence; (2) ritual, because music, pacing and solitude are used deliberately to get in; (3) regret, in the form of yearning, shame, secrecy and lost time. Because of this mix, people often describe MD as closer to a compulsion or addiction than to "spacing out."
- Common content themes: an idealized self, fame, success and recognition, romance, rescue, captivity and violence, celebrities, alternate families, and elaborate fictional worlds that run for years. For a content creator, fame and success fantasies may feel especially tempting because they look like "planning" while replacing real work. This is an inference, not a finding specific to creators.
- Music is not a side detail. It is a criterion item (A2), a separate MDS-16 factor, and was described as "essential" in qualitative work.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain detailed results of Wen, Soffer-Dudek & Somer (2024), "Daily Feelings and the Affective Valence of Daydreams in Maladaptive Daydreaming: A Time-lag Analysis" (*Psychology of Consciousness* 11(4):447–460); only the title was retrieved, [listed here](https://soffern.wixsite.com/soffer-dudek-lab-bgu/papers). The same applies to Meidan & Soffer-Dudek (2026), "Was That Really Me? Relationships Between Memory and Sense of Self in People with MD" (*British Journal of Clinical Psychology* 65(2)).
- No quantitative frequency table of content themes (for example, "% with fame fantasies") was found. Theme lists are qualitative.

## 3. How is MD measured and diagnosed? Is it in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11? What is the status of recognition efforts?

### Takeaway
The main self-report tool is the **MDS-16**. Its validated clinical cut-off is a mean of **50** (Somer et al. 2017), though some epidemiological studies use **40** to flag suspected cases. Diagnosis is confirmed with the **Structured Clinical Interview for MD (SCIMD)**, which showed good-to-excellent interrater agreement (κ = .63–.84). Newer tools include a 5-item screener (MD-SF5, 2024), an addiction-framed scale (ADS-20/ADS-5, 2025) and a questionnaire that separates immersive daydreaming from mind-wandering (DCQ, 2025). **MD is not in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11.** The main recognition push is a 2025 *British Journal of Psychiatry* position paper, signed by 27 dissociation experts, arguing that MD should be listed as a dissociative disorder.

### Cited Findings

**MDS-16 and the SCIMD (Somer et al. 2017)**
- Sample: **31 self-identified MDers and 31 matched controls** (MD group 20 F, 11 M; ages 18–60; mean 27.84) from **15 countries**. Each person had two independent, blinded SCIMD interviews over the internet.
- Agreement was κ = .63–.84 between interviewers and the screening criterion, and between the two interviewers.
- An MDS-16 cut-off of **50** gave sensitivity **96.8%** and specificity **100%** (AUC = .996), and agreement with the interview of κ = .68–.81.
- The authors flagged three limitations themselves: internet-based interviews, possible self-selection, and the need for wider samples.

— [Somer et al. 2017 (PDF)](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**MDS-16 structure across cultures**
- Across the USA, Italy, Turkey and the UK (N = 1,081), configural invariance held. Metric invariance held for Impairment, Kinesthesia and Music, **but not for Yearning**. The authors concluded that interference with daily life and long-term goals "may be the central defining factor of MD," while the craving component may mean different things in different cultures. — [Soffer-Dudek et al., J. Behav. Addictions](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/different-cultures-similar-daydream-addiction-an-examination-of-t/)

**Language versions**
- **Arabic version (MDS-16-AR):** 180 respondents from several Arab countries (ages 18–58, mean 26.28). A two-factor structure (immersive daydreaming, and distress/impairment) with "high sensitivity and specificity." — [Abu-Rayya, Somer & Meari-Amir 2019, Psychology of Consciousness 6(2):171–183](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/the-psychometric-properties-of-the-arabic-16-item-maladaptive-day/)
- **Turkish validation:** 377 university-educated volunteers. — [Turkmedline record](https://turkmedline.net/detay/turkish-validity-and-reliability-study-of-maladaptive-daydreaming-scale/3e71625941634737/tr/464%2B2022%2B12%2B1)

**Cut-off of 40 in epidemiology, and over-identification**
- Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz (2022) used **MDS-16 ≥ 40** to flag suspected MD. Only **60%** of the screen-positive people they interviewed met SCIMD criteria. — [Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022, Frontiers in Psychiatry 13:871041 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)

**MDS-16 accuracy when ADHD is present (156 adults, interview-diagnosed)**
- Without ADHD: AUC 98.9% (sensitivity 96%, specificity 94.1%).
- Full sample: AUC 90.7%.
- Distinguishing ADHD-only from ADHD plus MD: AUC 79.0%, with specificity only **44.7%**.

So the MDS-16 produces false positives among people with ADHD. — [Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025, Int. J. Clinical & Health Psychology 25:100616](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)

**Newer instruments**
- **Daydreaming Characteristics Questionnaire (DCQ):** two samples (346 students, 381 community adults) yielded two factors, *immersive daydreaming* and *daydream functionality*, both uniquely associated with MD. The DCQ reflects immersive daydreaming "much more than general attentional deficiency." — [Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025, J. Attention Disorders 29(7):515–528](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/10870547251319081)
- **MD-SF5:** a 5-item short form tested in **2,512 US panelists** (84.6% female, mean age 39.7). — [Soffer-Dudek & Oh 2024, Comprehensive Psychiatry 129:152441](https://doaj.org/article/f6e4a128e4294d68a93f1cf34023254e)
- **Addictive Daydreaming Scale (ADS-20 and ADS-5):** built on a behavioral-addiction framework. Tested in 182 mixed-clinical and 231 non-clinical Polish participants. α = .958 (ADS-20) and .862 (ADS-5). Cut-offs: **≥ 42** (ADS-20) and **≥ 8** (ADS-5). ADS scores correlated with quality-of-life impairment more strongly than existing MD measures. — [Pietkiewicz, Hełka & Tomalski 2025, Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1702416/full)

**Official status**
- "Neither DSM-5-TR nor ICD-11 currently includes maladaptive daydreaming as a formal diagnosis." — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025, BJPsych (Cambridge)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/maladaptive-daydreaming-should-be-included-as-a-dissociative-disorder-in-psychiatric-manuals-position-paper/54F944C59CB77D9224E6BB78ADBCDF4A)
- A 2025 Turkish clinical letter confirms it is "not yet included" in DSM-5 or ICD-11 and that it is often overlooked or misattributed to ADHD, depression or dissociative disorders. — [Ozmeral Erarkadas et al. 2025, Dusunen Adam](https://dusunenadamdergisi.org/article/1718/pdf)

**The 2025 BJPsych position paper**
- Published March 2025 in *BJPsych* 226(4), led by Nirit Soffer-Dudek.
- Signed by **27 dissociation researchers and clinicians**, including Colin Ross, David Spiegel and Eli Somer, and multiple current and past presidents of the ISSTD and ESTD. The search summary of the paper specifies the current ISSTD president plus 13 past presidents, and the current ESTD president plus 3 past presidents.
- Argues that MD meets **4 of 5 Robins–Guze validity criteria**: clinical description, psychological tests, differentiation from other disorders, and preliminary follow-up stability. The missing criterion is laboratory/neural markers and family studies.
- Argues that MD exceeds Blashfield's DSM-inclusion guideline of at least 50 papers in a decade, including at least 25 empirical studies.

— [BJPsych (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/); [Medical Xpress coverage](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-03-maladaptive-daydreaming-dissociative-disorder-psychiatric.html)

**Other recognition efforts**
- The 2025 meta-analysis also calls for formal recognition in DSM-5-TR and ICD-11. It concedes that "longitudinal studies and treatment response data are needed." — [Somer et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- Grassroots advocacy: people with MD sent petitions to the **American Psychiatric Association and the UK Parliament**, analyzed by Bershtling & Somer as a bottom-up "micropolitical process of medicalization." — [Bershtling & Somer 2018, The Qualitative Report 23(8)](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/tqr/vol23/iss8/14)

### Inferences
- In practice there are two steps: a self-report screen (MDS-16), then a structured interview (SCIMD). Self-report alone over-identifies MD. In the Israeli epidemiology, about 40% of screen-positives were not confirmed, and among people with ADHD the MDS-16's specificity drops to about 45%. Anyone scoring high on an online MDS-16 should treat the result as "possible MD," not a diagnosis.
- Two cut-offs (40 and 50) are in circulation, so the report should state which one a given study used.
- As of October 2026 I found no evidence that the APA or WHO has acted on the 2025 proposal. MD remains officially unrecognized, so clinicians may not know the term.

### Gaps
- I found no official APA DSM Steering Committee or WHO ICD response to the 2025 proposal.
- I could not verify an organization called the "International Society for Maladaptive Daydreaming." Some sources mention an "International Consortium for Maladaptive Daydreaming Research (ICMDR)," a group of researchers from the US, Poland, Switzerland, Israel, Greece and Italy, but this came only from a search summary and I could not confirm it from a primary page.
- Interrater reliability for the *new* 2025 criteria has not yet been established; the BJPsych authors say so explicitly.

## 4. How common is MD? Age of onset, gender and course

### Takeaway
The only interview-confirmed epidemiological study, from Israel, estimates a **point prevalence of about 2.5%** in the general Israeli-Jewish population. Rates are higher among young adults and students (about **4–8.5%**). Self-report-only studies, especially of Arab and African medical students, report much higher figures (34–70%) that almost certainly overestimate true MD. In clinical groups MD is common: about **20% of adults with ADHD**, **25–40% of people with DID**, and about **half of dissociative-disorder inpatients**. Onset is typically in childhood or early adolescence. Online samples are mostly female, but the representative Israeli data found **no clear female predominance**. Over 13 months MD was **highly stable** and predicted later increases in distress.

### Cited Findings

**General population and students (Israel; interview-confirmed)**
- Four samples:
  - Community sample A: N = 383 (56.4% female, mean age 40.4)
  - Student sample B: N = 301
  - Student sample C: N = 212
  - Student sample D: N = 127
- Screen-positive rates (MDS-16 ≥ 40): **4.2%** in the community, and **5.5%, 7.6% and 8.0%** in the student samples.
- Only 60% of interviewed screen-positives were confirmed. Estimated true prevalence: **2.5%** for the Israeli-Jewish population and about **4.4%** for students. Ages 18–30 had the highest rate (8.5%).
- "No clear female predominance emerged."
- Limitations: Israeli-Jewish samples only, not all screen-positives were interviewed, and some data were collected during COVID-19.

— [Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)

**Large US panel**
- In 2,512 panelists, suspected MD (screened with the MD-SF5) was associated with "psychological distress, loneliness, psychotic experiences, heavy drinking, and suicidality." Suspected MDers were "more than twice as likely to have recently attempted suicide" even after controlling for distress (**OR = 2.44**). — [Soffer-Dudek & Oh 2024](https://doaj.org/article/f6e4a128e4294d68a93f1cf34023254e)

**Muslim-majority and Arab student samples (self-report only; treat with caution)**
- **Saudi medical students:** "MD at 70%," using the 14-item MDS without interview confirmation. Maladaptive daydreamers had a "statistically significant decline in the GPA." — [Alenizi et al. 2020, Cureus 12(10):e10776 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7532859/)
- **UAE undergraduates:** 53.8% had scores indicating MD (master's thesis). — [UAEU thesis](https://scholarworks.uaeu.ac.ae/all_theses/1131)
- **Sudan:** 34.3% of University of Khartoum medical students self-identified as maladaptive daydreamers. — [JPMA](https://mail.jpma.org.pk/index.php/public_html/article/view/11316/3876)
- The UAE and Sudan figures come from search-result summaries; I did not read those full texts.

**Clinical samples**
- **Adult ADHD:** "only **20.5%** of the ADHD sample [N = 83] met the proposed diagnostic criteria for MD." — [Theodor-Katz, Somer, Hesseg & Soffer-Dudek 2022, J. Clinical Psychology 78(11)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23355)
- **Dissociative Identity Disorder (N = 67, UK):** "at least a quarter… had co-morbid MD, although the actual rate was probably closer to **40%**." — [Soffer-Dudek, Aquarone & Somer 2025](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-among-patients-with-dissociative-identity/)
- **Dissociative-disorder inpatients (N = 100):** **49** met criteria for MD and **23** for unspecified MD. — [Ross, Ridgway & George 2020, Psychiatric Research and Clinical Practice 2(2):53–61](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.prcp.20190050)

**Online communities (scale of self-identification)**
- As of January 2017, the Wild Minds Network served **6,198** participants with MD and a Facebook MD group had more than **3,490** users. — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- A Reddit MD community has more than **121,000** members, and "hundreds of thousands" seek peer support online. — [Somer et al. 2025 meta-analysis (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)

**Gender**
- Research samples are mostly female: 75 of 90 in Bigelsen & Schupak 2011, and **18,519 female vs 5,905 male** across the 40 studies in the 2025 meta-analysis.
- A 2025 scoping review of 89 studies found the literature dominated by "online cross-sectional data with younger female participants."
- The representative Israeli study found no clear female predominance, which suggests the female skew partly reflects who joins online communities and volunteers for surveys.

— [Europe PMC – Bigelsen & Schupak](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21959201); [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/); [Mansuklal et al. 2025, Clin. Psychol. & Psychotherapy 32(4):e70104 (Europe PMC)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40665643); [Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)

**Onset**
- "Reports of extensive immersive daydreaming starting at very early ages are the norm." Some children diagnosed with stereotypic movement disorder experience extensive imagery with their movements, which "may evolve into adult maladaptive daydreaming" (Hedderly et al. 2024, cited there). — [BJPsych 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- Somer 2002 and the 2016 grounded-theory study both describe a childhood "innate talent" or "normal childhood propensity" for vivid imagination that later became problematic. — [Somer 2002](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/); [Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016](https://doi.org/10.1097/nmd.0000000000000507)

**Course and duration**
- In 814 Italian participants followed over **13 months**, the authors found "high MD stability and positive cross-lagged pathways from MD to psychological distress." In other words, MD predicted later increases in distress. — [Musetti, Soffer-Dudek, Imperato, Schimmenti & Franceschini 2023, J. Behav. Addictions 12(1):288–294](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2023.00001)
- Fantasies "continued for years with characters aging appropriately over time." — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**Severity markers**
- In 39 interview-diagnosed MDers, **28.2%** had attempted suicide. — [Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017, J. Nerv. Ment. Dis. 205(7):525–530 (Europe PMC)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)
- In a sample Soffer-Dudek describes, "almost half the sample was unemployed and over a [quarter] attempted suicide." This is a popular-press summary of her own research. — [Soffer-Dudek 2022, Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/consciousness-and-psychopathology/202205/why-we-should-take-maladaptive-daydreaming-seriously)

### Inferences
- The most defensible headline is about 2.5% of the general population, with higher rates in people aged roughly 18–30 (around 4–8%). That makes MD comparable in frequency to many recognized disorders, but the estimate rests on one country's data.
- Self-report prevalence figures of 34–70% in Saudi, Sudanese and UAE student samples should not be taken as true prevalence. They use screeners without interview confirmation, and the Israeli data show that even a stricter screen over-counts by about 40%. They may still signal heavy immersive daydreaming among students under academic pressure.
- A young adult male is not atypical. Representative data show no strong female skew, and the 18–30 age band carries the highest risk.

### Gaps
- I found no verified mean age of onset figure, only qualitative statements that onset is in childhood or early adolescence.
- I found no representative prevalence studies outside Israel. The US MD-SF5 abstract did not report a prevalence rate.
- There are no longitudinal data from childhood onward and no data on natural remission rates.

## 5. How does MD differ from normal mind-wandering, fantasy proneness, and constructive daydreaming?

### Takeaway
Ordinary mind-wandering is spontaneous, unplanned drifting of attention. It takes up nearly half of waking thought and is "usually not particularly fanciful." MD is **intentional, narrative, vivid and emotionally immersive**, often deliberately started with music and movement, and it is **craved and hard to stop**. **Fantasy proneness** (Wilson & Barber) also involves vivid fantasy for long periods, but it was never framed as a disorder and includes hallucination-like experiences and fantasy–reality confusion that are typically absent in MD. **Positive-constructive daydreaming** (Singer; McMillan, Kaufman & Singer 2013) serves planning, creativity, problem-solving and memory. MD resembles Singer's other two styles, *guilty-dysphoric* and *poor attentional control*, combined with compulsion and impairment.

### Cited Findings

**Normal daydreaming and mind-wandering**
- Normal daydreaming makes up "almost half of all human" thought (Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010). It is conceptualized as off-task or undirected thought and "is usually not particularly fanciful" (Klinger & Cox 1987). Its four adaptive functions are "future planning; creativity and problem solving; attentional cycling…; and dishabituation." MD "differs from normative daydreaming and mind-wandering, because it is characterized by increased richness of fantasy and absorption in imagination." — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**Singer's three daydreaming styles**
- Singer (1975), as summarized by McMillan, Kaufman & Singer (2013):
  1. **Positive-constructive daydreaming:** "playful, wishful imagery, and planful, creative thought."
  2. **Guilty-dysphoric daydreaming:** "obsessive, anguished fantasies," linked to neuroticism.
  3. **Poor attentional control:** "inability to concentrate on either the ongoing thought or the external task."
- Benefits of positive-constructive daydreaming include future planning and autobiographical memory, creative incubation, attentional cycling, dishabituation, memory consolidation, moral reasoning and compassion.

— [McMillan, Kaufman & Singer 2013, "Ode to positive constructive daydreaming," Frontiers in Psychology 4:626](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00626/full)

**Mind-wandering in ADHD vs immersive daydreaming in MD**
- The daydreaming associated with ADHD "is better described as mind wandering — the spontaneous shifting of attention to task-unrelated thoughts," which differs from MD's immersive daydreaming. — [PsyPost coverage of Theodor-Katz et al.](https://www.psypost.org/study-suggests-maladaptive-daydreaming-should-be-classified-as-a-unique-mental-disorder-distinct-from-adhd/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/04/220412141107.htm)
- In 156 interview-diagnosed adults, an "immersive daydreaming" factor correlated **r = .71** with MD, **r = .23** with ADHD and **r = .31** with mind-wandering. — [Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)
- The 2025 BJPsych paper: "Despite significant impaired attention, the mental pattern underlying the distraction – absorptive emotional fantasising – is significantly differentiated from the typical mind-wandering of attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder." — [BJPsych 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

**Fantasy proneness**
- Wilson & Barber (1981) described fantasy-prone individuals who "engaged in highly vivid daydreams for as much as half their waking hours." They did not characterize this as disordered, and fantasy proneness includes "hallucinatory phenomena and confusion between fantasy and reality… which are typically absent in MD." — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- MD "is not characterised by psychotic-spectrum delusions, hallucinations or thought impairments." — [BJPsych 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

**Compulsive fantasy as deliberately constrained thought**
- Burrell et al. argue that "fantasy and daydreaming are distinct imaginative phenomena." Compulsive fantasy involves *more deliberate constraint* than spontaneous daydreaming, shares constraint dynamics with creative thought, and is "not inherently harmful but can become so when accompanied by relatively strong and sustained automatic constraints." This is a theoretical model, not yet tested empirically. — [Burrell, Lawson & Christoff 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)

**Imaginative capacity as a risk factor, not the disorder**
- "An innate ability for immersive daydreaming, which may not in itself be psychopathological, constitutes a risk factor or diathesis for maladaptive daydreaming." — [BJPsych 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

### Inferences
- What distinguishes MD from normal daydreaming is not imagination itself. Three markers together do: (1) *immersion and narrative*, meaning a deliberate, story-based, emotionally vivid world rather than drifting thought; (2) *compulsion*, meaning yearning, annoyance when interrupted and failed attempts to cut back; (3) *cost*, meaning displaced work, relationships or sleep. A vivid imagination used for planning or creative work (positive-constructive daydreaming) is healthy and even useful for a content creator. The problem is when imagination substitutes for real action and cannot be turned off.
- This suggests a practical self-check: does the daydream end in an action step (planning or a script idea), or does it replace the action and loop?

### Gaps
- I could not access a 2026 systematic review, "Maladaptive daydreaming and deliberate mind-wandering: a systematic review of psychopathological associations in adults" ([ResearchGate, 403 when fetched](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/400911493_Maladaptive_daydreaming_and_deliberate_mind-wandering_a_systematic_review_of_psychopathological_associations_in_adults)). It may sharpen the line between deliberate mind-wandering and MD.
- I found no taxometric study testing whether MD is a separate category or the extreme end of a continuum.

## 6. The competing viewpoints: what is MD fundamentally? (evidence and critiques for each)

### Takeaway
Six framings compete, and they overlap rather than exclude one another:
- **(a) A distinct disorder.** Somer, Bigelsen and Soffer-Dudek, supported by reliable interviews, characteristic comorbidity, stability and response to targeted treatment.
- **(b) A dissociative-absorption disorder.** The current official proposal (BJPsych 2025).
- **(c) A behavioral addiction or OCD-related compulsion.** Pietkiewicz, and much of the "yearning" data.
- **(d) An attention problem tied to ADHD.** Partly rejected: most people with ADHD do not have MD, and MD's immersion is not ordinary mind-wandering.
- **(e) A coping or escape strategy rooted in loneliness, trauma or unmet needs.** Strong evidence for the coping *function*, but trauma is neither universal nor required.
- **(f) Skeptical views.** Concern about pathologizing imagination, reliance on self-selected online samples, and the label itself.

Even the leading proponents concede that MD "could arguably be categorised as a behavioural addiction, an obsessive–compulsive spectrum disorder or a unique subtype of attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder."

### Cited Findings

#### (a) MD as a distinct mental disorder (Somer, Bigelsen, Soffer-Dudek)

**Evidence for**
- *Distinct presentation.* "MD is uniquely characterized by a kinesthetic component, a need for evocative music, and an addictive yearning to compulsively engage in this mental behavior." — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- *Reliable diagnosis.* Interview-based diagnosis is reliable (κ = .63–.84). — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- *Comorbidity like other recognized disorders.* The 2025 meta-analysis covered **40 studies, N = 24,977**. MD was associated with:

  | Measure | Pooled r |
  |---|---|
  | Overall psychopathology | .372 (k = 72) |
  | General psychopathology | .539 |
  | Dissociation | .447 |
  | Depression | .432 |
  | Anxiety | .387 |
  | ADHD | .312 |
  | OCD | .303 |
  | Psychotic symptoms | .297 |
  | Traumatic experiences | .198 |
  | Autism | .162 |
  | Psychological distress | .492 |
  | Shame | .451 |
  | Problematic internet use | .404 |
  | Emotion-regulation difficulties | .365 |
  | Loneliness | .292 |
  | Self-esteem | −.360 |
  | Self-efficacy | −.156 |

  The authors conclude that MD "behaves like other DSM disorders by showing comorbidity with various psychopathologies." — [Somer, Herscu, Samara & Abu-Rayya 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- *Interview-confirmed comorbidity (N = 39).*
  - **74.4%** met criteria for more than three additional disorders, and 41.1% for more than four.
  - ADHD **76.9%**, anxiety disorders **71.8%**, depressive disorders **66.7%**, OCD and related disorders **53.9%**.
  - Suicide attempts **28.2%**.

  — [Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017 (Europe PMC)](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)
- *Stability and predictive power.* MD was stable over 13 months and predicted later distress. — [Musetti et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2023.00001)
- *Response to targeted treatment.* Soffer-Dudek: "Nonspecific treatments often do not improve symptoms, whereas initial evidence suggests that targeted clinical interventions may be promising." — [Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-03-maladaptive-daydreaming-dissociative-disorder-psychiatric.html)
- *Randomized trial.* An 8-session web-based mindfulness plus self-monitoring program randomized **557** people (353 completers) to a full intervention, a partial intervention or a wait-list. Both intervention groups improved with large effects (MD η² = .24; frequency η² = .22; functioning η² = .15); the wait-list did not. Clinically significant improvement was **24%** and reliable improvement **39%**, both maintained at 6 months. These figures come from search summaries of the trial. — [Herscu, Somer, Federman & Soffer-Dudek 2023, J. Consulting & Clinical Psychology 91(5):285–300](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/mindfulness-meditation-and-self-monitoring-reduced-maladaptive-da-3/); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)

**Critiques acknowledged by the proponents themselves**
- "The diagnostic criteria suggested… are still somewhat arbitrary."
- Neural markers and family studies are missing.
- Adding MD would "contribute to the general problem of increasing the number of diagnoses."
- The boundaries between normal and pathological daydreaming need clarifying.

— [BJPsych 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

- Somer et al. (2017) note the reservation that MD "may simply represent an extreme variant of normal." They answer that this "applies equally to most, if not all, DSM-5 disorders," pointing to the poor field-trial reliability of major depression (κ = .28). — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

#### (b) MD as a dissociative-absorption disorder (Soffer-Dudek & Somer; BJPsych 2025)

**Core claim**
- MD should be listed under the **dissociative disorders**. It involves "disconnection from perception, behaviour and sense of self," including "double consciousness." — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025, BJPsych](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/maladaptive-daydreaming-should-be-included-as-a-dissociative-disorder-in-psychiatric-manuals-position-paper/54F944C59CB77D9224E6BB78ADBCDF4A)
- An earlier statement of this view: Soffer-Dudek & Somer (2022), "Maladaptive daydreaming is a dissociative disorder: Supporting evidence and theory," in Dorahy, Gold & O'Neil (eds.), *Dissociation and the Dissociative Disorders* (2nd ed.). — [Soffer-Dudek lab publications](https://soffern.wixsite.com/soffer-dudek-lab-bgu/papers)

**Evidence**
- Dissociation is the largest specific correlate in the meta-analysis (r = .447). — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- Depersonalization and derealization follow high-MD days. — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- High rates of MD in dissociative-disorder inpatients (49 of 100). — [Ross et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.prcp.20190050)
- 25–40% of people with DID have MD, and "absorption and imaginative involvement" predicts MD even within a DID sample. — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-among-patients-with-dissociative-identity/)
- In a Hungarian sample (N = 717, including 106 problematic daydreamers), maladaptive daydreamers had "significantly higher… dissociative propensity," and dissociation mediated the link between specific childhood traumas and MD. — [Sándor et al. 2023, Current Psychology 42:9509–9525](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-021-02223-3)
- In 510 self-identified MDers, dissociation and sense of control mediated the MD–OCD symptom link. — [Salomon-Small et al. 2021, J. Psychiatric Research 136:343–350](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2021.02.017)

**Critiques**
- Unlike classic dissociation, MD is sought out, enjoyable and partly voluntary. Burrell et al. stress that fantasizers *deliberately* use music, isolation and movement, which "contradict[s] purely automatic" models. — [Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)
- The position paper itself concedes MD "could arguably be categorised" elsewhere. Some disorders are "hard to classify… as they may have characteristics that overlap with more than one taxonomy dimension." — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- Much of the dissociation evidence comes from clinical dissociative samples, where MD is expected to be over-represented.

#### (c) MD as a behavioral addiction or compulsion (Pietkiewicz; overlap with OCD and OCRD)

**Evidence for the addiction framing**
- A single-case qualitative study (interview and mental state exam, analyzed with interpretative phenomenological analysis) found that MD "was developed as a strategy to cope with distress but led to uncontrollable absorption in fantasy, social withdrawal, and neglecting aspects of everyday life." It co-occurred with excessive internet and pornography use. The authors said further work must determine "whether MD constitutes a separate syndrome or is a part of other behavioral addictions." — [Pietkiewicz, Nęcki, Bańbura & Tomalski 2018, J. Behavioral Addictions 7(3):838–843](https://real.mtak.hu/86945/)
- The ADS-20 frames MD as meeting addiction criteria: impaired control, mood modification, tolerance and continued use despite harm. — [Pietkiewicz, Hełka & Tomalski 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1702416/full)
- Somer's own early framing called MD "a behavioral addiction for vivid fanciful imagery." The MDS has a separate "Yearning" (craving) factor, and 25% of Bigelsen & Schupak's sample spontaneously called it an addiction with withdrawal-like symptoms. — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- The cross-cultural MDS paper is titled "Different cultures, similar daydream addiction," and several key MD papers appear in the *Journal of Behavioral Addictions*. — [Soffer-Dudek et al.](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/different-cultures-similar-daydream-addiction-an-examination-of-t/)
- Problematic internet use correlates with MD (r = .404). — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)

**Evidence for the OCD and OCRD framing**
- 53.9% of interview-diagnosed MDers had an OCD-related disorder. — [Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)
- In daily diaries, obsessive-compulsive symptoms were "the only consistent temporal antecedent of MD." — [Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00194/full)
- MD relates more strongly to obsessions than to compulsions. — [Salomon-Small et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychires.2021.02.017)
- A case report describes improved control with fluvoxamine, an OCD medication. This is a single case, cited via Wikipedia. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maladaptive_daydreaming)

**Critiques from the dissociation camp**
- "MD does not necessarily include gradually increasing elements to reach the same effect" (no clear tolerance), and "there is no current addiction diagnosis suited for it." — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- On OCD: "indulging in a daydream storyline is satisfying and enjoyable in the short term, unlike the distress caused by intrusive obsessions or compulsions." — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- The "Yearning" factor was **not** measurement-invariant across cultures, so the craving component may be understood differently in different places. — [Soffer-Dudek et al.](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/different-cultures-similar-daydream-addiction-an-examination-of-t/)

#### (d) MD as an attention problem / linked to (inattentive) ADHD

**Evidence of overlap**
- ADHD is the most common comorbidity in interview-diagnosed MD (**76.9%**, N = 39). — [Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)
- Meta-analytic r = .312 between MD and ADHD. — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- Theodor-Katz et al. asked "Could immersive daydreaming underlie a deficit in attention?" They suggest absorbing daydreams *cause* inattention: "addictive and immersive daydreaming causes inattention." — [Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23355); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/study-suggests-maladaptive-daydreaming-should-be-classified-as-a-unique-mental-disorder-distinct-from-adhd/)

**Evidence against reducing MD to ADHD**
- Only **20.5%** of 83 ADHD adults had MD. Those who did had more depression and loneliness and lower self-esteem. Soffer-Dudek: "there is a subgroup of those diagnosed with ADHD who would benefit more from a diagnosis of MD"; such people find "an ADHD diagnosis and the subsequent treatment plan does not necessarily help them." — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/04/220412141107.htm); [Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.23355)
- In 156 interview-diagnosed adults (49 MD only, 38 ADHD only, 34 both, 35 controls), immersive daydreaming correlated r = .71 with MD but only r = .23 with ADHD. "ADHD cannot fully explain MD symptoms," and true comorbidity exists in some people. — [Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)

**Critique and nuance**
- People with ADHD "commonly score highly on the MD self-report screener." This questions the reliability of MD and ADHD symptom checklists and means self-report alone cannot settle the question. — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/study-suggests-maladaptive-daydreaming-should-be-classified-as-a-unique-mental-disorder-distinct-from-adhd/); [Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)
- Clinicians list ADHD and sluggish cognitive tempo (now "cognitive disengagement syndrome") in the differential diagnosis. — [Ozmeral Erarkadas et al. 2025](https://dusunenadamdergisi.org/article/1718/pdf)

#### (e) MD as a coping or escape strategy (loneliness, trauma, unmet needs)

**Evidence for the coping function**
- Somer's 2002 cases came from a trauma practice, and the main functions identified were escape from stress and pain and "companionship, intimacy, and soothing." — [Somer 2002](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/)
- The 2025 scoping review (89 studies) calls MD "a dysfunctional coping mechanism" sharing "compulsion and emotional dysregulation" with other disorders. — [Mansuklal, Pascoal, Somer, Costa & Andersson 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40665643)
- Daydreaming gives short-term relief from "stress, anxiety, guilt, shame, low self-worth or loneliness." It is also linked to *current* stressors such as "strenuous relationships at home or social anxiety" (Somer & Herscu 2017), and to emotion-regulation difficulties "which could play an aetiological part by strengthening the need for an alternative coping mechanism." — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- Loneliness (r = .292), emotion-regulation difficulties (r = .365) and shame (r = .451) are all associated with MD. — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- Daydreaming and social isolation reinforce each other. — [Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016](https://doi.org/10.1097/nmd.0000000000000507)
- Trauma type shapes fantasy content. — [Somer et al. 2021](https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=11619)

**Evidence that trauma is not required**
- Bigelsen & Schupak (2011) showed that MD "is spread among many people who have not had adverse childhoods." — [Jerusalem Post summary](https://www.jpost.com/business-and-innovation/health-and-science/studies-identify-disorder-of-maladaptive-daydreaming-453983)
- The BJPsych paper says MD "is often associated with past trauma" but caveats this by citing Bigelsen et al. 2011 and 2016 as showing trauma is not universal. It frames the main predisposition as an innate immersive-imagination capacity, a diathesis. — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- The pooled MD–trauma correlation is small (**r = .198**), much weaker than for dissociation, depression or distress. — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)

**Direction of effect**
- MD prospectively *predicts* later distress, so it is not only a consequence of distress. — [Musetti et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2023.00001)

#### (f) Skeptical or cautious views

**Pathologizing ordinary life**
- Jonathan Raskin (2022) warns against the "DSM-ing" of everyday life. He asks, "How about those of us who don't eat as balanced a diet as we should… Are we afflicted with 'dietary self-sabotage disorder'?" He warns against "conflating difference with dysfunction," notes that daydreaming serves pleasure, creativity and escape, and suggests "If she daydreams because she's bored, maybe the things she's doing bore her." He argues for addressing underlying causes rather than labeling the symptom. — [Raskin 2022, Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/making-meaning/202205/is-maladaptive-daydreaming-a-mental-disorder)

**Conceptual and naming problems**
- Burrell, Lawson & Christoff (2025) call the label misleading and argue that premature DSM inclusion would be "detrimental" given insufficient conceptual clarity. They note the field's "heavy reliance on Reddit self-reports from potentially unrepresentative samples." — [Burrell et al. 2025 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)

**Sampling and method problems**
- The 2025 meta-analysis lists its own limitations: mostly cross-sectional designs, self-report, many online-community recruits, and English-language publications only. — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- Somer et al. 2017 name internet interviewing and self-selection as limitations. — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- The scoping review finds the literature dominated by online, cross-sectional, young-female data. — [Mansuklal et al. 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40665643)
- Screeners over-identify: only 60% of screen-positives were confirmed. — [Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)

**Symptom of something else?**
- High comorbidity (74.4% have more than three other disorders) raises the possibility that MD is a shared feature of other conditions. The 2025 meta-analysis itself endorses a "transdiagnostic lens involving emotional dysregulation." — [Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955); [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- Some MD presentations are misread as autism. A 23-year-old man's "stimming" turned out to be MD-related movement, and he had no social-communication deficit. — [Somer & Soffer-Dudek 2025, Case Reports in Psychiatry](https://doi.org/10.1155/crps/9700504)

**Proponents' rebuttal**
- Soffer-Dudek (2022): "human behavior exists on a spectrum. If sadness or worry become too extensive… we diagnose depression or anxiety." Patients report that practitioners dismiss them ("my psychologist didn't want to listen to the problem"), and learning the term "has been utterly life-changing" for many. — [Soffer-Dudek 2022, Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/consciousness-and-psychopathology/202205/why-we-should-take-maladaptive-daydreaming-seriously)

**Not just an internet trend**
- A 2026 study of 20 literary works from the 17th to 21st centuries (*Frankenstein*, *Jane Eyre*, *Madame Bovary*, *The Great Gatsby*) found MD-like patterns long before the internet. Somer: "not merely an internet-era trend." — [PsyPost on Somer & Iyer 2026, Psychology of Consciousness](https://www.psypost.org/classic-literature-shows-maladaptive-daydreaming-is-not-a-new-psychological-trend/)

### Inferences
- The viewpoints are better seen as *lenses on different parts of the same experience* than as rival theories:
  - The **addiction/compulsion** lens explains the craving, the loss of control and the "one more scene" relapse.
  - The **dissociation** lens explains the absorption, the double consciousness and the detached feeling afterwards.
  - The **coping** lens explains *why* it started and what it is doing for the person (loneliness, stress, shame, unmet needs for recognition).
  - The **attention** lens explains the cost to focus and work.
  - The **distinct-disorder** lens argues the whole package hangs together and needs its own name and treatment.
- For someone whose main complaint is lost focus and productivity, the ADHD evidence is directly relevant. Immersive daydreaming *produces* inattention, and ADHD treatment alone may not fix MD. Conversely, a high MDS-16 score in someone with ADHD may be a false positive.
- The strongest convergence is that MD is associated with real distress and impairment, is stable over time, and responds to targeted (not generic) intervention. The weakest points are representativeness (online, self-selected, mostly female samples) and the lack of evidence on causes or development.
- The skeptical view does not claim MD sufferers are not suffering. Its target is the label and the medicalization. Even Raskin's critique points toward examining what the daydreams compensate for (boredom, unmet needs), which overlaps with the coping lens.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed article devoted to arguing *against* MD as a construct. The skeptical views found are a Psychology Today blog (Raskin), a theoretical reframing (Burrell et al.), and limitations the proponents acknowledge themselves.
- I found no head-to-head empirical test of the addiction model against the dissociation model, for example whether tolerance or escalation occurs over time.
- I found no data on whether treating comorbid ADHD (such as stimulants) reduces MD. The Turkish letter mentions methylphenidate use in a case, but outcome details were not extracted.
- I could not verify the claim, which surfaced in search summaries, that "childhood traumatization is neither necessary nor sufficient" for MD as a direct quote from a specific paper. The substance is supported by Bigelsen & Schupak 2011, the BJPsych caveat and the small meta-analytic r.

## 7. What do people living with MD say about it, in published research on online communities and lived experience?

### Takeaway
Published research on MD communities (the Wild Minds Network, Facebook groups, Reddit's r/MaladaptiveDreaming) shows recurring themes: relief and validation at finding a name, shame and secrecy (especially about pacing, gesturing and whispering), frustration that clinicians trivialize or misdiagnose it, a strong wish for help, and collective advocacy for official recognition. The communities themselves effectively helped create the field, since most early research recruited from them.

### Cited Findings

**Community size and origins**
- The Wild Minds Network had **6,198** participants and a Facebook MD group **3,490** users as of January 2017. — [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)
- A Reddit MD community has more than **121,000** members, and "hundreds of thousands" seek peer support online. — [Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)
- "Pathological immersive fantasising is not a new clinical phenomenon," but MD "has garnered recent popularity as individuals actively seek a label for their problem." — [BJPsych 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)

**What community members report**
- Community members report "distress associated with difficulty in controlling their urges to daydream, concerns about impairments in important areas of daily function…, as well as shame and efforts to conceal MD behavior, often observable by associated mimicry and gesturing."
- "Therapists tended to trivialize the condition, suggesting that it was normal," or treated better-known diagnoses "ineffective[ly] in curbing MD." Professionals "were unfamiliar with their problem and provided various diagnoses, including depressive disorder, anxiety disorder…"

— [Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)

**Interview studies**
- In the 2016 grounded-theory study, the themes included "shame and secrecy" and "failed treatment attempts." The authors call for "early identification of MD and its correct diagnoses in adulthood." — [Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016](https://doi.org/10.1097/nmd.0000000000000507)
- In the 21 "Parallel lives" interviews, participants "expressed a substantial need for help." — [Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/parallel-lives-a-phenomenological-study-of-the-lived-experience-o/)

**Advocacy discourse**
- Bershtling & Somer used critical discourse analysis of email exchanges and petitions to the APA and the UK Parliament. They found three rhetorical strategies:
  - **Professional:** appealing to scientist-practitioner identity.
  - **Social:** forming consensus groups and coalitions.
  - **Psychological:** gratitude, self-disclosure and humor.
- Somer describes this as an empowering "micropolitical process of medicalization — the bottom-up reification of maladaptive daydreaming."

— [Bershtling & Somer 2018, The Qualitative Report](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/tqr/vol23/iss8/14); [Univ. of Haifa record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/the-micro-politics-of-a-new-mental-condition-legitimization-in-ma-2/)

**Validation from naming**
- Learning the MD term "has been utterly life-changing" for many people with MD. — [Soffer-Dudek 2022](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/consciousness-and-psychopathology/202205/why-we-should-take-maladaptive-daydreaming-seriously)

**Online peer support compared with structured help**
- In the RCT, both structured interventions outperformed "relying on internet-based support forums only." — [Herscu et al. 2023 (summary)](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/mindfulness-meditation-and-self-monitoring-reduced-maladaptive-da-3/)

### Inferences
- In the lived-experience accounts, the label does real work: it reduces shame and isolation ("I'm not the only one"). The skeptics' concern is the flip side, that a label can also lock people into a "disorder" identity. Both points are relevant for a reader deciding how to talk about his own MD.
- Communities help with validation but appear insufficient on their own for change, judging by the forum-only comparison in the RCT.
- Bigelsen and the Wild Minds Network were both a source of participants and an advocate for recognition. That entanglement is part of the "self-selected sample" critique: early research partly reflected people who already identified strongly with the label.

### Gaps
- I found no published qualitative study specifically on Muslim or Arab people with MD, and none on religious coping or religious framing of MD. The only Arab-specific peer-reviewed work found is psychometric (Abu-Rayya et al. 2019, MDS-16-AR) plus student prevalence surveys (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Sudan).
- I found no first-hand published account of the Wild Minds Network's founding or current status. Jayne Bigelsen's role is documented here only through her co-authorship and the 2017 community-size figures.
- Gender-specific lived-experience accounts (for example, young men) were not found. Most qualitative samples are mostly female.

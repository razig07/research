# Interventions for Maladaptive Daydreaming (MD) and Regaining Focus: Treatment Evidence and Evidence-Based Self-Help Tools

Evidence grade labels used throughout:
- **[RCT]**: randomized controlled trial
- **[MA]**: meta-analysis or systematic review
- **[Guideline]**: clinical practice guideline
- **[Cross-sectional/Diary]**: correlational survey or daily-diary study (shows association, not effect of an intervention)
- **[Case]**: single case report or case series
- **[Model/Expert]**: expert opinion, position paper or formulation model
- **[Press/Secondary]**: press release, blog or news coverage of a study
- **[Preprint]**: not yet peer reviewed

User profile these notes are written for: a young Muslim content creator. His MD starts at work when the work gets uncomfortable. He then uses music to make the daydream stronger; he believes music is haram and is open to cutting it. Exciting events also trigger it. The daydreams are success fantasies, and they are active before sleep and in idle time.

## Q1. Direct MD treatment evidence: what studies exist, what did they find (sample sizes, effect sizes, dropout)?

### Takeaway
The MD treatment evidence is thin.
- **One RCT.** Herscu, Somer, Federman & Soffer-Dudek (2023, *J Consult Clin Psychol*) tested an 8-week, self-guided online program with these parts: psychoeducation, motivation enhancement, mindfulness, and self-monitoring.
  - Both active arms gave large improvements in MD severity, daydreaming frequency and functioning. The wait-list arm, which used online support forums only, did not improve.
  - About 24% of the full-program arm improved to a clinically significant degree and 39% improved reliably. Gains held at 6 months.
  - About 37% of randomized people did not complete the program.
- **Everything else is case reports.** These include CBT with mindfulness, CBT alone, schema therapy, fluvoxamine, and medication observations.
- **Medication evidence is weak.** In surveys, most MD sufferers report that medications do little for their daydreaming.

### Cited Findings

**The only RCT: Herscu et al. 2023 [RCT]**
- **Design.** "We tested the effectiveness of an eight-session internet-based self-help training for mindfulness and self-monitoring and compared three groups across three measurement points in time. A sample of 557 people was randomly assigned. A total of 353 participants (age M[SD] = 28.3[10.5], 76% female, 77% unmarried) completed our program." — [Herscu et al. 2023, JCCP, PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)
- **Arms.**
  - Full intervention, n = 114: psychoeducation + motivation enhancement + mindfulness + self-monitoring.
  - Partial intervention, n = 125: the same, without self-monitoring.
  - Wait-list, n = 125: "internet-based support as usual."
  - Note: the arm sizes add up to 364, not the stated 353 completers. The abstract does not explain this. — [Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)
- **Dropout.** 557 were randomized and 353 completed, so about 204 (about 37%) did not complete. This figure is my own arithmetic from the abstract. The registry lists actual enrollment as 697, which is higher than the 557 randomized, presumably because of screening. — [Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)
- **Effect sizes.** "All MD measures assessing daydreaming pathology, daydreaming frequency, and life functioning showed significant improvement with a large effect size (ES) from baseline to posttreatment in both intervention groups, whereas the wait-list group showed no significant improvement."
  - MD: F(3, 349) = 35.76, η² = 0.24
  - Frequency: F(3, 349) = 32.06, η² = 0.22
  - Functioning: F(3, 349) = 20.43, η² = 0.15
  - Source: [Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)
- **Self-monitoring and durability.**
  - "Mindfulness with self-monitoring training for MD was superior to mindfulness alone in the short term, but they both were equally efficient in the long term. Both interventions were superior to relying on internet-based support forums only."
  - "The clinically significant improvement rate of mindfulness with self-monitoring training was 24%, while the reliable improvement rate reached 39%. At the 6-month follow-up, achievements were maintained."
  - Source: [Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)
- **Outcome measures.**
  - MDS-16, scored 0–100. The registry describes a mean score of 40 as the cut-off for clinical-level MD.
  - Daydreaming Frequency Scale (DDFS).
  - Daydreaming Work and Social Adjustment Scale (DWSAS).
  - Brief Symptom Inventory (BSI) and Outcome Questionnaire (OQ).
  - Source: [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)
- **Format.**
  - The program ran for 8 weeks, with lessons of about 60 minutes each.
  - Lessons combined text, illustrations, video and audio lectures, and interactive worksheets.
  - Each lesson ended with a list of home assignments for the following week.
  - Source: [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)
- **What the wait-list group was told.** Participants were "instructed to reduce their daydreaming activity to the best of their ability" while they used the online forum. Willpower plus a forum alone did not produce significant improvement. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243); [Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)
- **Press release figures.** The university press release reports "80% of treatment participants showed significant positive change" and, for the full program, "~25% completely recovered; 40% significantly reduced symptoms." These are rounded press figures and are slightly different from the abstract's 24% and 39%. — [EurekAlert press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/980380) [Press/Secondary]
- **Peer-support coverage.** A peer-support site summarizes the result as "One-third of completers showed significant improvement; one-quarter improved sufficiently to no longer meet diagnostic criteria," and notes "a lot of people didn't see huge improvements." — [The Daydream Place (peer-support site)](https://daydreamplace.com/the-first-scientifically-validated-treatment-programme-for-maladaptive-daydreaming-disorder/) [Press/Secondary]

**Somer 2018: pilot case, CBT + motivational interviewing + mindfulness [Case]**
- **Publication.** *Frontiers in the Psychotherapy of Trauma and Dissociation*, 1(2), 1–22. One 25-year-old man was treated for 6 months, with measures taken before, after, and at a 2-month follow-up. — [Somer 2018, Univ. of Haifa record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-ontological-analysis-treatment-rationale-/)
- **Rationale.** The ontological analysis classified MD as three things: a dissociative disorder of absorption, a behavioral addiction, and an obsessive-compulsive spectrum disorder that produces attention deficits. The therapy plan was built from evidence-based treatments for those conditions. — [Somer 2018](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-ontological-analysis-treatment-rationale-/)
- **Outcomes.**
  - Daydreaming time fell by more than 50%.
  - Internet time fell by more than 70%.
  - Work and social adjustment improved by more than 70%.
  - Source: [Somer 2018](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-ontological-analysis-treatment-rationale-/)

**Other psychotherapy case reports [Case]**
- **Shanbhag & Pothiyil 2024.** A 24-year-old man received a "tailor-made cognitive approach" in four phases: building coping skills, behavior modification, cognitive restructuring, and relapse prevention. His MDS-16 score fell from 66 to 32, below the cut-off of 40. — [Shanbhag & Pothiyil 2024, Indian J Psychol Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39545109/)
- **Sharma & Mahapatra 2021.** A 16-year-old with MD plus internet gaming disorder (MDS-16 = 65) was "managed primarily with psychological intervention", focused on behavior modification and cognitive restructuring. — [Sharma & Mahapatra 2021, Gen Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33782659/)
- **Ranga & Sharma, 2026 (SAGE).** A six-month Schema Therapy plan "to address emotional triggers, build healthier coping, and reduce reliance on maladaptive daydreaming." I saw this only through a search summary and did not read the full text. — [Ranga & Sharma 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09731342251379190)

**Medication [Case / Survey]**
- **Fluvoxamine (Schupak & Rosenthal 2009).** One patient with long-standing excessive daydreaming "responded favorably to fluvoxamine therapy, stating that it helps to control her daydreaming," over more than 10 years of treatment. This is a single case. — [Schupak & Rosenthal 2009, Conscious Cogn](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19062309/) [Case]
- **Self-reported medication effects (Ross, West & Somer 2020).**
  - In a survey of 202 MD sufferers, "most of the participants reported little to no effect of drugs or medications on daydreaming."
  - The authors made only "tentative recommendations… in favor of prescribing antidepressants and against the use of marijuana."
  - Source: [Ross, West & Somer 2020, J Nerv Ment Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31688494/) [Cross-sectional]
- **Mamah et al. 2025 (pharmacotherapeutic and neuroimaging case study).** A 20-year-old woman with depression and OCD features.
  - A secondary summary reports that dopaminergic drugs appeared to increase daydreaming, while certain antipsychotics appeared to reduce its intensity.
  - The full text could not be fetched (HTTP 403), so drug names and doses are unverified.
  - Sources: [Mamah et al. 2025, Psychiatry Res Case Rep](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2773021225000069); [secondary summary, Simply Neuroscience](https://www.simplyneuroscience.org/post/maladaptive-daydreaming-in-light-of-fmri-findings) [Case; Secondary]
- **Recurring OCD link.** Somer's teaching slides point out that OCD comes up repeatedly in MD; the only medication case study involved an SSRI used for OCD. — [Somer, CPD slides "MD: Pathways to an Unrecognized Dissociative Condition"](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Daily-diary support for the OCD link.** In a 14-day diary study (n = 77), "obsessive-compulsive symptoms were the only consistent temporal antecedent of MD." — [Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018, Front Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/) [Diary]

**Reviews and position papers [MA / Model/Expert]**
- **Position paper (Soffer-Dudek, Somer, Spiegel, Dorahy, Cardeña and others, *Br J Psychiatry* 2025).** "Whereas non-specific therapy is ineffective, targeted treatment seems promising." The paper argues MD should be classified as a dissociative disorder. — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025, BJPsych](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- **Narrative review (Ricci et al. 2025, *Int Rev Psychiatry*).** "Treatment options will be discussed even if still not validated." — [Ricci et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40965365/)
- **Scoping review (Mansuklal 2025, *Clin Psychol Psychother*).** Per the search snippet: "Preliminary findings suggest that psychotherapy may reduce MD symptoms… emphasizing the need to develop and formalize evidence-based interventions." The full text was blocked (403). — [Mansuklal 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cpp.70104)
- **Critique of the RCT (Lucas & Bone 2025, CBT formulation model).**
  - "Recent pilot studies, though promising, have had mixed results."
  - Herscu's mindfulness and self-monitoring "may only target certain elements of MD (e.g. difficulty sustaining attention, compulsive cycles) and neglect others of potential importance (e.g. attachment to fantasies)."
  - Source: [Lucas & Bone 2025, The Cognitive Behaviour Therapist](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9) [Model/Expert]

### Inferences
- **Grading.** The total direct evidence is one moderately large RCT (self-guided, wait-list control) plus a handful of single cases.
  - There is no RCT of therapist-delivered CBT, ACT, MBCT or medication for MD.
  - The RCT used a wait-list/forum control, not an attention-matched placebo. Its effects therefore include expectancy and engagement effects.
- **Realistic expectations.** If he follows a structured program like Herscu's, the evidence predicts that roughly 1 in 4 people recover to below clinical level and roughly 2 in 5 improve reliably. Most people improve somewhat, and some do not respond.
- **Monitoring.** Self-monitoring speeds up gains, since the full arm beat the partial arm in the short term. That makes daily logging a high-value, low-cost component.
- **Forums.** Passive participation in online MD communities is not by itself an effective strategy.
- **Medication.** No medication has trial evidence for MD itself. Medication is relevant mainly for comorbid depression, OCD or ADHD, through a prescriber.

### Gaps
- **Follow-up trials through 2026.** I found no second RCT of an MD-specific treatment, including any trial of ACT, MBCT or therapist-delivered CBT. Searches of Europe PMC and the web through Oct 2026 turned up only the Herscu trial plus case reports.
- **Unverified naltrexone report.** A search summary mentioned a 2026 report on "naltrexone treatment for MD in a patient with treatment-resistant ADHD." I could not locate or verify this paper.
- **ADHD stimulants.** I found no peer-reviewed data on stimulant or ADHD-medication effects on MD, beyond the Ross 2020 survey's general "little to no effect" and the secondary report that dopaminergic drugs may increase daydreaming (Mamah 2025, unverified).
- **Per-arm dropout** in Herscu 2023 and standardized pre-post Cohen's d values were not in the accessible abstract. The full text (APA PsycNet) was paywalled.
- **Full Somer 2018 paper.** I could not access the full paper for exact pre/post numbers such as minutes per day or MDS scores. Percentages come from the publication record, and technique descriptions come from Somer's own teaching slides.

## Q2. What techniques do MD programs use, and does evidence favor reducing/controlling MD over full abstinence?

### Takeaway
MD programs share a common core:
1. Psychoeducation that frames MD as an addictive or compulsive pattern.
2. Motivational interviewing.
3. Mindfulness practice, both as a daily habit and as an in-the-moment skill.
4. Daily self-monitoring logs of daydreaming time, circumstances and feelings.
5. Scheduling and stimulus control, including a single designated late-evening slot for recreational internet.
6. Early interception of plots.
7. Coping statements and self-praise.
8. Accountability check-ins.

The goal in both the RCT and Somer's case was framed as **controlling/reducing** MD, not total abstinence. No study compares the two. Models describe "withdrawal" and binges after suppression, and the thought-suppression literature shows rebound, so a control-and-redirect approach is better supported than white-knuckle abstinence from the inner life. Removing **behavioral cues** completely (for him, music) is a different matter: stimulus control fits his values and the trigger evidence.

### Cited Findings

**RCT components (Herscu 2023 / NCT05235243)**
- The full intervention "received a psycho-education module about MD and its addictive behavior mechanism, a motivation enhancement module based on motivational interviewing, mindfulness modules, and self-monitoring modules." — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)
- The press release describes the components as: "Mindfulness meditation techniques for non-judgmental awareness; Daily self-monitoring of fantasy time spent; Motivation enhancement exercises"; weekly online video and text lessons with daily homework. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/980380)
- The trial's stated aim was "improving control over MD." — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)

**Somer 2018 case: step-by-step elements, from Somer's own slides [Case]**
- **Motivational interviewing.** "Open ended questions, reflective listening, affirmation, and summarization." This helped the patient "become more aware of the impending complications, consequences and jeopardies resulting from his escapist absorption… visualize a better future… become increasingly motivated to commit himself to the hard work involved in controlling his MD." — [Somer CPD slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Self-monitoring diary.** The patient "monitored his target behaviors and maintained a diary in which he noted the circumstances surrounding his MD and Internet use as well as his associated thoughts and feelings." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Scheduling and stimulus control.** He "prepared a detailed schedule for the next day's activities in which he designated a single, late evening hour for recreational Internet use," "read the day's plan in the mornings and reread a printed copy of the schedule several times a day." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Early plot interception.** "To suppress the reinforcing pleasure of his MD, Pt. strove to intercept his developing MD plots as early as possible and to terminate these plots with negative or aversive endings (e.g., a distressful twist to the fantasized plot, a reminder… that daydreaming is a detestable, self-destructive act)." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Self-reinforcement.** He was "instructed to reinforce himself with self-praise for successful interceptions of MD." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Accountability.** He "maintained accountability by texting reports of self-monitored MD/Internet time and coping measures. Intermittent feedback was provided in the form of encouraging text messages or brief phone coaching sessions to remind him of the skills and resources available to him (e.g., calling his girlfriend)." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Coping statements.** "Daydreaming is detrimental to me—it is a harmful addiction"; "I will mindfully deal with my distress in the present rather than escape into a future fantasy." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Mindfulness.** It was "practiced as a morning routine and as a versatile mode of coping with a variety of challenges." — [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)

**CBT formulation model (Lucas & Bone 2025) [Model/Expert]**
- **Maintaining factors.** Music sets "the 'emotional soundtrack' for fantasies: evoking, intensifying, and prolonging emotional responses." Repetitive music and stereotyped movement (pacing, rocking, gesturing) act as "hypnotic behaviour." The model also lists a "rush" or "wave of pleasure" on entry, yearning or craving, and attachment to ongoing "episodes which need to be finished." — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)
- **Suppression.** Suppression attempts produce withdrawal-like symptoms: irritation, feeling sick, binges. MD is framed as "experiential avoidance and unconscious attempts at repair." — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)
- **Intervention targets.** Behavior patterns, cognitive restructuring of core beliefs, exploring fantasy themes to "tend to needs within real life," emotion regulation, "reclaiming life," and treating comorbidities. — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)

**Trigger evidence that fits his pattern**
- **Discomfort at work.**
  - MDers (n = 142 of 317) had higher frustration intolerance (U = 9952, p < .01) and more avoidance-focused coping (U = 10170, p < .01).
  - In the MDer network only, MD had significant edges with avoidant coping (0.05), discomfort intolerance (0.10), entitlement (0.07) and emotional intolerance (0.10).
  - The authors conclude: "the role of discomfort intolerance and its links to avoidance should be of significant focus in MD therapy."
  - Source: [Nowacki & Pyszkowska 2024, Current Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-024-06382-x) [Cross-sectional]
- **Experiential avoidance.** MDers (n = 210 vs 218 controls) showed higher experiential avoidance, more self-judgment and over-identification, and lower mindfulness. — [Pourmoazzen et al. 2026, Iran J Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111966/) [Cross-sectional]
- **Cognitive fusion.** Childhood trauma relates to MD fully through cognitive fusion and distress (n = 366). This supports defusion/ACT-type and distress-tolerance targets. — [Karaağaç et al. 2026, BMC Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13159354/) [Cross-sectional]
- **Music.**
  - Music was "reported as the most effective tool for triggering and maintaining daydreaming, especially among MDers."
  - "Positive valence and pleasure" in music drove daydreaming, and "musical absorption predicted MD severity."
  - Source: [Ferreri et al. 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/a5cjf_v1) [Preprint]
  - Music is one of the four factors of the MDS-16 (Yearning, Impairment, Kinesthesia, Music) across cultures. — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2020, J Behav Addict](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8969720/)
- **Idle time and isolation.** MDers under COVID lockdown "spent more time in fantasy, experienced more intense and vivid daydreaming, and had a stronger urge to daydream" (n = 1,565, 70+ countries). — [Somer et al. 2020, Front Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667241/) [Cross-sectional]
- **Lockdown prevalence.** Among 6,277 Italian adults during lockdown, 17.2% were probable MDers, with higher anxiety and depression. — [Musetti et al. 2021, Front Psychol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8024516/) [Cross-sectional]
- **Kinesthetic movement.** Many adults with MD have childhood "intense imagery movements" (complex stereotypies) that "will need active management to prevent significant distress and impairment." — [Hedderly et al. 2024, Mov Disord Clin Pract, case series n = 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11145148/); see also [Somer & Soffer-Dudek 2025 case](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11955288/) [Case]
- **Daily co-occurrence.** Daily rises in MD went with same-day rises in depression, anxiety, OCD symptoms, dissociation and negative emotion, and with lower positive emotion (n = 77, 14 days). — [Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/) [Diary]

**Abstinence vs. control**
- Both the RCT ("improving control over MD") and Somer's case ("controlling his MD", one scheduled recreational slot) aimed at control and reduction. — [NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243); [Somer slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- The CBT formulation paper "does not explicitly address abstinence versus controlled use." — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)

### Inferences
- **Two kinds of abstinence.** "Abstinence" should be split into two targets.
  - **Abstinence from cues and rituals.** Music sessions, pacing or kinesthetic rituals, and the bed used as a daydream venue are behaviors, so complete removal is realistic. It also fits his religious values. Cutting music lines up his own values with the strongest known MD amplifier, which in motivational-interviewing terms strengthens "change talk."
  - **Abstinence from daydreaming as a mental act.** This is unrealistic. Thought-suppression research (Q6) predicts rebound.
  - The evidence-consistent target is: notice early, then redirect, while keeping a small, scheduled, non-immersive outlet or a constructive outlet (Q4).
- **Exciting-event triggers.** Ferreri's finding that positive valence and pleasure drive music-induced daydreaming fits his exciting-event triggers. Positive emotion, not only distress, is a trigger, so his if-then plans need an "after exciting news" cue too.
- **Aversive endings.** Somer's technique of giving plots aversive endings is a form of covert sensitization. It was never tested on its own. Given the shame and self-judgment found in MDers (Pourmoazzen 2026), self-shaming coping statements ("detestable") may backfire for some people. A non-judgmental mindfulness framing (Herscu) is the better-tested option.
- **"Unfinished episodes."** Fantasies that feel like episodes "which need to be finished" suggest a practical rule: close the episode deliberately, for example by writing one line of "where it stops." Do not leave it open. This is my inference; it has not been tested.

### Gaps
- **Abstinence vs. moderation.** No study compares the two for MD.
- **"Scheduled daydream time."** No study tests a scheduled-worry-style "daydream window." Somer scheduled internet time, not daydream time.
- **Kinesthetic replacement.** Swapping pacing for another movement is recommended in community and self-help materials but has not been tested in any MD study I found.
- **Herscu's weekly curriculum.** The week-by-week content (which mindfulness exercises, the exact log format) was not in the accessible sources.

## Q3. Adjacent, well-evidenced tools that match MD's mechanisms

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced adjacent tools are these:
- **Implementation intentions (if-then plans).**
  - Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006: d = 0.65 across 94 tests.
  - Sheeran et al. 2024, across 642 tests: d from .27 to .66, stronger with a strict if-then format, high motivation and rehearsal.
- **Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (MCII/WOOP)** in place of pure success fantasies: g ≈ 0.28–0.34, small but reliable. Research by Oettingen shows pure positive fantasies sap energy and predict later depression.
- **CBT-type procrastination interventions,** the strongest category in the van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018 meta-analysis.
- **Mindfulness-based urge and craving skills.** MBRP had lower relapse than treatment as usual and a 12-month advantage over standard relapse prevention. A 2-week mindfulness RCT reduced mind-wandering.
- **Habit reversal training** for repetitive behaviors: d = 0.80.
- **Stimulus-control-based CBT-I** for bedtime.

Evidence is weaker or mixed for Pomodoro-style fixed breaks, the "phone presence" effect, body-doubling, cognitive shuffle and constructive worry. Exercise has only correlational links to mind-wandering.

### Cited Findings

**A. Implementation intentions ("If X, then I will Y") [MA]**
- **Original meta-analysis.** "94 independent tests from 63 reports… forming an implementation intention produced an average effect of d = 0.65 on goal attainment." Benefits appeared at getting started, protecting an ongoing activity from interference, and abandoning unworkable courses. — [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Adv Exp Soc Psychol (Univ. Konstanz record)](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)
- **Updated meta-analysis.**
  - "Across 642 independent tests, forming implementation intentions proved effective for cognitive, affective, and behavioural outcomes (.27 ≤ d ≤ .66). Effect sizes were larger when plans had a contingent (if-then) format, participants were highly motivated to pursue the goal, and plans were rehearsed."
  - The taxonomy includes "task juncture" cues and "ignore- or inner speech-responses."
  - Source: [Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2024/2025, Eur Rev Soc Psychol](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)
- **Children.** g = 0.31 (42 studies, N = 12,957). "Stronger… in (some analyses) children with ADHD, suggesting that implementation intentions are particularly effective when self-regulation abilities are limited." — [Breitwieser & Reinelt 2026, Br J Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41784001/)
- **Breaking habit-like behaviors.**
  - Substance use: alcohol g = 0.31 and tobacco g = 0.31 (21 RCTs). — [Malaguti et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32622228/)
  - Smoking cessation: OR = 1.70, quit rates 10.7% vs 4.9%. — [McWilliams et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31414843/)
  - Heavy episodic drinking: d = −0.01, a null effect, and online delivery effects were weaker. — [Cooke et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36173203/)

**B. Mental contrasting / MCII (WOOP) vs. success fantasies [MA + experiments]**
- **Wang et al. 2021.** MCII meta-analysis of 21 studies, 24 effect sizes, N = 15,907.
  - Overall g = 0.336.
  - Delivery through interaction with an experimenter: g = 0.465. Delivery through a written document: g = 0.277.
  - "Because of some publication bias, the actual effect sizes may be smaller."
  - Source: [Wang, Wang & Gai 2021, Front Psychol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)
- **Cross & Sheffield 2019.** Mental contrasting for health behavior, N = 1,528.
  - g = 0.28 at up to 4 weeks, and g = 0.38 at up to 3 months (k = 5).
  - MCII: g = 0.28 (k = 7).
  - Adding implementation intentions "did not further strengthen the effects."
  - Source: [Cross & Sheffield 2019, Health Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30879403/)
- **Positive fantasies sap energy (Kappes & Oettingen 2011).** Four experiments. "Positive fantasies about outcomes created lower physiological (systolic blood pressure) and behavioral energization than negative or neutral fantasies." Fantasies that depict obstacles "should be more beneficial for mustering energy." — [Kappes & Oettingen 2011, JESP 47(4):719–729 (LSE repository)](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/why-positive-fantasies-make-your-dreams-less-likely-come-true)
- **Positive fantasies and depression (Oettingen, Mayer & Portnow 2016).** Four studies, follow-up up to 7 months. Positive future fantasies "related to decreased symptoms of depression when measured concurrently; however, positive fantasies predicted more depressive symptoms when measured longitudinally." Low academic success partly mediated this. — [Oettingen, Mayer & Portnow 2016, Psychol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26825106/)
- **Biased information seeking.** Positive fantasies create a preference for information about the pros of a goal over the cons, which "may lead to poor decisions." — [Kappes & Oettingen 2012, PSPB](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22649113/)
- **Disappointment.** The more positively soccer fans fantasized about a win, the stronger their sadness and frustration after a loss. — [Sevincer, Wagner & Oettingen 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31842663/)
- **A related self-report case.** A single self-observational preprint describes a cycle in which "imagined success precedes underperformance." — [Almasabi 2025, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/s2xr7_v1) [Preprint; Case — very weak]

**C. Procrastination and task aversion [MA]**
- **van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018.** 24 studies (k = 44, N = 1,173).
  - "A large reduction in procrastination after the interventions," and the effect stayed stable at follow-up.
  - "Cognitive behavioral therapy reduced procrastination more strongly than the other types of interventions." The types compared were self-regulation, CBT, other therapeutic approaches, and strengths-based interventions.
  - Intervention duration did not moderate the effect.
  - Source: [van Eerde & Klingsieck 2018, Educ Res Rev 25:73–85](https://dare.uva.nl/id/12627c11-4b2f-401a-b9fc-1a7ee4d7cf91)
- **Fixed breaks vs self-regulated breaks.**
  - **Smits et al. 2025 (n = 94).** Pomodoro (25 min work, 5 min break) "led to a faster increase in fatigue" and a faster decrease in motivation than self-regulated breaks. There were no differences in productivity, task completion or flow. — [Smits, Wenzel & de Bruin 2025, Behav Sci](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/) [RCT-type experiment]
  - **Göksu et al. 2026 (n = 176).** No performance difference. Self-regulated breaks gave higher energy, concentration and motivation and less distraction. — [Göksu, Wiradhany & de Bruin 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13405840/)
  - **Conflicting review.** Across 3 small RCTs (n = 87), structured Pomodoro intervals gave "~20% lower fatigue" and slightly better distractibility and motivation than self-paced breaks. — [Ogut 2025 scoping review, BMC Med Educ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12532815/)

**D. Mindfulness for mind-wandering and urges [RCT]**
- **Mrazek et al. 2013.** In an RCT, a 2-week mindfulness course "improved both GRE reading-comprehension scores and working memory capacity while simultaneously reducing the occurrence of distracting thoughts." The gains were mediated by reduced mind-wandering among those prone to distraction. — [Mrazek et al. 2013, Psychol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23538911/)
- **Bowen et al. 2014 (MBRP).** An RCT of 286 people after substance-use treatment, with 8 weekly sessions.
  - At 6 months, MBRP and standard relapse prevention both beat treatment as usual on relapse risk.
  - At 12 months, "MBRP participants reported significantly fewer days of substance use and significantly decreased heavy drinking compared with RP and TAU."
  - The proposed mechanism is "strengthening the ability to monitor and skillfully cope with discomfort associated with craving or negative affect."
  - Source: [Bowen et al. 2014, JAMA Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4489711/)

**E. Habit reversal training (HRT) [MA]**
- **Bate et al. 2011.** 18 studies, 575 participants. HRT was efficacious for "a wide variety of maladaptive repetitive behaviors" (tics, nail biting, stuttering and others), d = 0.80 versus controls. — [Bate et al. 2011, Clin Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21549664/)
- **Yu et al. 2020.** For tics specifically: 10 RCTs, n = 586, SMD = −0.43. — [Yu et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32948114/)

**F. Thought suppression (what to do instead of "just stop") [MA]**
- **Wang, Hagger & Chatzisarantis 2020.** k = 31 studies. "Rebound effects were observed regardless of cognitive load, whereas immediate enhancement effects were observed only in the presence of cognitive load." — [Wang, Hagger & Chatzisarantis 2020, Perspect Psychol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286932/)
- **Abramowitz et al. 2001.** "A small to moderate rebound effect of thought suppression." — [Abramowitz, Tolin & Street 2001, Clin Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11434226/)

**G. Sleep and pre-sleep cognitive arousal [Guideline, RCT, SR]**
- **AASM 2021 guideline.**
  - STRONG recommendation for multicomponent CBT-I.
  - CONDITIONAL recommendations for stimulus control, sleep restriction and relaxation as single-component therapies.
  - Source: [Edinger et al. 2021, AASM guideline, J Clin Sleep Med](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/) [Guideline]
  - Note: the same guideline advises against using sleep hygiene as a single-component therapy. This was truncated in the fetched abstract; see the full guideline at the same link.
- **Constructive worry (Jansson-Fröjmark et al. 2012).** In 22 insomnia patients, adding a constructive-worry session to behavior therapy (stimulus control + sleep restriction) gave a larger drop in insomnia severity (controlled d = 1.10–1.68) and in worry. — [Jansson-Fröjmark, Lind & Sunnhed 2012, Br J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22574800/) [small RCT]
- **Constructive worry vs mindfulness in adolescents (Bartel et al. 2018).** n = 232. In those with sleep-onset latency of 30 minutes or more, a 15-minute mindfulness body scan shortened sleep-onset latency compared with both constructive worry and control. Neither technique changed pre-sleep arousal. — [Bartel et al. 2018, J Sleep Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29441644/) [RCT; conflicting with the above]
- **Systematic review of pre-sleep cognition (Lemyre et al. 2020).** Insomnia is linked to planning and problem-solving thoughts before sleep. Normal sleep onset is marked by "sensorial imagery, deactivation of higher cognitive processes." Several strategies have been tested: mental imagery, articulatory suppression, paradoxical intention and others. — [Lemyre et al. 2020, Sleep Med Rev](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smrv.2019.101253) [SR]
- **To-do list before bed (Scullin, Baylor).** People who wrote a specific to-do list fell asleep about 9 minutes faster than those who wrote about completed tasks. — [TIME coverage](https://time.com/5097840/how-to-fall-asleep-faster); [HealthDay](https://consumer.healthday.com/sleep-disorder-information-33/misc-sleep-problems-news-626/to-do-list-before-bedtime-prompts-better-sleep-730215.html) [Press/Secondary of a small lab experiment]
- **Cognitive shuffle / serial diverse imagining (Beaudoin).** The technique is picturing a series of random, unrelated, neutral images in bed. A 2016 study of 154 students reportedly found app-guided shuffling improved sleep quality about as much as journaling. — [Psychology Today 2025](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-innovator-next-door/202504/a-creatively-random-idea-for-falling-asleep); [sleep.me summary](https://sleep.me/post/cognitive-shuffling-sleep-technique) [Press/Secondary; primary appears to be a conference abstract]
- **MD and sleep (Marcusson-Clavertz et al. 2019).** A daily diary of 126 MD sufferers. "Maladaptive daydreaming did not significantly predict sleep disturbances the following night (B = −0.00)." Poor sleep predicted next-day mind-wandering (B = 0.20) but not next-day MD. — [Marcusson-Clavertz et al. 2019, PLoS One](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880993/) [Diary]

**H. Environmental design: phone, apps, music access [MA / experiments]**
- **Phone presence meta-analysis (Parry 2022).** k = 56, n = 7,093. Only working memory capacity was reliably reduced by a phone's mere presence. Other cognitive functions showed null pooled effects, and "poor statistical power" was noted. — [Parry 2022, meta-analysis preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/tnyda) [MA; Preprint]
- **A positive single experiment.** One experiment found lower attention performance when a phone was present. — [Skowronek et al. 2023, Sci Rep](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10249922/)
- **Internet use.** MD is linked to problematic internet use. — [Somer et al. 2025 meta-analysis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ijop.70027)
- **Somer's case.** Daily internet time was confined to one scheduled late-evening hour, and internet time fell by more than 70%. — [Somer 2018 record](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-ontological-analysis-treatment-rationale-/)

**I. Physical exercise [Cross-sectional only]**
- **Zhu et al. 2026.** In 3,472 Chinese college students, physical activity and self-control acted in a chain between internet addiction and mind-wandering. This is cross-sectional only. — [Zhu et al. 2026, Front Psychol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13593867/)
- **Deng et al. 2022.** Physical activity and mindfulness were examined as mediators between mind-wandering and flow. This is also cross-sectional. — [Deng et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9358024/)

### Inferences
These are protocol building blocks. Step wording is mine, built on the cited mechanisms.

- **If-then plans (aim for the strict format, high motivation and rehearsal, per Sheeran 2024).** Write 3–5 plans, say each aloud daily for the first 2 weeks, and keep them on a card at the desk.
  - Work discomfort: "If I notice the urge to drift when the edit/script gets hard, then I name it ('urge'), take 3 breaths, and do the next 2-minute micro-step (e.g., write one bad sentence, cut one clip)."
  - Exciting news: "If I get exciting news, then I say 'Alhamdulillah', write one line about it, and walk for 5 minutes without audio. No headphones."
  - Idle moments: "If I'm idle (queue, commute), then I use dhikr or a prepared podcast-free micro-task."
  - Bedtime: "If I'm in bed and a success plot starts, then I switch to the cognitive shuffle, or get up if I'm still awake after about 20 minutes."
- **WOOP instead of success fantasies.** The evidence on positive fantasies (lower energy, later depression, biased decisions) is the strongest case for turning his success fantasies into a 5-minute WOOP once a day:
  1. **Wish:** e.g., "100k subs."
  2. **Outcome:** imagine it for 1 minute only.
  3. **Obstacle:** the inner obstacle, e.g., "when editing gets boring I escape into fantasy."
  4. **Plan:** the if-then plan.
  - The experimenter-delivered effect was larger (g = 0.465), so doing WOOP with an accountability partner or coach may help.
- **Mindfulness, two formats.** Follow both the RCT and Somer's case.
  - A fixed daily practice of 10–20 minutes, for example a morning breath or body-scan practice.
  - Brief "notice, name, return" reps during work. Urge-surfing style: observe the urge's rise and fall without acting on it or arguing with it.
- **HRT adapted to the kinesthetic and ritual component.**
  - Awareness training: log the first sign (reaching for earbuds, standing up to pace).
  - Competing response: an incompatible action held for about 1 minute, for example sitting, both hands on the keyboard, feet flat.
  - Social support: an accountability partner praises the use of the competing response.
  - Note: the component description is standard HRT; the source cited here gives efficacy only.
- **Pomodoro.** The evidence for a rigid 25/5 cycle is mixed. For someone who escapes during aversive tasks, the useful part is probably the **commitment to start** and a **pre-defined break activity that is not a daydream trigger** (no music, no lying down). The exact interval length matters less.
- **Phone and environment.**
  - The mere-presence effect is small and contested.
  - Removing **access** to music and streaming apps during work blocks (app blockers, phone in another room) works through stimulus control and friction, not through mere-presence effects.
- **Bedtime.** MD at night is better treated as a sleep-onset or stimulus-control problem than as a cause of poor sleep, since the diary data found no next-night effect.
  - Bed is only for sleep.
  - Phone and earbuds stay out of the bedroom.
  - Do a written to-do list or constructive-worry page 1–2 hours before bed. This closes "open loops" about work and success.
  - Use the cognitive shuffle or a body-scan audio in bed. A spoken-word or Qur'an recitation audio would be his own choice; no data on it.
  - Get out of bed if awake for a long time.
- **Exercise.** No causal evidence links exercise to less MD or less mind-wandering. Exercise can still be scheduled as an outlet for kinesthetic or arousal urges, for example a brisk walk when the "pacing" urge hits. It should be framed as plausible, not proven.

### Gaps
- **Urge surfing on its own.** I did not retrieve primary data for urge surfing as a standalone technique (e.g., Bowen & Marlatt 2009 in smokers). MBRP data are for substance use, not MD.
- **Mindfulness and mind-wandering meta-analyses.** I did not retrieve one beyond Mrazek 2013, and Mrazek 2012 (brief mindful breathing) was not verified.
- **Zeigarnik / "just start."** Evidence that "just start" works through the Zeigarnik effect was not retrieved. Recent replications of the memory effect are reportedly mixed (unverified here).
- **Body doubling.** I found no RCTs or controlled studies of body doubling. Evidence appears limited to ADHD community reports. Accountability check-ins appear only in Somer's case.
- **Timeboxing.** No controlled studies were found.
- **Exercise.** No intervention studies on exercise and MD or immersive daydreaming were found.
- **Cognitive shuffle.** No peer-reviewed full-paper RCT was found; only secondary reports of a 2016 study.
- **Scullin to-do list study.** Sample size and design details were not verified from the primary paper (J Exp Psychol Gen, 2018).

## Q4. Constructive channeling: can daydreaming be turned into goal-directed or creative imagination, and what are the risks?

### Takeaway
"Positive constructive daydreaming" is a recognized, adaptive style in Singer's tradition. MD clinicians suggest exploring fantasy themes to find unmet real-life needs.

However, no study shows that channeling MD into fiction or scripts, or into scheduled ideation, reduces MD. The best-evidenced "constructive" use of future imagery is mental contrasting/WOOP, which combines imagery with obstacles. Pure success imagery is linked to lower effort and later depression.

### Cited Findings
- **Positive constructive daydreaming.** Singer's research program emphasized "the adaptive nature of positive constructive daydreaming." Mind-wandering may occupy "as much as 50% of our waking time." — [McMillan, Kaufman & Singer 2013, Front Psychol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3779797/) [Model/Expert]
- **Fantasy themes as signals.** "Exploration of fantasy themes—where appropriate—may assist this process and help clinicians and sufferers tend to needs within real life." Compensatory themes (pride, power, success) can point to low self-esteem or unmet needs. — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9) [Model/Expert]
- **Idealized-self themes.** Common MD content includes "fantasizing about an idealized self… compensatory scripts like power, escape, and rescue." — [Shanbhag & Pothiyil 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39545109/)
- **Musician success fantasy example.** Somer's slides quote a "musician success" fantasy: "People I know suddenly realize that I am an awesome musician." — [Somer slides, citing Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf)
- **Risks of success imagery.** Positive fantasies lower energization (systolic blood pressure) and predict less effort and success. They predict more depressive symptoms over up to 7 months. — [Kappes & Oettingen 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284); [Oettingen, Mayer & Portnow 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26825106/)
- **Safer constructive alternative.** MCII is effective (g ≈ 0.28–0.34). — [Wang et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/); [Cross & Sheffield 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30879403/)
- **Music-based interventions.** The music-in-MD preprint mentions "the potential for music-based interventions to improve mental well-being," but presents no intervention data. — [Ferreri et al. 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/a5cjf_v1)

### Inferences
- **For a content creator, a bounded and written form of channeling is the defensible option.** Example: a 15-minute "idea capture" block at a fixed time, at a desk, with no music, ending in a concrete next action.
  - It turns imagery into output: video concepts, hooks, script beats.
  - It keeps the "imagery + obstacle + plan" structure that the MCII evidence supports.
- **Risks.**
  - The "ideation" slot can become a legitimized gateway to immersive MD.
  - Repeatedly rehearsing pure success outcomes, such as imagining the viral video's reception, is the specific content the Oettingen research flags.
- **Guardrails.**
  - Time-cap the block.
  - Write; do not just imagine.
  - Ban sound and movement rituals during it.
  - End every session with an obstacle-and-if-then line.
  - Track in the log whether the block increases or decreases total MD time. Stop if it increases.

### Gaps
- **Channeling and MD outcomes.** I found no study testing whether writing fiction or scripts from daydreams, or scheduled creative ideation, reduces or increases MD.
- **2026 systematic review not read.** The 2026 systematic review on MD versus deliberate mind-wandering (Current Psychology, [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-026-09120-7)) was not read in full. It may contain relevant data on deliberate versus compulsive daydreaming.

## Q5. When should he see a professional, what kind, and what to ask for?

### Takeaway
See a professional if any of these apply:
- signs of depression, especially any suicidal thoughts;
- significant anxiety;
- OCD symptoms;
- possible ADHD;
- dissociation or a blurred line between fantasy and memory;
- trauma history;
- major work or social impairment despite a few weeks of structured self-help.

MD comorbidity is very high:
- about 62% depressive disorders;
- about 72% anxiety disorders;
- about 54% OCD;
- about 77% inattentive ADHD (Somer's figures).

MD researchers state that "non-specific therapy is ineffective, targeted treatment seems promising." He should therefore look for a CBT- or dissociation-informed psychologist and name MD explicitly. He can ask for structured assessment (MDS-16, SCIMD) and for ADHD to be differentiated from MD.

### Cited Findings
- **Comorbidity in MDers.** 54% comorbid obsessive-compulsive disorders, 62% depressive disorders, 72% anxiety disorders, 77% inattentive ADHD. Also: "27% of MDers are survivors of CA [childhood abuse]; emotional neglect has the strongest relationship to MD." — [Somer CPD slides](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SOMER-Maladaptive-Daydreaming.pdf) [Model/Expert summarizing clinical-interview studies]
- **Comorbidity meta-analysis.** 40 studies, N = 24,977. MD is positively associated with depression, anxiety, dissociation, OCD, ADHD, psychotic symptoms, autism, trauma, emotion-regulation difficulties, loneliness, shame, problematic internet use. It is negatively associated with self-efficacy and self-esteem. — [Somer, Herscu et al. 2025 meta-analysis, Int J Psychol](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ijop.70027) [MA]
- **Suicide attempts.** Lucas & Bone cite one study in which "28% attempted suicide," and list escalation to suicidal ideation in the outer cycle. — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9) [Model/Expert; underlying sample not specified in my fetch]
- **Prevalence.**
  - Point prevalence is about 2.5% after clinical interview.
  - 4.2% of a community sample exceeded the screening cut-off, rising to 5.5–8.5% in young adult and student samples.
  - Only 60% of screen-positives met criteria at interview, so the MDS-16 alone over-identifies.
  - Source: [Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022, Front Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)
- **MD in people with ADHD.** Only 20.5% of adults with ADHD met MD criteria; that subgroup had more depression and loneliness and lower self-esteem. "In some cases presenting with ADHD symptoms, an MD conceptualization may better explain the clinical picture." — [Theodor-Katz et al. 2022, J Clin Psychol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)
- **Differentiating MD from ADHD.**
  - The MD screener "was compromised in the face of ADHD."
  - Adding an immersive-daydreaming measure improved correct MD identification (n = 156, four groups).
  - "Immersive daydreams are distinct mentation not necessarily characterizing ADHD."
  - Source: [Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025, IJCHP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12504816/)
- **MD in adult ADHD diagnosis.**
  - Higher MD severity predicted being diagnosed with ADHD only in adulthood (n = 214).
  - In adults with ADHD, the MDS-16 Impairment items overlap most with ADHD. Yearning and Music/Kinesthesia items are more MD-specific.
  - Sources: [Kandeğer et al. 2025, J Atten Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39758016/); [Kandeğer et al. 2026, Br J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41736174/)
- **Fantasy mistaken for memory.** One case confused vivid daydreams with real memories (false memories); PTSD and OCD were also present. — [Somer 2024, Case Rep Psychiatry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022530/) [Case]
- **Clinician awareness.**
  - MD is "not included in the diagnostic systems, and this leads to underdiagnosis and a lack of targeted treatments."
  - The MDS-16 and the Structured Clinical Interview for MD (SCIMD) are the clinical tools.
  - Sources: [Ricci et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40965365/); [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025 position paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- **Asking about MD.** "Patients should be questioned about MD during clinical assessment" (MD was coupled with excessive internet use in that case). — [Pietkiewicz et al. 2018, J Behav Addict](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6426361/)
- **Medications.** Most sufferers report little effect. There is a tentative signal in favor of antidepressants and against cannabis. — [Ross, West & Somer 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31688494/)

### Inferences
**Practical referral triggers** (my synthesis from the comorbidity data):
- Persistent low mood or anhedonia for 2 weeks or more, hopelessness, or **any suicidal thoughts**. Seek help urgently.
- Intrusive or repetitive thoughts and rituals (OCD).
- Lifelong inattention or disorganization that predates MD (possible ADHD).
- Losing track of time or reality, or confusing fantasy with memory (dissociation).
- Trauma history.
- MD causing missed deadlines or income loss despite about 4–8 weeks of the structured self-help protocol. That length matches Herscu's 8-week program.

**Professional type:** a clinical psychologist or CBT therapist, ideally with dissociation or behavioral-addiction experience. Add a psychiatrist if medication for comorbid depression, OCD or ADHD is being considered.

**What to ask for:**
1. Assessment with the MDS-16 and, if available, the SCIMD.
2. Screening for depression, anxiety, OCD, ADHD and dissociation, and an explicit MD-vs-ADHD differential.
3. Targeted CBT with mindfulness, self-monitoring and motivational work (the elements of the trial and of Somer's case), not generic supportive talk therapy.
4. Bring the published papers (Herscu 2023; Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025 position paper; Lucas & Bone 2025 formulation model), since many clinicians are unfamiliar with MD.

**A Muslim client** may prefer a clinician comfortable integrating faith-consistent goals, such as no music. MI-style values work fits this well. This is an inference; no MD-specific data exist.

### Gaps
- **Clinician directories.** I did not find an official clinician directory or a formal "what to ask your therapist" guide from MD researchers (e.g., the International Society for Maladaptive Daydreaming). Recommendations above are synthesized.
- **Severity thresholds.** I found no published MDS-16 threshold for "seek professional help" beyond the clinical cut-off. The cut-off is 40 in the registry description; some studies use 50.

## Q6. What backfires or lacks evidence?

### Takeaway
These are likely to backfire or have no evidence:
- **Pure thought suppression** ("just stop thinking about it"): robust rebound.
- **Relying on willpower plus online support forums:** no improvement in the RCT's wait-list arm.
- **Indulging success fantasies as "motivation" or "manifesting":** lower energy, later depression.
- **Generic, non-specific therapy:** described as ineffective by MD researchers.
- **Cannabis:** the survey recommends against it.
- **Sleep hygiene alone:** not recommended as a single-component therapy by the AASM.
- **Self-shaming coping:** MDers already show high self-judgment.

Lacking evidence: medications specifically for MD, body-doubling, rigid Pomodoro timing, exercise as an MD treatment, and channeling MD into creative work.

### Cited Findings
- **Thought suppression.** Rebound after suppression is consistent across studies, and the effect during suppression appears under cognitive load. — [Wang, Hagger & Chatzisarantis 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286932/); [Abramowitz et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11434226/)
- **Suppression in MD.** Suppression attempts in MD are described as producing "withdrawal" (irritation, sickness, binges). — [Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)
- **Willpower and forums.** The wait-list group, told to reduce daydreaming as much as possible while using forums, did not improve significantly. — [Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/); [NCT05235243](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)
- **Non-specific therapy.** "Whereas non-specific therapy is ineffective, targeted treatment seems promising." — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)
- **Positive fantasies.** They reduce energization and predict later depression and lower achievement. — [Kappes & Oettingen 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284); [Oettingen et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26825106/)
- **Drugs and medication.** Little to no effect reported, and a recommendation against marijuana. — [Ross, West & Somer 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31688494/)
- **Dopaminergic drugs.** These reportedly increased daydreaming in one case; secondary report only. — [Mamah et al. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2773021225000069); [Simply Neuroscience summary](https://www.simplyneuroscience.org/post/maladaptive-daydreaming-in-light-of-fmri-findings)
- **Self-judgment.** MDers show higher self-judgment, isolation and over-identification and lower mindfulness. — [Pourmoazzen et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111966/)
- **Sleep.** Multicomponent CBT-I is the strong recommendation; single components are only conditional. — [AASM guideline, Edinger et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/)
- **Rigid Pomodoro timing.** It did not outperform self-regulated breaks on productivity, and it raised fatigue faster in one study. — [Smits et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/); [Göksu et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13405840/)
- **Online delivery of if-then plans.** Online implementation-intention interventions showed weaker effects than paper delivery for alcohol (d = −0.04 vs −0.26). Writing plans by hand may matter. — [Cooke et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36173203/)
- **Lockdown and idle time.** Idle, isolated time increased MD urges and intensity. — [Somer et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667241/)

### Inferences
- **Replace "stop thinking about it" with three steps.**
  1. Notice and name it, mindfully and without judgment.
  2. Accept that the urge is present.
  3. Redirect attention to a pre-planned concrete action through an if-then plan, and remove the cue (music, phone, bed).
- **Avoid pure success visualization** as a "productivity" ritual. Convert it to WOOP.
- **Avoid harsh self-talk** after lapses. Log the lapse, identify the trigger, and refine the if-then plan. This mirrors relapse-prevention logic (Bowen 2014).
- **Do not rely on the forum** or community participation as the main strategy.
- **Fill idle time deliberately.** Lockdown data suggest unstructured idle time is a risk period, so planned "idle-time defaults" are needed: dhikr, a short walk, a pre-chosen micro-task, or talking to someone.

### Gaps
- **No MD-specific harm studies.** I found no studies on harms of any MD-specific self-help technique. Statements about backfiring are extrapolated from adjacent literatures, except the forum-only and medication-survey findings.
- **Nasheeds and other audio.** No data exist on whether non-instrumental audio (nasheeds, Qur'an recitation, podcasts) triggers MD the way music does. Ferreri 2026 implicates "positive valence and pleasure" and "musical absorption." It is therefore plausible that any emotionally evocative, absorbing audio could act as a trigger. This is untested.

# Salah and attention: focus, mind-wandering and calm in the Islamic ritual prayer

## 1. Empirical research: what peer-reviewed studies find about salah and attention, concentration, stress/anxiety, mood and cognitive function

### Takeaway
No RCT or meta-analysis has tested whether salah improves attention, concentration, anxiety or mind-wandering. The evidence consists of cross-sectional surveys (regular, attentive prayer goes with better self-reported mental health), one uncontrolled saliva-hormone study and three small EEG/heart-rate lab studies from one group. The few RCTs that exist measured only physical outcomes, so the honest framing is "associated with better well-being", not "proven to sharpen focus".

### Cited Findings

#### Grading key used throughout
- **RCT** = randomised controlled trial; **Crossover** = randomised crossover; **Pre–post** = one group measured before and after, with no control; **Lab** = small within-subject laboratory study; **XS** = cross-sectional survey (correlational); **NR** = narrative (non-systematic) review; **Qual** = qualitative interviews.

#### Attention, mood, stress and mental health
- **Ijaz, Khalily & Ahmad (2017), *Journal of Religion and Health*. Grade: XS, n=174 (mean age 21.6; 62% male), convenience sample, Pakistan.** People who prayed regularly scored higher on "mindfulness in Salah" and on mental health (RAND Mental Health Inventory) than irregular pray-ers. Those who prayed "with mindfulness" had better mental health than those who prayed without it (p<.01). Religious education, Salah education and mindfulness together explained 13% of the variance in mental health, and only Salah education and mindfulness were significant predictors. **Weaknesses:** convenience sample; all three religious scales were built by the authors for this study; all measures were self-report; the cross-sectional design cannot show direction. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28502025/)
- **Saleem et al. (2021), *Journal of Religion and Health*. Grade: XS, university students, International Islamic University Islamabad.** Frequency of prayer offering, religious activities and belief salience each positively predicted mental health (MHI-38). **Weaknesses:** one university; self-report; sample size not given in the abstract; no causal design. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32519250/)
- **Achour et al. (2019), *Community Mental Health Journal*. Grade: XS, n=300 Muslim nurses, one Malaysian hospital (UMMC).** Prayer moderated the link between job stress and well-being. The authors note that the single public hospital limits generalisation. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31111298/)
- **Achour et al. (2021), *Journal of Religion and Health*. Grade: XS, n=335 nurses, same hospital.** Salat correlated positively with life satisfaction and moderated the job stress–life satisfaction link. The abstract's phrase "Salat reduces stress" overstates what a cross-sectional survey can show. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31079348/)
- **Sobhani et al. (2022), *Journal of Medicine and Life*. Grade: Pre–post, n=83 regular practitioners, Iran.** After namaz, salivary cortisol rose in people with a low baseline (<5 ng/ml) and fell in people with a high baseline (>15 ng/ml). Alpha-amylase rose in low-baseline people, and BDNF gene expression fell (11 samples only). The authors describe the changes as moving "to approach the optimal range" and say they "need more studies". **Weaknesses:** no control condition such as sitting quietly; shifts toward the middle from both extremes are exactly what regression to the mean produces; the gene subsample is tiny. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35646186/)
- **EEG and heart-rate studies (kept brief). Grade: Lab, one research group, n=9, 30 and 20.**
  - Alpha power rose in prostration even when the movements were only mimicked, without recitation (n=9).
  - During salat, a parasympathetic heart-rate-variability index rose and sympathetic indices fell (n=30 men).
  - Gamma power was higher in real than in mimicked salat (n=20).
  - **Weaknesses:** tiny samples; no matched non-prayer control in the 2014 study. The authors' conclusions (salat "may help... minimize anxiety"; salat as "focus attention meditation") go beyond what they measured.
  - Sources: [2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21965118/); [2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24827587/); [2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27502795/)

#### Where RCTs exist (physical outcomes only)
- **Boy, Lelo & Sagiran (2023), *Saudi Journal of Biological Sciences*. Grade: RCT, n=26 elderly women (24 completed).** Participants prayed 2 vs 8 rak'ahs of Duha for 6 weeks. There was no between-group difference in oxidative-stress markers; the only change was within the 8-rak'ah group. **Weaknesses:** very small sample; the between-group comparison was null; no cognitive outcomes. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36852007/)
- **Alam et al. (2022), *Frontiers in Public Health*. Grade: Crossover, n=30 overweight adults, 4 weeks.** Salat after meals reduced body weight and body fat and raised immune-cell counts. There were no attention or mood outcomes. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36353274/)

#### Reviews
- **Chamsi-Pasha & Chamsi-Pasha (2021), *Medical Journal of Malaysia*. Grade: NR**, based on a Medline search covering 1966 to October 2020. It lists psychological, neurological, cardiovascular and musculoskeletal benefits but gives no quality appraisal or pooled effect sizes. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33510116/)

### Inferences
- **The defensible claim:** Muslims who pray regularly and report attentive prayer also report better mental health. The effect is modest (13% of variance, shared with other religious variables, in Ijaz 2017), and the direction is unknown: people with low mood or attention problems may simply find focused prayer harder. For a viewer with MD, struggling to focus in salah is not evidence of weak faith; it fits attention difficulty in general.
- In the 2012 EEG study, alpha rose with mimicked movements too. This hints that posture and stillness, not only recitation, contribute to physiological "settling", but the samples are too small to build on. One sentence of neuroscience is enough for the video.
- Claims to avoid in the video: "science proves salah cures anxiety, ADHD or daydreaming", and "brain scans prove salah is meditation". No study supports either.

### Gaps
- **No controlled evidence:** I found no RCT, controlled trial or meta-analysis on salah and attention, anxiety, mood or mind-wandering. PubMed searches run on 4 Nov 2026 returned:
  - 35 records for salat/salah/"Islamic prayer"/"Muslim prayer"/namaz × attention/concentration/cognition;
  - 36 records × anxiety/stress/depression/well-being;
  - 25 records × trial/intervention terms.
  - None tested attention with a standard task (e.g., Stroop or a sustained-attention test) before and after salah.
- **No studies of salah or khushu' in people with maladaptive daydreaming.** The only PubMed hit for "maladaptive daydreaming" plus religion/prayer/Muslim terms was a Pakistani survey of 485 members of an online MD community about academic performance and suicidal ideation. It did not study prayer. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39828827/)
- **Left out under the source rules:**
  - Newberg et al. (2015), a brain-imaging (SPECT) case series of 3 people during "intense Islamic prayer": I could not confirm the prayer types were not Sufi practices. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26296991/)
  - "Islamic mindfulness-based interventions", e.g., a 2026 scoping review in the *Harvard Review of Psychiatry*: excluded as muraqaba-style. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42725621/)
  - Saraei et al. (2024), a study of body and heart-rate synchrony in congregational prayer: not used (no attention outcome; prayer type not checked). — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39155713/)
- Regional Malaysian, Indonesian and Arabic-language journals were not systematically searched and may hold more small studies of variable quality.

## 2. Khushu' (humility and concentration in prayer): validated scales, correlates, predictors of distraction and mind-wandering during salah

### Takeaway
Measurement is new and thin. There is one purpose-built khushu' scale (Jordan, 2023), one author-built "Mindfulness in Salah" scale (Pakistan, 2017), and a general "Mindfulness during Worship Scale" (UK, 2022) that fit in Persian but fit poorly in an Indonesian sample. Khushu' scores correlate with better mental health, mindfulness, happiness and well-being, but no study has directly measured mind-wandering during salah or tested what predicts it.

### Cited Findings
- **Measure of Reverence in Muslim Praying (MRMP, "Khushoo"); Aldahadha (2023), *Journal of Religion and Health*. Grade: psychometric, XS.**
  - **Rationale:** "Many Muslims complain of straying while praying because of life's problems and psychological pressures (i.e., reflecting on topics other than prayer)."
  - **Development:** 17 experts reviewed the items, reducing them to 39. Exploratory factor analysis used n=396 and confirmatory factor analysis used a separate n=362.
  - **Structure:** four factors (**groveling/humility, focused attention, contemplation, praying-behaviour control**) explaining 67.27% of variance, with acceptable bifactor model fit.
  - **Reliability:** Cronbach's α .77–.85 for the subscales and .92 for the total.
  - **Correlates:** the total score correlated significantly with mental health, mindfulness, happiness and well-being.
  - **Weaknesses:** Jordanian samples only; self-report; the abstract gives no correlation sizes.
  - Sources: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36943556/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10943-023-01801-y)
- **Mindfulness in Salah Scale; Ijaz et al. (2017).** The authors built it for the study and established its psychometrics in the same sample (n=174). Higher scores went with better mental health. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28502025/)
- **Mindfulness during Worship Scale (MWS); Yousaf, Love, Hampson, Hedderly & Rogers (2022), *Personality and Individual Differences*.** A scale to "conceptualise and measure mindfulness during worship and prayer". — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2022.111683)
- **Persian MWS; Heidari et al. (2025), *BMC Psychology*. Grade: psychometric, n=170 Iranian university students.**
  - The original three factors (**Concentration, Presence, Absorption**) were confirmed, though two items loaded below 0.5.
  - Composite reliability was .76–.85.
  - The total score correlated with spiritual well-being (r=.37).
  - Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41316312/)
- **MWS adapted as a khusyu' measure for Indonesian Muslims. Grade: psychometric, n=220 (Yogyakarta).** Reliability was 0.715, but confirmatory factor analysis showed poor fit and the items clustered into four factors. The authors call for further testing. These details come from the journal's abstract page; I did not read the full text. — [Indigenous: Jurnal Ilmiah Psikologi](https://journals2.ums.ac.id/indigenous/article/view/11345)
- **Correlates of khushu'/attentive prayer found so far:**
  - mental health ([Ijaz 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28502025/); [Aldahadha 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36943556/));
  - mindfulness, happiness and well-being ([Aldahadha 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36943556/));
  - spiritual well-being ([Heidari 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41316312/)).
  - All are correlational.

### Inferences
- The MRMP factors map onto attention-science concepts: "focused attention" is sustained attention, "praying-behaviour control" is self-regulation of gaze and movement, and "contemplation" is processing meaning. That gives the video a ready-made structure: **body, eyes, mind, meaning**.
- **Likely predictors of distraction in salah** (by analogy with general mind-wandering research; untested in salah; see §5):
  - automaticity from years of repetition ([Mason et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17234951/));
  - low task demand, plus unresolved personal concerns feeding thought content ([Smallwood & Schooler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25293689/));
  - sleepiness ([Lim & Dinges 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438143/));
  - pressing bodily needs ([Lewis et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21058363/)).
- The classical commentary (§4) separates thoughts that arrive unbidden (forgiven) from thoughts one chooses to follow. This parallels the research distinction between unintentional and intentional mind-wandering ([Seli et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27318437/)). For someone with MD it is probably the single most useful idea: the decision point is the moment of *noticing*, not the moment the thought appears.

### Gaps
- No experience-sampling, thought-probe or self-report-plus-EEG study of mind-wandering during salah was found. There is no quantitative study of what predicts distraction in salah, and no khushu'-training trial.
- I did not retrieve the MRMP item wording or the Yousaf et al. (2022) abstract. I also did not check the MWS's conceptual background, such as whether it draws on al-Ghazali. The scale itself is a secular psychometric instrument, but the user may want to check this before relying on it.
- Malaysian and Indonesian local-language khushu' scales were not systematically searched.

## 3. The five fixed daily prayers as structure: prayer times and routine, sleep timing (Fajr), breaks and productivity

### Takeaway
Direct research is minimal. One tiny sleep-lab study (n=8) found that waking for Fajr and going back to sleep did not worsen sleep or daytime sleepiness if total sleep was kept the same. A qualitative study and a narrative review flag late Isha and early Fajr as squeezing sleep. No controlled study of prayer breaks and productivity exists. The links to break research, vigilance, habit formation and if-then planning are inference, though the general findings behind them are reasonably strong.

### Cited Findings

#### Direct studies of prayer timing
- **BaHammam et al. (2012), *Annals of Thoracic Medicine*. Grade: single-centre observational lab study, n=8 healthy men (mean age 32).**
  - **Design:** each man had one consolidated-sleep night (11:30 pm–7:00 am) and one split-sleep night (11:30 pm–3:30 am, awake 45 min, then 4:15–7:45 am). Sleep was recorded in the lab (polysomnography) and daytime sleepiness was tested the next day.
  - **Result:** there were no differences in sleep efficiency, sleep stages or daytime sleepiness "when the total sleep duration was maintained".
  - **Weaknesses:** n=8, men only, one night per condition, lab setting.
  - Source: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22347349/)
- **Zarhin (2023), *Journal of Sleep Research*. Grade: Qual, 31 religious midlife Israeli Muslims and Jews.** Prayer times shortened sleep because respondents "woke up early for prayer but could not always retire early enough". Respondents said faith and praying "helped reduce stress, which contributed to sleep quality". Among Muslims, "missing prayers could arouse feelings of guilt and unease, thus affecting sleep quality". — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36560913/)
- **Irfan & Yaqoob (2024), *Cureus*. Grade: NR.** In northern latitudes in summer, Isha "can extend late into the night, potentially shortening sleep if adherents rise early for Fajr". The authors suggest flexible sleep schedules, strategic naps and consistent routines. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38800282/)

#### Textual anchors for timing and routine (details in §4)
- Ibn Kathir on 23:9 ("those who strictly guard their prayers") reads it as "they persistently offer their prayers at their appointed times", and quotes Qatadah: "At the fixed times, with the proper bowing and prostration." — [Tafsir Ibn Kathir, quran.com](https://quran.com/23:1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- The most beloved deed to Allah is "prayer at its time" (Arabic: *al-salatu 'ala waqtiha*). — [Bukhari 527](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:527)
- "Allah's Messenger ﷺ disliked to sleep before the 'Isha' prayer and to talk after it." — [Bukhari 568](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:568)
- Bedtime routine: "When you want to go to bed, perform ablution as you do for prayer, then lie down on your right side and say..." followed by a set supplication, and "let these words be the last you say". — [Bukhari 6311](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6311)

#### General research the structure resembles
- **Micro-breaks. Grade: meta-analysis of experimental and quasi-experimental studies, 22 samples, N=2,335.** Breaks boosted vigour (d=.36) and reduced fatigue (d=.35). The effect on overall performance was small and non-significant (d=.16), and significant only for less cognitively demanding tasks. Longer breaks gave a bigger performance boost, and "recovering from highly depleting tasks may need more than 10-minute breaks". — [Albulescu et al. 2022, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/)
- **Vigilance and brief goal switches. Grade: lab experiment.** When observers occasionally switched briefly to a second task during a long vigilance task, their usual decline in vigilance was "averted". The authors explain the decline as the task goal habituating, not as resources running out. — [Ariga & Lleras 2011, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21211793/)
- **Attention Restoration Theory.** Kaplan's framework holds that some environments restore directed attention. — [Kaplan 1995](https://doi.org/10.1016/0272-4944%2895%2990001-2) A systematic review of 31 studies found "some support": benefits on three measures (Digit Span Forward, Digit Span Backward, Trail Making B), while 10 other pooled analyses "did not show marked beneficial effects". — [Ohly et al. 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27668460/)
- **Implementation intentions (if-then plans). Grade: meta-analysis.** Across 94 independent tests, if-then plans had a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d=.65); a later citing paper reports more than 8,000 participants. — [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601%2806%2938002-1); [KOPS record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f); [Wieber et al. 2015, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500900)
- **Habit formation. Grade: prospective field study, 96 volunteers, 12 weeks.**
  - Each volunteer did a chosen behaviour daily "in the same context (for example 'after breakfast')".
  - Automaticity rose along a curve that levelled off. Reaching 95% of that ceiling took 18–254 days; the often-quoted median of 66 days is in the paper's body.
  - "Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process", and more consistent performance gave a better model fit.
  - Source: [Lally et al. 2010, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- **Sleep and attention. Grade: meta-analysis, 70 articles.** Short-term sleep deprivation had its largest effect on lapses in simple attention (g=−0.776). — [Lim & Dinges 2010, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438143/)

### Inferences
*(All inference: no study tests salah times as breaks, cues or habit anchors.)*
- **Prayers as breaks:** the five prayers act as externally timed, non-negotiable breaks of roughly 5–15 minutes spread through the waking day. By analogy with the micro-break meta-analysis, the most likely benefit is less fatigue and more vigour, not a performance jump. By analogy with Ariga & Lleras, a short switch away from a long task and back may refresh focus on that task.
- **Adhan as an if-then cue:** prayer times fit the if-then format directly ("When Dhuhr comes in, I stop, make wudu and pray"). Other habits can be chained onto them, e.g., "After 'Asr I write the hook for 25 minutes". Lally's finding that habits form through repetition in a stable context, and survive the occasional miss, supports anchoring to the same daily prayer.
- **MD trigger, uncomfortable work:** his daydreams start when work gets uncomfortable. The next prayer time gives a visible end-point, so the hard stretch is bounded (time-boxing). Choosing the specific next micro-task *before* each prayer gives a re-entry plan afterwards. This is untested for MD.
- **MD trigger, bedtime:** the Sunnah bedtime pattern (no idle talk after Isha, wudu, set words "the last you say") is a fixed pre-sleep script that leaves less unstructured time for daydreaming. Because sleep loss reliably impairs attention, protecting sleep around Fajr is itself an attention strategy.
- **Fajr and sleep:** the tiny lab study suggests waking for Fajr and returning to sleep need not harm sleep if total sleep is protected. The practical risk is late bedtimes (late Isha in summer at high latitudes, screens after Isha), not the Fajr waking itself.
- Questions on how to handle late Isha or Fajr timing at high latitudes, combining prayers, or delaying Isha: ask a qualified scholar.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found on prayer times and routine, productivity, or attention, or on whether waking for Fajr affects next-day attention in everyday (non-lab) life.
- No controlled study of prayer breaks and workplace productivity was found; searches returned only legal, HR and news pieces, which were not used.
- No study of salah as a break from screen-based creative work such as content creation.

## 4. What the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah say about focus and distraction in prayer (references and gradings verified)

### Takeaway
The texts present focus in prayer as both a high goal and a normal struggle. Khushu' defines the successful believers (23:1–2) and prayer is "difficult except for the humbly submissive" (2:45). The Prophet ﷺ himself removed a distracting garment and curtain, told people to deal with food and the call of nature first, described Shaytan's "remember this, remember that", and taught a specific response to whispers. Ibn Hajar and al-Nawawi explain that passing, unbidden thoughts are forgiven, and a hasan hadith says reward is recorded in fractions (a tenth up to a half), which cuts against all-or-nothing guilt.

### Cited Findings

#### Qur'an (Sahih International) with Tafsir Ibn Kathir (abridged English)
- **23:1–2:** "Certainly will the believers have succeeded: They who are during their prayer humbly intent." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/23/1-2)
  - Ibn Kathir records these readings of *khashi'un* and *khushu'*:
    - 'Ali b. Abi Talhah from Ibn 'Abbas: *khashi'un* means "those with fear and with tranquillity" (also narrated from Mujahid, al-Hasan, Qatadah and al-Zuhri);
    - 'Ali b. Abi Talib: "Khushu' means the Khushu' of the heart" (also the view of Ibrahim al-Nakha'i);
    - al-Hasan al-Basri: "Their Khushu' was in their hearts. So they lowered their gaze and were humble towards others."
  - Ibn Kathir himself: "Khushu' in prayer is only attained by the one who has emptied his heart totally, who does not pay attention to anything else besides it, and who prefers it above all else. At that point it becomes a delight and a joy for eyes", citing the Prophet's ﷺ words "Salah was made the joy of my eye".
  - Source: [Tafsir Ibn Kathir](https://quran.com/23:1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- **2:45–46:** "And seek help through patience and prayer; and indeed, it is difficult except for the humbly submissive [to Allah] Who are certain that they will meet their Lord..." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/2/45-46)
  - Ibn Kathir: "The prayer is one of the best means of assistance for firmly adhering to Allah's orders." The pronoun "it" refers to prayer (Mujahid; Ibn Jarir's choice), so prayer is "heavy and burdensome" except for the khashi'in.
  - Because the khashi'in are certain they will return to Allah, "it is easy for them to perform the acts of obedience".
  - Source: [Tafsir Ibn Kathir](https://quran.com/2:45/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- **2:153:** "O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/2/153)
  - Ibn Kathir: "the best tools to help ease the effects of the afflictions are patience and prayer". He lists kinds of sabr: in avoiding prohibitions, in worship and obedience, and in hardship. — [Tafsir Ibn Kathir](https://quran.com/2:153/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- **29:45:** "...establish prayer. Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/29/45)
  - Ibn Kathir: "Prayer includes two things": giving up immoral and evil deeds ("praying regularly enables a person to give up these things"), and "the remembering of Allah, which is the higher objective".
  - He quotes Abu al-'Aliyah: prayer has three attributes, sincerity (ikhlas), fear, and remembrance of Allah, and "any prayer that contains none of these attributes is not truly prayer".
  - Source: [Tafsir Ibn Kathir](https://quran.com/29:45/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- **107:4–5:** "So woe to those who pray [But] who are heedless of their prayer." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/107/4-5)
  - Ibn Kathir lists the possible meanings: hypocrites who pray in public but not in private (Ibn 'Abbas); praying outside its time (Masruq, Abu al-Duha); habitually delaying it to the end of its time; not fulfilling its pillars and conditions; or not performing it "with humility and contemplation of its meanings". "The wording of the Ayah comprises all of these meanings."
  - He quotes 'Ata' b. Dinar: "All praise is due to Allah, the One Who said 'with their Salah are Sahun' [heedless *of* (*'an*) their prayer] and He did not say, 'those who are absent minded in their prayer'".
  - He also cites "the prayer of the hypocrite... pecks four... does not remember Allah except very little" (see Muslim 622 below).
  - Source: [Tafsir Ibn Kathir](https://quran.com/107:1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- **4:43:** "do not approach prayer while you are intoxicated until you know what you are saying." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/4/43)
  - Ibn Kathir: praying drunk was forbidden "for one does not know the meaning of what he is saying in that state". — [Tafsir Ibn Kathir](https://quran.com/4:43/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)
- **20:14:** "establish prayer for My remembrance." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/20/14)
- **13:28:** "by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured." — [quran.com](https://quran.com/13/28)

#### Commentary on "la yuhaddithu fihima nafsahu" (Ibn Hajar, *Fath al-Bari*, vol. 1, Kitab al-Wudu', Bab al-wudu' thalathan thalathan, p. 313)
- **Ibn Hajar:** "المراد به ما تسترسل النفس معه ويمكن المرء قطعه": the hadith means self-talk the mind *runs along with* and that a person *can cut off*. — [islamweb, Fath al-Bari](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/52/123/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7?idfrom=312&idto=313&start=1)
- **Ibn Hajar:** "فأما ما يهجم من الخطرات والوساوس ويتعذر دفعه فذلك معفو عنه": thoughts and whispers that *rush in* and are hard to repel are *forgiven*. — [islamweb, Fath al-Bari](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/52/123/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7?idfrom=312&idto=313&start=1)
- **al-Nawawi, as reported by Ibn Hajar in the same passage:** "الصواب حصول هذه الفضيلة مع طريان الخواطر العارضة غير المستقرة": the correct view is that the virtue is *still attained* when passing, unsettled thoughts occur. — [islamweb, Fath al-Bari](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/52/123/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7?idfrom=312&idto=313&start=1)
- **Ibn Hajar:** worldly thoughts are to be pushed away in every case ("المراد دفعه مطلقا"). Thoughts about the hereafter that are unrelated to the prayer are treated like worldly ones, but thoughts connected to the prayer itself are not covered. — [islamweb, Fath al-Bari](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/52/123/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7?idfrom=312&idto=313&start=1)

### Inferences
- The texts treat distraction as expected and respond to it in practical ways rather than with condemnation. The Prophet ﷺ was affected by patterns and pictures and removed them (Bukhari 373/374). He recalled a worldly matter during prayer and dealt with it straight afterwards (Bukhari 1221). He gave 'Uthman b. Abi al-'As a concrete counter-action (Muslim 2203) and gave doubters a rule for moving on (Muslim 571).
- Ibn Hajar and al-Nawawi separate *having* a thought from *going along with* it. 'Ata' b. Dinar's remark in Ibn Kathir points to the severe warning of 107:5 being about heedlessness *of* the prayer (neglect, delay, rushing) rather than passing absent-mindedness *in* it. Together these can relieve scrupulous guilt, but whether a particular prayer is valid is a fiqh question: ask a qualified scholar.
- Abu Dawud 796 (hasan per al-Albani) frames focus as a matter of degree. Even a partly attentive prayer is recorded in part, which counters the "I drifted, so it's ruined" thinking common in perfectionism.

## 5. Attention-science parallels for each text (all labelled INTERPRETATION)

### Takeaway
Most of the prophetic guidance maps onto well-supported attention principles: removing salient cues, meeting pressing bodily needs, not working while drowsy, engaging with meaning, and answering intrusive thoughts with a brief counter-action instead of suppression, plus a fixed rule that ends doubt. These are parallels that show consistency, not scientific "proof" of the texts, and they do not reduce the unseen (e.g., Shaytan's whispering) to psychology.

### Inferences
- The overall pattern: the Sunnah's distraction guidance works mostly on **context** (environment, body, timing) and **response** (seek refuge, follow the doubt rule, keep going). Attention research finds these two levers more reliable than raw willpower, though that summary is my inference from the studies above.
- Mind-wandering also has benefits such as creativity and future planning ([Smallwood & Schooler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25293689/)). For a content creator, the aim can be to *give imagination its time* (planning sessions) and keep it out of salah, rather than to "kill" it. This is interpretation.

### Gaps
- None of these parallels has been tested in salah; every row is an interpretation of general findings.
- No hunger-and-food-cue attention literature was retrieved (it was excluded from Benau 2014), so the hunger half of Muslim 560 has a weaker scientific parallel than the toilet half.
- The scrupulosity and obsessive-doubt literature among Muslims was not reviewed (one PubMed hit, Ayoub 2024, not read).

## 6. Practical ways to build focus in prayer, graded by evidence strength

### Takeaway
The best-supported practices combine an authentic text with a solid general mechanism:
- protect sleep and avoid drowsiness;
- deal with bodily needs first;
- clear the visual field;
- use the prayer time as an if-then cue;
- answer intrusive thoughts with a short counter-action and a return, not suppression;
- learn the meanings of what you recite.

None has been tested as a "khushu' intervention", so every grade below concerns the mechanism, not salah outcomes.

### Inferences
*(Tailored to the user; all inference.)*
- **Work discomfort trigger:** the most leverage is probably at the *edges* of prayers. Before the adhan, write down the next 10-minute work task. Make wudu as a deliberate "reset". After the salam, start that written task at once, before the urge to daydream sets in. This combines if-then planning, goal reactivation and bounded work blocks.
- **Daydreams during the prayer itself:** treat the first drift as forgiven and normal (Ibn Hajar, al-Nawawi). The skill to train is the *hand-off*: noticing the moment he starts going along with the fantasy, then returning to the meaning of the next phrase. Suppression ("stop thinking about it!") tends to backfire.
- **Idle time and bedtime:** the prayer timetable and the Isha-to-sleep script remove two long unstructured windows. Music was his amplifier, so a quiet, cue-free prayer space (stimulus control) matters doubly; I did not research the music question itself.
- **Success fantasies:** praying "like a man bidding farewell" (hasan per al-Albani, disputed by Zubair 'Ali Za'i) and Ibn Kathir's "emptied his heart" frame each prayer as present-focused. This is a textual reframe, not a tested technique.

### Gaps
- No khushu'-training or focus-in-salah intervention trial exists in the sources found, so no practice can be graded on salah outcomes.
- No study tests any of these strategies in people with maladaptive daydreaming. Clinical treatment of MD was outside this note's scope; others' notes should cover it.
- Exact wording of the bedtime and post-prayer adhkar beyond Bukhari 6311 was not verified here.
- Every fiqh detail (validity of a distracted prayer, sutrah, spitting gesture, sujud al-sahw, prayer timing, food versus congregation): ask a qualified scholar.

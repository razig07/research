# Dhikr, Qur'an listening and recitation, and hifz: calm, attention, mind-wandering, memory and learning (notes for a young Muslim creator with maladaptive daydreaming)

## 1. Dhikr: what do empirical studies of authentic dhikr find on anxiety, stress, rumination, attention and mind-wandering, and what does mantra research add as a labelled analogy?

### Takeaway
Direct research on dhikr is thin. There are a handful of small, unblinded clinical studies, mostly from Indonesia, on anxiety before a medical procedure and in haemodialysis. They report large effects (d of about 0.9), but they have weak controls and rarely say which phrases were used. No study of dhikr measured rumination, attention or mind-wandering. The closest analogy, repeated-phrase (mantra) meditation, gives small-to-moderate reductions in anxiety and stress in meta-analyses of RCTs (anxiety g = −0.46). One fMRI and behavioural study found that silently repeating a single word reduced spontaneous self-related thought. That is plausible support for dhikr as an interrupter of mind-wandering, but it is untested for dhikr or for maladaptive daydreaming (MD).

### Cited Findings
#### A. Empirical studies of dhikr (all clinical; none on attention or mind-wandering)
- Cancer patients in Indonesia, quasi-experimental non-equivalent control design (20 dhikr, 20 control). Dhikr for at least 10 minutes once a day. Anxiety (Visual Analog Scale for Anxiety) fell more in the dhikr group (P = 0.001; reported effect size 0.87). The abstract does not say which phrases were used. — [Sulistyawati et al. 2019, Asia-Pac J Oncol Nurs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31572762/)
- One 20-minute nurse-led "Dhikr Istighfar" session, given one hour before percutaneous coronary intervention (Indonesia, single-arm pre–post, n = 34). Anxiety fell from 22.3 ± 3.1 to 7.7 ± 2.1 (Cohen's d = 0.90), and 91% moved into the "no anxiety" category. The authors state that causal inference is limited because there was no control group. — [Wilandika et al. 2026, J Holist Nurs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42240339/)
- Dhikr combined with salawat in haemodialysis patients (single-centre RCT, Islamic hospital in Surabaya; 54 enrolled, 53 analysed). Anxiety (HARS) and sleep quality (PSQI) improved compared with control (p < 0.001). Because dhikr is bundled with salawat, its own contribution cannot be isolated. — [Wijayanti et al. 2025, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632388/)
- A multi-component "Islamic-based intervention" RCT in Malaysia (n = 62, randomised within gender) reduced anxiety in women (d = 0.75) and depression in men (d = 0.80) compared with typical-care controls. It was a package, not dhikr alone. — [Saged et al. 2022, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34981449/)
- A PubMed search (run 4 Nov 2026) for dhikr, zikr, zikir, istighfar, tasbih or "remembrance of God/Allah" returned 45 records. Most were clinical nursing studies, qualitative studies and conceptual papers. None measured rumination, sustained attention, mind-wandering or daydreaming. — [PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=dhikr+OR+zikr+OR+zikir+OR+%22remembrance+of+God%22+OR+%22remembrance+of+Allah%22+OR+istighfar+OR+tasbih)

#### B. Labelled analogy: repeated-phrase (mantra) meditation. This is not dhikr.
- Meta-analysis of RCTs of mantra-based meditation (searches to April 2021). Compared with controls: anxiety g = −0.46 (95% CI −0.60 to −0.32; I² = 33%; prediction interval −0.99 to 0.07); depression g = −0.33; stress g = −0.45; post-traumatic stress g = −0.59; mental-health quality of life g = +0.32. The authors say the evidence is "weakened by the risk of study bias and the paucity of studies with psychiatric samples and long-term follow-up." — [Álvarez-Pérez et al. 2022, IJERPH](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35329068/)
- Full text of the same review: the funnel plot was symmetric (Egger's test p = 0.68), so there was no sign of publication bias. Most of the 52 studies were rated high risk of bias and seven were uncertain. Instructors cannot be blinded, so no study had low risk in the blinding domain. About half of the trials tested Transcendental Meditation (TM). — [Álvarez-Pérez et al. 2022, full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8949812/)
- TM meta-analysis (16 studies, 1,295 participants), trait anxiety: d = −0.50 compared with active controls (10 studies) and d = −0.62 compared with treatment as usual. Reductions were larger in people who started with higher anxiety. — [Orme-Johnson & Barnes 2014, J Altern Complement Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24107199/)
- Mantra meditation for pain (7 controlled trials, n = 374, low-to-moderate quality): mental quality of life and negative mood improved, but there was no significant effect on pain, anxiety, stress, depression overall or sleep. — [Coitinho Biurra et al. 2026, Pain Res Manag](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42878220/)
- Meditation programmes in general (47 RCTs with active controls, 3,515 participants): moderate evidence for anxiety (ES 0.38 at 8 weeks), depression (0.30) and pain (0.33). For attention and sleep there was "low evidence of no effect or insufficient evidence", and meditation was not shown to be better than active treatments. — [Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Intern Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24395196/)
- The one neuroscience point: in 23 adults with no meditation experience, silently repeating one word caused a widespread drop in activity centred on the default mode network, which is linked to self-related thought. A follow-up behavioural study found less spontaneous ("intrinsic") thought during the repetition. — [Berkovich-Ohana et al. 2015, Brain Behav](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26221571/)
- Breathing: reciting the rosary or most mantras slows breathing to about 6 breaths a minute, with calming autonomic effects. Random speech does not have this effect. — [Bernardi 2005 review, Arch Ital Biol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16097498/)
- A single-case multiple-baseline study of mantra meditation (n = 57) found that people high in absorption gained across several outcomes, and that adding physical yoga reduced mind-wandering. — [Matko et al. 2022, J Clin Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35956171/)

#### C. Context: what has actually been tested for MD
- The first treatment RCT for MD tested an 8-session web self-help programme (psychoeducation, motivation, mindfulness, with or without self-monitoring). 557 were randomised and 353 completed. Compared with wait-list, there were large improvements in MD (η² = 0.24), daydreaming frequency (0.22) and life functioning (0.15). Adding self-monitoring helped more in the short term; both versions were equal in the long term. — [Herscu et al. 2023, J Consult Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)
- A scoping review of 89 MD studies describes MD as a dysfunctional coping mechanism with compulsive features and emotional dysregulation. Most studies are online cross-sectional surveys of young women. — [Mansuklal et al. 2025, Clin Psychol Psychother](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40665643/)

### Inferences
- **Evidence grades (my own judgement, GRADE-style: high, moderate, low, very low):**
  - Dhikr lowers short-term anxiety in stressed medical patients: **low to very low**. The direction is consistent, but n is 34–62, there is one country, no blinding, passive or no controls, outcomes are measured straight after the session, and the formulae are unspecified.
  - Dhikr improves sleep: **very low**. One RCT, bundled with salawat, in haemodialysis patients.
  - Dhikr reduces rumination or mind-wandering, or improves attention: **no direct evidence**.
  - Mantra-style repetition reduces anxiety and stress (analogy): **low to moderate**. Small-to-moderate effects are consistent, but most trials are at high risk of bias and half are TM.
  - Silent repetition reduces spontaneous self-related thought (analogy): **very low**. One fMRI study with a behavioural follow-up, n = 23.
- **Common weaknesses to flag in the video:** small samples; single centre or single country; no blinding (impossible for a devotional act); no *active* control, such as a neutral repeated phrase or calming audio, so expectancy, meaning and simple "time out" effects cannot be separated; very short follow-up; self-report outcomes; and participants who are believers under acute medical stress, so the results may not carry over to a healthy young man at a desk.
- **Why the analogy is imperfect:** mantra trials mostly involve taught programmes, such as TM at about 20 minutes twice a day. Dhikr is usually brief and spread through the day, and it carries meaning, devotion and expectation of reward. These differences could strengthen the effects, weaken them or change their nature. None of this has been tested.
- **The most MD-relevant mechanism is plausible but unproven.** MD episodes are vivid internal *narratives*. A repeated verbal phrase occupies the inner-speech channel that narrative fantasy also uses, so dhikr on the tongue may compete with the daydream rather than run beside it. This is inferred from Berkovich-Ohana 2015 and the articulatory-suppression work in Section 5.
- The only effective MD treatment tested pairs attention training with **self-monitoring** (logging triggers and episodes). A practical inference is that dhikr will probably work best as a *replacement behaviour within a monitored plan*: note the urge, say a short dhikr, return to the task, log it. It is unlikely to work as a stand-alone cure. This is not muraqaba-style practice; it is ordinary habit tracking.

### Gaps
- No study of authentic dhikr measured mind-wandering, attention, rumination or MD, and none ran an active control or followed people for more than a few weeks.
- Most dhikr studies do not report which formulae were used, so it cannot be confirmed that they match tasbih, tahmid, takbir, tahlil, istighfar or the authentic adhkar.
- Left out because the content or tradition of the practice was unclear: a 2013 Iranian CABG trial of "spiritual/religious training congruent with Zikr" ([PMID 23942577](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23942577/)) and a 2024 Aceh cohort of "dhikr assembly" members ([PMID 38445035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38445035/)). One more PubMed study was excluded because it defined dhikr in Sufi terms; per the source rules, it is not described.
- Not reviewed beyond the title: Hidayat et al. 2024, dhikr plus prayer therapy for appendectomy pain ([PMID 37277995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37277995/)).
- Not retrieved: Lynch et al. 2018, a systematic review of mantra meditation, and a dedicated search of the Journal of Muslim Mental Health.

## 2. Qur'an listening and recitation: anxiety, stress, pain and sleep (effect sizes and quality), attention and cognition, and reciting versus listening

### Takeaway
Many small trials, mostly from Iran, Indonesia and Jordan, report that listening to recitation lowers state anxiety in acute medical settings, with some evidence for pain. Sleep evidence is very thin. Evidence on attention and cognition is limited to EEG studies with high risk of bias and to observational studies of older readers. The only published meta-analysis with a pooled number (anxiety before elective surgery: 9 RCTs, all Iranian, 652 patients) reports an effect of about −8.9, which is apparently a raw difference in anxiety-scale points, not a standardised effect size. Where the Qur'an was compared with music, the effects were similar. Reciting and listening have barely been compared directly.

### Cited Findings
#### A. Systematic, scoping and meta-analytic reviews
- **Ghiasi & Keramat 2018 (systematic review):** 28 RCTs and quasi-experiments of listening to recitation for anxiety, mostly measured with the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory. They found "a positive effect ... in various settings", and only one study was non-significant. The authors call for "methodologically strong randomized controlled trials". — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30386389/)
  - Full text: Iranian databases were searched. The authors "could not find any evidence assessing the effect of Holy Quran recitation on anxiety in other Islamic countries. Hence, this systematic review focused on studies that conducted in Iran." They say "the low methodological quality and the high heterogeneity" call for caution, and "all included studies failed to provide information about the blinding of participants, personnel and outcome assessors." — [PMC6178573](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6178573/)
- **Zarea Gavgani et al. 2022 (meta-analysis, *Archive for the Psychology of Religion* 44(2):111–126):** RCTs of listening to recitation before elective surgery. 12 studies in the qualitative synthesis and 9 in the meta-analysis. The pooled effect is reported as "d = −8.893 (95% CI −10.763 to −7.022)", with benefit in both major and minor surgery and "no publication bias". — [relbib.de record](https://relbib.de/Record/1815281626/Details)
  - Preprint versions say all included studies were in Iran (652 patients) and describe the estimate as a difference in means. I could not open the full text; these details come from search-indexed text. — [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-48044/v1)
- **Moulaei et al. 2023 (scoping review):** 15 studies, all in Asian countries. Outcomes were anxiety (45%), stress (30%) and depression (25%). The authors call recitation and listening a "simple, affordable, practical and cost-effective" nonpharmacological treatment. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078304/)
  - Full text: most studies were from Indonesia (5) and Iran (3). One study is listed as reporting "improving the level of mood and attention". — [PMC10704108](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10704108/)
- **Che Wan Mohd Rozali et al. 2022 (systematic review of listening, reciting and memorising):** 20 of 230 records were included. Favourable effects were reported on depression, anxiety, physiological measures, quality of life, sleep quality and IQ. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36119448/)
  - Full text: no RCT gave clear information on allocation concealment, and all failed to report blinding. Some samples were tiny (n = 6 and n = 10 healthy volunteers). The one sleep study had older adults (n = 65) listen for 20 minutes before sleep for 4 weeks, compared with no intervention. The authors call the field "greatly understudied" with "many inconclusive findings". — [PMC9470760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9470760/)
- **Owens et al. 2023 (systematic scoping review):** 44 studies. Interventions included salah and supplication, recitation, reading, memorising and listening. There was a "paucity of evidence from Western countries", and interventions were mostly biomedical. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37408452/)
- **PubMed coverage:** a search pairing Quran/Koran with anxiety, stress or depression and with meta-analysis or systematic/scoping review returned 17 records (4 Nov 2026). The Qur'an-specific ones are the reviews above. No PubMed-indexed meta-analysis gives a standardised pooled effect size. — [PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=%28Quran+OR+Koran+OR+%22Holy+Qur%27an%22%29+AND+%28anxiety+OR+stress+OR+depression%29+AND+%28meta-analysis+OR+%22systematic+review%22+OR+%22scoping+review%22%29)

#### B. Representative trials, including the Qur'an compared with music
- 238 cancer patients before chemotherapy were randomised to Qur'an, music or control. Both the Qur'an and music reduced anxiety, with **no difference between them**. — [Al-Jubouri et al. 2021, Nurs Open](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587831/)
- Partially randomised patient-preference trial during MRI (n = 400): Qur'an, music, nature sounds or no sound. The Qur'an group had the lowest anxiety and stress and music came second; people chose their own condition. — [Abuejheisheh et al. 2026, J Health Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42844849/)
- RCT after coronary bypass surgery (Jordan, 4 hospitals, n = 132): listening for 10 minutes twice a day. Pain fell from 6.82 to 4.65 within the group, and ICU stay (5.0 vs 6.58 days) and hospital stay (10.15 vs 15.01 days) were shorter than with usual care. Registered as NCT05419554. — [Eid Aburuz et al. 2023, Crit Care Res Pract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37965250/)
- Orthopaedic surgery RCT in Indonesia (11 per group): murottal and classical music **both** reduced pain compared with control, and neither changed IL-6. — [Prihatno et al. 2025, J Pain Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41229901/)
- Surah al-Rahman for women hospitalised with depression: 12 patients, 6 per group. — [Rafique et al. 2019, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28900859/)
- Iranian neurosurgery candidates: "the sound of the Quran" reduced anxiety. Effect sizes are reported as percentages (53.5% and 40%) without a standard metric. — [Salari et al. 2025, Health Sci Rep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40201704/)
- A 2022 trial comparing Qur'an recitation, nature sounds and control before surgery reportedly found a **non-significant** difference between the Qur'an and control groups. This comes from a search snippet only; I did not read the paper. — [Pakistan J Psychol Res abstract](https://pjpr.scione.com/cms/abstract.php?id=18)

#### C. Attention, cognition and EEG
- Systematic review of 22 EEG studies: alpha and theta power rose during listening (read as relaxation), including in non-Muslims. There were "significant variations in study designs ... with many studies displaying a high risk of bias." — [Majidi et al. 2025, Iran J Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40521283/)
  - Full text: only 2 of 22 studies were at low risk of bias. A meta-analysis was impossible because of insufficient data, and samples were small; one study compared the Qur'an with "hard music" in 3 healthy males. — [PMC12159570](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12159570/)
- Older adults: 5 mostly cross-sectional studies, 4 of which found that more or longer Qur'an reading was linked to better cognitive function. — [Riviati et al. 2024, Gerontol Geriatr Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38505741/)
  - Full text: by AXIS appraisal, one study was high risk of bias, two moderate and the rest low. Arabic-language studies were excluded. — [PMC10949544](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10949544/)
- Across these reviews I found no study that measured mind-wandering or sustained attention *during* Qur'an listening or reciting. (Searches cited above.)

#### D. Reciting versus listening
- **Empirical comparison (non-randomised):** three groups. Passive control (n = 20; could not read Qur'anic Arabic), sight-reading (n = 30) and recitation from memory (n = 30). Both active recitation modes reduced cortisol compared with passive rest, with no difference between them. Recitation from memory raised post-task salivary CREB. The authors note there was no non-Qur'anic vocalisation or neutral reading control, "so the specificity ... to Qur'anic content cannot be determined." — [Okur et al. 2026, Brain Behav](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42605212/)
- The reviews pool recitation and listening together, and I found no head-to-head trial on anxiety, sleep or attention. — [Moulaei et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10704108/); [Che Wan Mohd Rozali et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9470760/)
- **Textual context (authentic):**
  - On reciting: a man recited Surat al-Kahf, a cloud came over him, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "That was As-Sakina (tranquility) which descended because of (the recitation of) the Qur'an." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 5011](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5011)
  - On listening: the Prophet ﷺ asked Ibn Mas'ud to recite to him. At 4:41 he said "Enough for the present," and "his eyes were overflowing with tears." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 5050](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5050)
  - Those who "assemble in ... the houses of Allah (mosques) and recite the Book of Allah and ... learn and teach the Qur'an ... there would descend upon them tranquility". — [Sahih Muslim 2699](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2699)

### Inferences
- **Evidence grades (my judgement):**
  - Listening lowers acute state anxiety compared with usual care: **low**.
  - Pain: **low**. A few RCTs, compared with usual care.
  - Sleep: **very low**. One small study of older adults inside a review, plus the dhikr and salawat RCT in Section 1.
  - Attention and cognition: **very low**. EEG alpha is not a measure of attention, and the older-adult studies are correlational.
  - The Qur'an beats other calming audio: **not supported**. Head-to-head trials show parity with music.
- **Main quality problems:**
  - One-country concentration: all studies were Iranian in two reviews, and all were Asian in another.
  - No blinding.
  - Passive or no-treatment controls.
  - Small samples.
  - Outcomes measured immediately after the session.
  - With about 9 studies, publication-bias tests have little power, so "no publication bias" is weak reassurance.
  - Implausible numbers: a −8.9 "d", and a preprint SMD of −2.06 (see Gaps). These suggest mislabelled metrics or small-study inflation.
- **Fit to this person:** the trials were in patients facing surgery, chemotherapy or dialysis, where any calming, meaningful audio helps. Lower anxiety in the moment does not show that recitation improves focus at a desk. Equal effects with music suggest that much of the acute calm comes from generic relaxation, distraction and expectancy. Meaning and devotion may add something for believers, but that has not been tested.
- **Reciting versus listening:**
  - *Reciting* uses the voice, breath and memory retrieval. That makes it harder to daydream through than *passive listening*, which can become a backdrop for imagery in the way music is. This is inferred from Okur 2026 (active recitation reduced cortisol compared with passive rest), Bernardi 2005 (recitation paces breathing) and Berkovich-Ohana 2015 (repeated speech dampens self-generated thought).
  - So for MD, *active* recitation or dhikr is the better interrupter.
  - *Attentive* listening, for example following the translation, is a separate, focused activity.

### Gaps
- No standardised pooled effect size has been verified. A 2020 Research Square preprint, "The effectiveness of listening to the Holy Quran to improve mental disorders and psychological well-being: Systematic review and meta-analysis", is reported in search snippets as giving SMD −2.06 (95% CI −2.57 to −1.54) for anxiety. The full text could not be extracted, and an SMD that large is implausible for a behavioural intervention. — [Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-44376/v1)
- No sleep-specific systematic review or meta-analysis was found.
- No trial in healthy young adults on focus, mind-wandering or work performance.
- No study tested whether understanding the Arabic changes the effects.
- No head-to-head trial of reciting versus listening on anxiety.

## 3. Hifz: memory, working memory, cognition and academic results; causal versus selection effects; how muraja'ah maps onto learning science

### Takeaway
Huffaz show favourable associations with memory, IQ, quality of life and later-life health in observational studies. But the only study with matched controls and standard memory tests found **no general memory advantage**: hifz built a very specific skill, not a bigger memory. There are no RCTs or baseline-controlled longitudinal studies, so selection, survivorship and confounding cannot be ruled out. The traditional method does map closely onto three of the best-supported principles in learning science. New lessons (sabaq) are learned to fluency, which is overlearning. Recent and old material (sabqi and manzil) are revised on a schedule, which is spacing. Recitation to a teacher and in prayer is retrieval practice with feedback.

### Cited Findings
#### A. Studies of huffaz and tahfiz students
- **The key null result:** 10 huffaz, 12 Muslim controls with a similar background and 10 non-Muslims were tested on standard verbal and visuospatial learning. There were "no differences ... in their capacity to memorise verbal or visuospatial material and hence no evidence of generalisation of learning capacity in the Hafiz group." Half of the huffaz did not understand Arabic, yet their Qur'anic memory equalled that of those who did. — [Black et al. 2020, Memory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32870071/)
- **Evidence synthesis:** 7 observational studies, 690 students. 5 of 7 found a significant positive association with at least one cognitive or psychological outcome, 1 was neutral and 1 uncertain, and none was negative. Memory performance, IQ and quality of life were the most consistent outcomes. The authors stress that "the observational design precludes causal interpretation." Their weighted score of 71.5% was labelled "moderate" by their own vote-counting scheme. — [Souissi et al. 2026, NeuroRehabilitation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42862738/)
- Saudi men aged 55 and over (cross-sectional): those who had memorised 10 or more sections were 64%, 71% and 81% less likely to have hypertension, diabetes and depression than those with less than half a section, after adjustment. — [Saquib et al. 2017, SAGE Open Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29163949/)
- MRI, cross-sectional, ages 35–80 (19 full huffaz, 28 partial, 16 non-memorisers): full huffaz had larger grey- and white-matter volumes than controls. — [Rahman et al. 2020, Acta Neurobiol Exp](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32214278/)
- Teenagers (16 tahfiz, 16 non-tahfiz): heart-rate variability differed by task (recalling familiar verses, learning new ones, recalling new ones). Pages memorised correlated r = 0.61 with LF/HF. — [Ghazali et al. 2024, World J Clin Cases](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39417055/)
- Children and young adults who memorised passages "despite limited Arabic comprehension": background noise reduced recognition of missing final words. Exploratory ERP study. — [D'Angiulli et al. 2026, Brain Cogn](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42843130/)
- One systematic review reports favourable effects on IQ (across its listening, reciting and memorising studies, not memorisation alone), but notes very small samples and no reporting of blinding. — [Che Wan Mohd Rozali et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9470760/)
- PubMed held only 17 records pairing the Qur'an with memorisation, hafiz, huffaz or tahfiz terms (4 Nov 2026), and none was an RCT or a longitudinal study with baseline cognition. — [PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=%28Quran+OR+Koran%29+AND+%28memorization+OR+memorisation+OR+memorizer*+OR+hafiz+OR+huffaz+OR+tahfiz+OR+hafidz%29)

#### B. Learning science relevant to muraja'ah
- **Spacing:** a meta-analysis of 839 assessments in 317 experiments found spaced study beats massed study, and the best gap between sessions grows as the retention interval grows. — [Cepeda et al. 2006, Psychol Bull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)
- **Optimal gap:** in more than 1,350 learners, the best review gap was about 20–40% of the test delay for a 1-week test and about 5–10% for a 1-year test. — [Cepeda et al. 2008, Psychol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/)
- **Retrieval practice:** being tested beats restudying. Recall tests give larger benefits than recognition tests, which supports "effortful processing". — [Rowland 2014, Psychol Bull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150680/)
- **In real classrooms:** across 222 studies and 48,478 students, quizzing raised achievement (g = 0.499). The effect was moderated by corrective feedback and the number of test repetitions. — [Yang et al. 2021, Psychol Bull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683913/)
- **Technique ratings:** a review of 10 study techniques rated practice testing and distributed practice as the highest-utility techniques. — [Dunlosky et al. 2013, Psychol Sci Public Interest](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)
- **Overlearning:** a meta-analysis found a significant, moderate effect on retention, moderated by the degree of overlearning, the type of task and the length of the retention interval. — [Driskell, Willis & Copper 1992, J Appl Psychol (abstract record)](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/373035)
  - In 218 students, overlearners recalled far more at 1 week, but "this difference decreased dramatically thereafter". The authors call overlearning on its own inefficient for long-term retention. They also note that most earlier overlearning studies used retention intervals of a week or less. — [Rohrer et al. 2005, Appl Cogn Psychol](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1772/)
  - Press summary: the benefit had largely vanished by four weeks. — [ScienceDaily 2007](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/08/070829122934.htm)
- **Sleep between sessions:** 40 people learned foreign vocabulary to a perfect score. Sleeping between learning and relearning halved the practice needed to relearn and improved retention at 1 week and at 6 months. — [Mazza et al. 2016, Psychol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27530500/)

#### C. Traditional revision structure (descriptions of teaching practice, not religious sources)
- The common South Asian and Indonesian structure has three tracks:
  - **sabaq**: the new lesson;
  - **sabqi**: recently memorised portions, defined variously as the last 7 days or the last 7–30 days;
  - **manzil**: older portions in rotation, for example the whole memorised amount split into 7 parts, one a day.
  - Sessions often run sabqi, then manzil, then sabaq. Definitions differ by teacher and institution.
  - Sources: [Quran Sheikh revision schedule](https://www.quransheikh.com/quran-hifz-revision-schedule/); [study of the Saba', Sabqi, Manzil method in an Indonesian boarding school](https://ejournal.iainpalopo.ac.id/index.php/jiis/article/view/12297); [account of the subcontinent and Panipati process](https://medium.com/how-to-memorise-the-quran/the-way-to-memorise-the-qur%C4%81n-in-the-subcontinent-a-detailed-process-869c429ab2df)

### Inferences
- **Causal or selection? Mostly unresolved, and leaning towards selection and specificity:**
  - *Selection:* children who are enrolled in, and who complete, hifz may already differ in memory aptitude, conscientiousness, family religiosity and structure, and schooling.
  - *Survivorship:* dropouts are not studied.
  - *Reverse causation:* better memory makes completing hifz easier.
  - *Confounding:* in older adults, community, lifestyle, mosque attendance and social support may explain the health and brain-volume links seen in Saquib 2017 and Rahman 2020.
  - *Specificity:* Black 2020 is the only study with matched controls and standard tests, and it found no general memory gain. That fits the usual finding that practice improves the trained skill more than general capacity.
  - Huffaz are excellent at retaining *this* text. Claims that hifz "upgrades your brain" go beyond the evidence. Grade for causal cognitive benefit: **very low**.
- **Mapping the method onto learning science (inference, consistent with the sources above):**

| Traditional element | Learning-science principle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sabaq repeated until fluent, recited without the text | Retrieval practice + overlearning | Overlearning alone fades within weeks (Rohrer 2005), so sabaq only lasts when followed by sabqi and manzil |
| Sabqi (last ~7–30 days) daily | Short-gap spaced retrieval | Matches the short-delay end of Cepeda 2008's ridgeline |
| Manzil rotation (e.g., 1/7 per day; weekly or monthly khatm, cf. Bukhari 5052) | Long-gap cumulative spaced retrieval | Gaps lengthen as material ages, as Cepeda 2008 recommends for long retention |
| Reciting to a teacher who corrects errors | Retrieval with corrective feedback | Feedback is a moderator of testing benefits (Yang 2021) |
| Annual full review with a partner (Jibril, Bukhari 4998) | Very long-interval cumulative review; recitation with a partner checks accuracy | The "camel" hadiths (5031–5033) describe forgetting when maintenance stops |
| Struggling yet persisting gets double reward (Bukhari 4937 / Muslim 798) | Effortful recall | Rowland 2014: effortful recall tests give bigger benefits. A religious motive to tolerate difficulty is an inference |
| Evening learning, morning revision | Sleep between sessions | Mazza 2016. This is my suggestion, not a documented hifz rule |
| Revising in salah | Retrieval in varied contexts | Mentioned in practitioner sources; not separately tested |

- **For him as a creator:** the same structure applies to learning scripts, Arabic or anything else. New material each day, short-gap review, a long rotation and self-testing rather than rereading. Hifz is also a ready-made "meaningful effort" task for idle time and before sleep (see Section 5). It is effortful and sequential, so it is harder to daydream through than passive listening. That last point is an inference.

### Gaps
- No RCT, natural experiment or longitudinal study measures cognition *before* hifz begins. Souissi 2026's seven included studies could not be read individually (not open access).
- No study compares hifz *methods* (sabaq/sabqi/manzil against alternatives) on retention, and none tests muraja'ah schedules against spaced-repetition algorithms.
- Academic achievement of tahfiz students compared with matched peers is not separated in the retrieved sources; it may sit inside Souissi 2026.
- The original Driskell et al. 1992 paper was not opened; its effect size (d) and number of studies vary across secondary sources.

## 4. His music-to-daydream problem: does voice-only recitation, or a nasheed without instruments, trigger imagery the way music does? What does the irrelevant-speech effect imply for background recitation? And the adab of 7:204.

### Takeaway
No study tests whether recitation or instrument-free nasheeds trigger vivid imagery or daydreaming. That is a genuine gap. Music research suggests the main drivers are emotion and valence (heroic or sad), a steady beat, absorption, and a track's learned link to the daydream ritual. Solitude and repetitive movement drive MD whatever the audio. By inference, recitation lacks the drum beat and the genre-coded "heroic" emotion. It keeps the human voice, emotional delivery and familiarity, and it could become conditioned to daydreaming if used in the same pacing-with-headphones ritual. So it can replace the *calming* role of music, but it will not automatically break the *ritual*. On background use, intelligible speech reliably harms reading comprehension and writing a little, while non-understood foreign speech mostly harms verbal serial memory. Whether playing recitation in the background fits the adab of 7:204 is a question for a qualified scholar.

### Cited Findings
#### A. MD and music
- In 21 phenomenological interviews, MD was "typified by complex fantasized mental scenarios that were often laced with emotionally compensatory themes involving competency, social recognition, and support". Solitude was needed, and "kinesthetic activity and/or exposure to evocative music also appeared to be essential features." — [Somer et al. 2016, J Trauma Dissociation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26943233/)
- MD involves "fanciful, narrative and emotional daydreaming for hours on end, often relying on stereotypical movements and music to facilitate the absorbed state". In a US general sample (N = 2,512), suspected MD was linked to distress, loneliness and suicidality. Even after controlling for distress, people with suspected MD were 2.44 times as likely to have recently attempted suicide. — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2024, Compr Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38061294/)
- Point prevalence of MD was about 2.5% in an Israeli population sample, and 5.5–8.5% of young adult and student samples scored above the cutoff. MD is described as happening "at times with the aid of music and/or repetitive movements". — [Soffer-Dudek et al. 2022, Front Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35573338/)
- The standard 16-item scale has four factors: Yearning, Kinesthesia, Impairment and **Music**. The Music and Kinesthesia items capture more MD-specific features than the Impairment items. — [Kandeğer et al. 2026, Br J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41736174/)
- MD scores go with dissociative absorption, obsessive-compulsive symptoms, attention deficit and a "high sense of presence during daydreaming". — [Somer et al. 2016, Conscious Cogn](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26707384/)

#### B. What drives music-evoked imagery and mind-wandering
- Mechanisms by which music evokes emotion: brain-stem reflex, **rhythmic entrainment**, **evaluative conditioning**, contagion, **visual imagery**, **episodic memory**, musical expectancy and aesthetic judgement (BRECVEMA). — [Juslin 2013, Phys Life Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23769678/); [Juslin & Västfjäll 2008, Behav Brain Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18826699/)
- **Emotion steers thought content.** In 62 people, both heroic and sad music produced mind-wandering. Heroic music "evoked more positive, exciting, constructive, and motivating thoughts", and sad music evoked "more calm or demotivating thoughts". — [Koelsch et al. 2019, Sci Rep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31311967/)
- **Sad music drives more inward thought.** Across three experiments, sad music produced stronger mind-wandering than happy music, along with greater default-mode-network centrality. That is the only brain detail needed here. — [Taruffi et al. 2017, Sci Rep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29089542/)
- **In daily life**, sampled over 10 days in 26 people, the emotions music evoked predicted how positive or negative mind-wandering thoughts were. — [Taruffi 2021, IJERPH](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34886046/)
- **Music writes the themes of imagination.** In 100 people imagining a journey, task-irrelevant music (compared with silence) produced longer reports and imagery marked by affect, social dynamics and **confidence**. — [Taruffi et al. 2023, Imagin Cogn Pers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37928803/)
- **Beat and "human feel":**
  - In 100 people, "repetitive quasi-isochronous drumming enhanced mental imagery vividness", more so with natural micro-variation in timing and loudness.
  - Micro-varied drumming also increased imagined distance and time travelled compared with silence.
  - Absorption, the ability to imagine vividly and musical training interacted with these effects.
  - [Ayyildiz et al. 2025, Sci Rep](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40846722/)
- Music listening evokes story-like visual imagery whose content is partly idiosyncratic and partly shared. — [Hashim et al. 2023, PLoS One (title; correction notice PMID 39752350)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0293412)
- **Counterpoint:** in 40 students on a low-demand vigilance task, self-chosen background music *reduced* mind-wandering and increased task focus, though reaction times did not improve. — [Kiss et al. 2021, Psychol Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32748062/)
- Curiosity about the music was negatively linked to mind-wandering during listening. — [Pérez Ariza et al. 2025, Behav Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40150287/)

#### C. Irrelevant speech, background sound, reading and writing
- **Bayesian meta-analysis of 65 studies:** noise, speech and music all have "a small but reliably detrimental effect on reading performance". "Intelligible speech and lyrical music resulted in the biggest distraction." Adults and children did not differ. — [Vasilev et al. 2018, Perspect Psychol Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29958067/)
  - Funnel plots showed some heterogeneity but no clear asymmetry, so there was no evidence of publication bias. — [PMC6139986](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6139986/)
- **Eye-tracking:** English speech that readers could understand caused more regressions and rereading than Mandarin they could not understand, or silence. The distraction was "mostly semantic", and comprehension suffered when rereading was prevented. — [Vasilev et al. 2019, J Exp Psychol Hum Percept Perform](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31436455/)
- Foreign-language background speech did not disrupt sentence processing, while meaningful speech did. — [Hyönä et al. 2016, PLoS One](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27003410/)
- Meaningful, but not meaningless, background speech increased rereading. — [Yan et al. 2018, Q J Exp Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28590881/)
- **Lyrics:** music with lyrics hurt verbal memory, visual memory and reading comprehension (d ≈ −0.3). Instrumental lo-fi hip-hop had no reliable effect, and students knew the lyrics were hurting them. — [Souza et al. 2023, J Cogn](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37152835/)
- **Writing:** background speech had a small effect on word-processed writing, a dialogue disrupted more than a one-sided conversation, and speech increased perceived workload. — [Keus van de Poll et al. 2016, Appl Cogn Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27818574/)
- **Serial verbal memory (e.g., holding a sequence of words or digits):**
  - "any kind of irrelevant speech impaired recall", including an incomprehensible language, compared with pink noise; native, intelligible speech was the most disruptive. — [Ueda et al. 2019, J Acoust Soc Am](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31255145/)
  - The disruption was large and only slightly larger in the native language. — [Ellermeier et al. 2015, J Acoust Soc Am](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26428793/)
- **Two kinds of distraction:** "interference-by-process", where the sound's processing clashes with the task, is not controllable. "Attentional capture", where the sound pulls attention away, can be reduced by greater engagement in the task. — [Hughes 2014, PsyCh J](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26271638/)

#### D. Qur'an 7:204 with Tafsir Ibn Kathir (adab context only; no ruling)

### Inferences
- **How recitation and nasheeds compare with his music. All rows are inference from music research; nothing here has been tested on recitation:**

| Driver of music-evoked imagery and mind-wandering | Phonk / rap / slowed+reverb | Murattal recitation (voice only) | Instrument-free nasheed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady beat and rhythmic entrainment (Ayyildiz 2025; Juslin 2013) | Strong: drums and 808s, repetitive pulse | Largely absent: phrase-by-phrase delivery, no drum pulse | Varies; some layer vocal "beats" or percussion-like effects |
| Emotion that steers thought content (Koelsch 2019; Taruffi 2017/2021) | Heroic or aggressive (phonk) feeds "empowering" thoughts; melancholic slowed+reverb fits the sad-music → more inward thought finding | Emotional delivery present (awe, gravity, sometimes sadness), so not emotionally neutral | Often "epic" or nostalgic delivery, closer to music |
| Themes of confidence, social status and competence (Taruffi 2023; Somer 2016) | Lyrics and genre often centre on dominance, status and money, the same themes as success fantasies | Content (if understood) points to Allah, accountability and the next life, the opposite of self-aggrandising themes | Depends on lyrics |
| Learned link to the daydream ritual (evaluative conditioning, episodic memory; Juslin 2013) | Strong: the same tracks have been paired with daydreaming many times | Weak at first, but could become a cue if used in the same headphones + pacing + idle ritual | Same risk |
| Absorption trait (Ayyildiz 2025; Somer 2016 MDS) | Interacts with audio | A trait of the person, unchanged by switching audio | Same |
| Intelligible words (irrelevant-speech effect) | His-language lyrics disrupt reading and writing | Depends on how much Arabic he understands | Lyrics in his language would disrupt |

- **Can recitation or dhikr replace the music ritual?** In part.
  - They can replace music's role as a *calming transition*: dhikr out loud or under the breath, active recitation, or attentive listening as its own activity.
  - Removing beat-driven, heroic and status-themed music probably removes the strongest *amplifiers* of vivid success fantasies.
  - But MD's other requirements remain: solitude, pacing or repetitive movement, and the "work feels uncomfortable" trigger (Somer 2016; Mansuklal 2025).
  - If he paces with headphones and plays recitation the way he played phonk, the *ritual* survives with a new soundtrack, and the recitation could become a new daydream cue.
  - Better: change the behaviour, not just the audio. In urge moments, do *active, verbal, countable* dhikr while standing still or returning to the desk, and use self-monitoring (Herscu 2023). Keep Qur'an listening as a separate, attentive act.
- **Background recitation while writing or reading:**
  - If he understands the Arabic, or knows the meaning of familiar surahs well, recitation acts like *intelligible speech*: expect small but real costs to comprehension and writing, about the size of the lyrics effect (d ≈ −0.3, Souza 2023).
  - If he does not understand it, the semantic cost for reading is probably small (Hyönä 2016; Vasilev 2019). It would still disrupt verbal serial tasks such as memorising lines or holding a sentence in mind (Ueda 2019; Ellermeier 2015).
  - A beloved, emotionally rich voice may also *capture* attention (Hughes 2014).
  - Silence is the evidence-based default for reading and drafting. Even noise has a small cost (Vasilev 2018).
  - This also avoids the adab question entirely.
- **Ask a qualified scholar:**
  1. whether playing Qur'an recitation in the background while working, scrolling or falling asleep fits the adab of 7:204, given that Ibn Kathir's narrations emphasise prayer;
  2. which nasheeds, if any, are permissible, and whether vocal "beat" effects change that;
  3. any other fiqh point here. These notes give no rulings.
- **Severity flag:** given the clinical associations of MD (Soffer-Dudek et al. 2024), the report should encourage professional support if daydreaming is severe or linked to low mood. Dhikr and the Qur'an can sit alongside that support but should not replace it.

### Gaps
- **Untested:** imagery vividness, daydream frequency or mind-wandering during Qur'an recitation, nasheeds or other unaccompanied vocal religious audio, compared with music.
- No research found on "slowed + reverb" or phonk specifically. The links to sad or heroic music are inference.
- No study of how much Arabic comprehension changes the irrelevant-speech effect of recitation.
- Herff et al. 2021 (*Sci Rep*, music and imagined journeys) was not retrieved directly. Its findings are reflected in the follow-up studies (Ayyildiz 2025; Taruffi 2023).
- Küssner et al. 2023 (*Trends Cogn Sci*, causal routes of music-induced imagery) had no abstract and was not read.

- **The four phrases:** saying "Subhan Allah, al-hamdu lillah, la ilaha illallah, Allahu akbar" is "dearer to me than anything over which the sun rises". — [Sahih Muslim 2695](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2695)
- **Tasbih 100 times a day:** "Subhan Allah wa bihamdihi" said 100 times a day brings forgiveness of sins "even if they were as much as the foam of the sea". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6405](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6405)
- **Tahlil 100 times:** "La ilaha illallah wahdahu la sharika lahu ..." 100 times brings reward like freeing ten slaves, 100 good deeds written, 100 sins removed, and "a shield for him from Satan". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6403](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6403)
- **Istighfar:** the Prophet ﷺ sought forgiveness "more than seventy times a day". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6307](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6307)
  - "There is (at times) some sort of shade upon my heart, and I seek forgiveness from Allah a hundred times a day." — [Sahih Muslim 2702](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2702)

#### C. Bedtime adhkar (his daydreaming peaks at bedtime)
- **Ayat al-Kursi (Bukhari 2311, verified):** "Whenever you go to bed, recite 'Ayat-al-Kursi' ... till you finish the whole verse. (If you do so), Allah will appoint a guard for you who will stay with you and no satan will come near you till morning." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 2311](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2311)
- **Tasbih 33/33/34 (Bukhari 3705 and 5361, verified):** to Fatimah and 'Ali, who had asked for a servant: "When you go to bed, say 'Allahu-Akbar' thirty-four times, and 'Subhan Allah' thirty-three times, and 'Al hamdu-li l-lah' thirty-three times for that is better for you both than a servant." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 3705](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3705); [5361](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5361); [6318](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6318); [Sahih Muslim 2727](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2727)
- **The sleeping du'a (Bukhari 6311, verified):** "When you want to go to bed, perform ablution as you do for prayer, then lie down on your right side and say: 'Allahumma aslamtu wajhi ilaika, wa fawwadtu amri ilaika, wa alja'tu dhahri ilaika, raghbatan wa rahbatan ilaika, la malja'a wa la manja minka illa ilaika. Amantu bikitabika alladhi anzalta wa binabiyyika alladhi arsalta.' If you should die then ... you will die on the religion of Islam; so let these words be the last you say". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6311](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6311); [Sahih Muslim 2710](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2710)
- On going to bed, "Bismika amutu wa ahya"; on waking, "Al-hamdu lillahi alladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilaihin-nushur". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6312](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6312)
- Shake out the bed, then say "Bismika Rabbi wada'tu janbi wa bika arfa'uhu, in amsakta nafsi farhamha, wa in arsaltaha fahfazha bima tahfazu bihi 'ibadaka al-salihin". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6320](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6320); [Sahih Muslim 2714](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2714)
- Every night the Prophet ﷺ cupped his hands, blew into them after reciting al-Ikhlas, al-Falaq and al-Nas, and wiped over his body starting with his head and face, three times. — [Sahih al-Bukhari 5017](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5017)
- "If somebody recited the last two Verses of Surat Al-Baqara at night, that will be sufficient for him." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 5009](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5009); [Sahih Muslim 807](https://sunnah.com/muslim:807)
- With his right hand under his cheek, three times: "O Allah, guard me from Thy punishment on the day when Thou raisest up Thy servants". **al-Albani: Sahih.** — [Sunan Abi Dawud 5045](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5045); [grading data](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/5045.json)
- "Recite 'Say, O you disbelievers!' and then go to sleep at its end, for it is a declaration of freedom from polytheism". **al-Albani: Sahih.** — [Sunan Abi Dawud 5055](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5055); [grading data](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/5055.json)
- Abu Bakr's du'a ("Allahumma 'alim al-ghaybi wash-shahadah ...") for morning and evening, "and when you go to bed". **al-Albani: Sahih.** — [Jami' al-Tirmidhi 3392](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3392); [grading data](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-tirmidhi/3392.json)

#### D. Morning and evening adhkar
- Sayyid al-Istighfar ("Allahumma anta Rabbi ..."): said "during the day with firm faith" by someone who dies before evening, or "at night" by someone who dies before morning, makes him one of "the people of Paradise". — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6306](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6306)

- "There are two blessings that many people are deceived into losing: health and free time." — [Sahih al-Bukhari 6412](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6412)
- "If anyone sits at a place where he does not remember Allah, deprivation will descend on him from Allah; and if he lies at a place where he does not remember Allah, deprivation will descend on him from Allah." **al-Albani: Hasan Sahih; Zubair 'Ali Za'i: Hasan.** — [Sunan Abi Dawud 4856](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4856); [grading data](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/4856.json)

### Inferences
- **How each text links to his pattern (practical inference, not a ruling):**

| Moment | Texts | Practical link |
|---|---|---|
| **Idle time** (queues, transitions, after uploads, scrolling urges) | Tirmidhi 3375 / Ibn Majah 3793; Bukhari 6406; Muslim 2695; Bukhari 6405, 6403; Bukhari 6412; Abu Dawud 4856; Bukhari 6407 | Make "moist tongue" the default for gaps so that idle time has a ready-made occupant. Short, portable phrases need no device. Daily counts (100 tasbih, 100 tahlil) give a target spread across idle moments. The "living and dead" and "free time" hadiths reframe idle time as something to protect, not to fill with fantasy |
| **Urge moments** (work gets uncomfortable and the pull to daydream starts) | Qur'an 7:201 + Ibn Kathir; Bukhari 3282, 3276; Qur'an 18:24; Muslim 2702; Bukhari 7405; Abu Dawud 5088 (narrator forgot when angry) | A three-step script: **notice → short dhikr or isti'adha (or istighfar) → return to the task**. 7:201 models remembering at the moment an impulse "touches" and then "seeing aright". 18:24 frames *catching a drift* as the cue to remember, not as failure. Muslim 2702 shows that heart-lapses are normal and answered with repeated istighfar. Silent remembrance "in himself" (7405) is possible at a desk |
| **Bedtime** (peak MD window) | Wudu and right side (6311); dusting the bed (6320); Ayat al-Kursi (2311); last two verses of al-Baqarah (5009); three Quls with wiping (5017); 33/33/34 (3705/5361); Abu Dawud 5045 (3×); al-Kafirun (Abu Dawud 5055); Bismika amutu wa ahya (6312); al-Bara' du'a **as the last words** (6311); Abu Dawud 4856 ("lies at a place ..."); Qur'an 3:191 | Turn the pre-sleep window into a fixed, sequenced routine of verbal, countable items. Counting 33/33/34 occupies working memory, a labelled analogy to the "imagery distraction" and articulatory-suppression findings below. "Let these words be the last you say" gives a clear stop point, a natural end to the day's talking and imagining. If he wakes at night, use Bukhari 1154 instead of resuming a daydream |
| **Morning and evening** | Qur'an 7:205 + Ibn Kathir, 33:41–42; Bukhari 6306; Muslim 2692, 2723, 2709; Abu Dawud 5088, 5068, 5082, 5074, 5077; Tirmidhi 3392 | Fixed anchors at the two transitions; the evening set can lead into the bedtime routine. Abu Dawud 5088's narrator forgot when angry, which argues for tying adhkar to fixed cues (after Fajr and after 'Asr or Maghrib) rather than to mood |

- **Labelled analogy for bedtime (not dhikr research):**
  - The normal transition into sleep involves sensory imagery. Insomnia-related pre-sleep thought is mostly planning and worry. Tested techniques (imagery, articulatory suppression, distraction) were beneficial, negligible or harmful. — [Lemyre et al. 2020, Sleep Med Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31918338/)
  - An engaging imagery task shortened sleep onset in 41 people with insomnia, credited to occupying "cognitive space". — [Harvey & Payne 2002, Behav Res Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11863237/)
  - Silently repeating a word ("articulatory suppression", from Baddeley's working-memory model) was proposed for intrusive thoughts in insomnia. The evidence is a preliminary, uncontrolled case series and a single-case experiment. — [Levey et al. 1991, Behav Res Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2012592/)
  - By analogy, quiet, repetitive, counted adhkar at bedtime may occupy the same verbal resources that narrative daydreams use. **Untested for dhikr.** The goal is not to stop all imagery, since some at sleep onset is normal (Lemyre 2020), but to avoid *deliberately building* emotional fantasy scenes.
- **During work blocks:** dhikr on the tongue *while* drafting a script would compete with the inner speech that writing needs, by the same articulatory-suppression logic. Dhikr is therefore best used at *transitions* and urge points, not as a constant accompaniment to verbal work. This is an inference.

# Islamic view of wishing, idle time, "if only" and small consistent deeds, and the psychology of religious coping, scrupulosity and MD in Muslims

## 1. Wishful thinking (amani / tamanni), "if only", and hope tied to action

### Takeaway
The Qur'an calls amani (wishful hopes) a delusion that Shaytan stirs up. Ibn Kathir glosses this as making repentance seem skippable, promising wishes, and ordering "taswif" (procrastination) and delay (4:119). The amani deluded the hypocrites while they "postponed repentance from time to time" (57:14), and they "distract" (15:3). The authentic Prophetic alternative (Muslim 2664) is a concrete action sequence: strive for what benefits you, seek Allah's help, do not be helpless, and do not dwell on "if only" about what has already happened. This lines up closely with Oettingen's finding that fantasies sap effort while expectations drive it. It also lines up with research separating useful counterfactuals, which produce a plan, from ruminative regret. Wishing is not condemned when it is tied to doing (Bukhari 7232; Ibn Kathir on 4:32).

**The core hadith on hope with action**
- [Sahih Muslim 2664; in-book ref. Book 46, Hadith 52] «الْمُؤْمِنُ الْقَوِيُّ خَيْرٌ وَأَحَبُّ إِلَى اللَّهِ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِ الضَّعِيفِ وَفِي كُلٍّ خَيْرٌ احْرِصْ عَلَى مَا يَنْفَعُكَ وَاسْتَعِنْ بِاللَّهِ وَلاَ تَعْجِزْ وَإِنْ أَصَابَكَ شَىْءٌ فَلاَ تَقُلْ لَوْ أَنِّي فَعَلْتُ كَانَ كَذَا وَكَذَا. وَلَكِنْ قُلْ قَدَرُ اللَّهِ وَمَا شَاءَ فَعَلَ فَإِنَّ لَوْ تَفْتَحُ عَمَلَ الشَّيْطَانِ». In English: "The strong believer is better and more beloved to Allah than the weak believer, and in both there is good. Be keen on what benefits you, seek Allah's help, and do not be helpless. If something befalls you, do not say 'if only I had done such-and-such, it would have been so-and-so,' but say 'Allah decreed, and what He willed He did,' for 'if' opens [the door to] the work of Shaytan." The number is confirmed. — [Muslim 2664](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2664); [text and al-Nawawi commentary on hadithunlocked](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2664)
- [al-Nawawi, *al-Minhaj*, on Muslim 2664] al-Nawawi first relays al-Qadi 'Iyad:
  - Some scholars held that the prohibition applies to someone who says "if only" believing it with certainty, i.e. that had he acted, the harm would definitely not have reached him.
  - 'Iyad's own view is that the prohibition is general but is one of *tanzih* (discouragement, not strict prohibition), because "'if' opens the work of Shaytan" means "it casts into the heart opposition to the decree, and Shaytan whispers it."
  - al-Nawawi then adds that the Prophet himself used "if" about the past («لو استقبلت من أمري ما استدبرت…»), «فالظاهر أن النهي إنما هو عن إطلاق ذلك فيما لا فائدة فيه»: "so the apparent meaning is that the prohibition concerns using it where there is no benefit."
  - — [hadithunlocked, Muslim 2664 (al-Minhaj)](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2664)
- [Ibn Hajar, *Fath al-Bari*, on Bukhari's chapter «باب ما يجوز من اللو» ("what is permissible of saying 'if'"), *Kitab al-Tamanni*] The commentary carries the conclusion that "if" said in regret over a missed act of obedience, or over something that was not possible for one, «فلا بأس به، وعليه يحمل أكثر الاستعمال الموجود في الأحاديث» ("is no harm, and most of the usages found in the hadiths are understood this way"). — [hadithunlocked, Bukhari 7238 (Fath al-Bari)](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:7238)
- [Sahih al-Bukhari 6363] The Prophet often sought refuge «مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ…» ("from worry and grief, from incapacity and laziness…"). [Sahih Muslim 2706] has refuge «مِنَ الْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ وَالْجُبْنِ وَالْهَرَمِ وَالْبُخْلِ…» ("from incapacity, laziness, cowardice, senility, miserliness…"). The 'ajz ("helplessness") named in this du'a is the same word as «وَلاَ تَعْجِزْ» in Muslim 2664. — [Bukhari 6363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6363); [Muslim 2706](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2706)
- [Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2517: graded **Hasan** by al-Albani; also Hasan per Ahmad Shakir and Zubair 'Ali Za'i] «اعْقِلْهَا وَتَوَكَّلْ» ("Tie it and rely [upon Allah]"), in answer to "Shall I tie it and rely, or leave it loose and rely?" — [Tirmidhi 2517](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2517); [grades in dataset](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-tirmidhi/2517.json)

- [Qur'an] 3:159: "And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him]." — [Quran.com 3:159](https://quran.com/3:159)
- [Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2325: **Sahih** per al-Albani (Bashar 'Awwad: Hasan Sahih; Zubair 'Ali Za'i: Da'if)] "The world is only for four persons…":

**Psychology to map onto (the user already knows Oettingen)**
- [Experiments/longitudinal] Positive fantasies about the future predict lower effort and poorer attainment, while positive expectations predict more. Positive fantasies also lower energy (systolic blood pressure) in experiments. — [Oettingen & Mayer 2002, JPSP (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1198); [Kappes & Oettingen 2011, JESP (LSE repository)](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284) (links reused from the sibling notes on MD)
- [Meta-analysis] Upward counterfactual thinking ("if only…") correlates with depressive symptoms at r = .26 (42 effect sizes, pooled N = 13,168). Upward counterfactuals and regret produce effects of similar size, and cross-sectional and longitudinal designs give similar results. — [Broomhall et al. 2017, Clin Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28501706/)
- [Theory review] According to the functional theory of counterfactual thinking, "what might have been" thoughts are best explained by their role in regulating behavior and improving performance. Their useful route runs through specific behavioral intentions that then change behavior. — [Epstude & Roese 2008, PSPR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18453477/)
- [Review] Repetitive thought can be constructive (planning, recovery) or unconstructive (depression, anxiety). One key moderator is the level of construal: concrete processing is more helpful than abstract "why/what if" processing. — [Watkins 2008, Psychol Bull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18298268/)

### Inferences
- **4:123 + Ibn Kathir ↔ fantasy versus expectation.** The Qur'anic contrast is between claim or wish and deed. This is the same contrast as positive fantasy versus effort-linked expectation in Oettingen's data. The video can present Ibn Kathir's line "salvation is not by mere wishing; what counts is obedience" as the textual counterpart of "fantasies don't produce effort; plans and expectations do."
- **4:119 + Ibn Kathir ↔ fantasy as an energy drain.** Ibn Kathir names *taswif* (procrastination), delay and self-delusion as the content of Shaytan's amani. A success fantasy that "feels like progress" while work is postponed fits that description almost word for word. This is an analogy, not a claim that every daydream is from Shaytan.
- **Muslim 2664 maps onto WOOP as follows:**
  - "Be keen on what benefits you" is the Wish and Outcome, kept concrete and useful.
  - "Do not be helpless" names the inner Obstacle: the urge to escape when the task gets uncomfortable.
  - "Seek Allah's help" plus action is the Plan, the collaborative stance.
  - "Do not say 'if only'" blocks past-focused regret loops.
  - al-Nawawi's reading lines up with Epstude & Roese's functional theory of counterfactuals: "if" is objectionable where there is no benefit, and fine when it expresses regret over a missed act of obedience. Counterfactuals help when they produce a next-time intention and hurt when they become regret-rumination (Broomhall r = .26).
- **"What if" rumination.** Combining the Qur'anic warnings about amani with Muslim 2664's ban on "if only" covers both directions of mental time-travel: future fantasy (amani) and past regret (law). The Prophetic replacement in both directions is present, concrete action.
- **Success fantasies and comparison.** Qur'an 4:32 (with Ibn Kathir's reconciliation) and Bukhari 7232 give a usable rule: wishing for someone else's success itself is discouraged, while wanting the like of a good gift is fine if you would *do what they do*. For a creator, the line is between "I wish I had his channel," a fantasy of the outcome, and "if I had his skills I'd post weekly; so I will practise X," a wish turned into a process.
- **"Tie the camel" (Tirmidhi 2517) and 3:159.** Effort first, then reliance. This sets up the coping-styles evidence in section 5: collaborative coping versus deferring.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that tests Muslim teachings on amani or tamanni against fantasy-realization measures. All the mappings above are conceptual.
- Ibn Kathir's commentary on 3:159 and 13:11 was not retrieved. 13:11 ("Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves") is often quoted for self-change, but its tafsir context was not checked, so it is left out.
- Ibn Hajar's full discussion in the "*ma yajuzu min al-law*" chapter was only partly read. He also quotes al-Qurtubi's *al-Mufhim*, but al-Qurtubi's text is not among the user's permitted commentators and was not verified inside Fath al-Bari, so it is not used.
- The grading of Tirmidhi 2517 is contested: al-Albani graded it hasan, while Yahya al-Qattan, as reported, called the Anas chain munkar. The writer should present it as "graded hasan by al-Albani" and avoid calling it sahih.

## 2. Time and idle time: Bukhari 6412, "five before five", Qur'an 94:7–8 and 103, and Salaf on idleness

### Takeaway
Authentic texts treat health and free time as capital that "many people" lose:
- Bukhari 6412, with Ibn Hajar's commentary.
- "Five before five" (al-Mustadrak 7846), graded sahih by al-Hakim, al-Dhahabi and al-Albani.
- Ibn 'Umar's "do not talk to yourself about the evening… take from your health for your sickness" (Bukhari 6416 / Tirmidhi 2333).
- Being questioned about one's life "in what he spent it" (Tirmidhi 2417, sahih per al-Albani).

Qur'an 94:7–8 even has an if-then shape: "when you have finished, then exert yourself." The requested Ibn Mas'ud saying on idleness could not be verified and is excluded. Ibn 'Umar's saying in Sahih al-Bukhari is a stronger replacement.

### Cited Findings
- [Sahih al-Bukhari 6412, first hadith of Kitab al-Riqaq] «نِعْمَتَانِ مَغْبُونٌ فِيهِمَا كَثِيرٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ، الصِّحَّةُ وَالْفَرَاغُ» ("Two blessings in which many people are cheated: health and free time"). Number confirmed. — [Bukhari 6412](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6412)
- [Ibn Hajar, *Fath al-Bari*, on 6412]
  - The commentary states: «فمن فرط في ذلك فهو المغبون، وأشار بقوله كثير من الناس إلى أن الذي يوفق لذلك قليل». In English: whoever neglects using them in obedience "is the one cheated," and the words "many people" indicate that "those granted success in this are few."
  - Ibn Hajar relays Ibn al-Jawzi: a person may be healthy but not free (busy earning a living), or free but not healthy, «فإذا اجتمعا فغلب عليه الكسل عن الطاعة فهو المغبون». So when both come together and laziness overcomes him, he is the one cheated. "Whoever uses his free time and health in obedience to Allah is the envied one (maghbut)… because free time is followed by busyness and health by illness."
  - Ibn Hajar relays al-Tibi: health and free time are the merchant's capital (ra's al-mal).
  - — [hadithunlocked, Bukhari 6412 (Fath al-Bari)](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:6412)
- ["Take advantage of five before five": *al-Mustadrak* of al-Hakim no. 7846, from Ibn 'Abbas] «اغتنم خمسا قبل خمس: شبابك قبل هرمك، وصحتك قبل سقمك، وغناك قبل فقرك، وفراغك قبل شغلك، وحياتك قبل موتك». That is: youth before old age, health before sickness, wealth before poverty, free time before busyness, life before death.

### Inferences
- **Idle time is the danger zone in both frameworks.** Bukhari 6412 names free time as the place where people are "cheated." The user's MD peaks in idle time. Ibn Hajar's line that "few are granted success in this" normalises the struggle: it is the common human failure, not a personal defect.
- **94:7 ("fa-idha faraghta fa-nsab") is literally an implementation intention: "When I finish X, then I do Y."** For idle-time MD, the problem moment is the gap after finishing or pausing a task. A plan in the 94:7 shape, such as "When I close the editing timeline, then I stand and do [two rak'ahs / tidy the desk / walk]," fills that gap with a cue-linked action instead of an open slot for fantasy. Ibn Kathir's "with an unoccupied mind," and the hadith he cites about removing competing urges (food, call of nature) before prayer, both support clearing distractions before a focus block.
- **Ibn 'Umar's "do not talk to yourself about the evening" (Tirmidhi 2333 wording) targets future-oriented inner monologue, a close cousin of success fantasies.** Ibn 'Umar's own fix is behavioral: "take from your health for your sickness." In other words, act today.
- **Bedtime.** The Sunnah gives a fixed pre-sleep script: wudu, lying on the right side, a set du'a, and "make it your last words." It can work as a replacement routine for bedtime fantasy, a habit cue in the place where daydreams start. Caveat for someone with OCD traits: the script should be done once as worship, not repeated until it feels "right" (see section 7).
- **Music.** Whatever the ruling (that is a fiqh question for a qualified scholar), the psychological point is that removing a habitual amplifier leaves the triggering cue in place: discomfort at work, idle time, bedtime. A replacement routine is still needed.

### Gaps
- No authentic, sourced saying from Ibn Mas'ud on idleness was confirmed. Ibn 'Umar's saying in Bukhari 6416 is the recommended substitute.
- al-Shafi'i (d. 204 AH) comes after the three generations as the user defines them. His line on al-'Asr is usable only as part of Ibn Kathir's commentary, or should be dropped.
- Ibn al-Jawzi and al-Tibi are quoted *inside* Ibn Hajar's commentary. The writer may present their words as "Ibn Hajar relays…" or keep only Ibn Hajar's own sentence.
- al-Albani's grading of "five before five" was confirmed only through a Dorar search result, not by opening the page. al-Hakim's and al-Dhahabi's gradings were confirmed directly.
- al-Nawawi's "hasan" grading of Tirmidhi 2317 in his 40 Hadith (no. 12) was not checked in this session.

## 3. Consistency: "the most beloved deeds are the most consistent, even if small", mapped onto habit and if-then research

### Takeaway
The "most consistent even if small" hadith is confirmed in both Sahihs: Bukhari 6464 and 6465 (also 43), and Muslim 782 and 783. Several texts reinforce the same message:
- The Prophet forbade overburdening oneself.
- His household made deeds constant.
- He made up missed night prayer with twelve rak'ahs by day (Muslim 746).
- He warned against starting and then dropping a practice (Bukhari 1152).
- Ibn Hajar relays the observation that everyone who goes to extremes in religion eventually breaks off (Bukhari 39).

al-Nawawi explains why a little done continuously beats a lot that stops: it keeps obedience and remembrance ongoing and "grows until it exceeds the interrupted much many times over." This matches habit research: repeat in a stable context, and a single miss does not necessarily derail the habit, although replication evidence is mixed.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Same rule, two languages.** "A little that continues beats a lot that stops" (al-Nawawi) is the Islamic phrasing of habit research's emphasis on frequent, small, context-stable repetition. Ibn Hajar's "do what you can keep up; do not burden yourself with what you cannot" translates directly into "set a minimum you can hit on your worst day."
- **The Prophetic "make-up" (Muslim 746) works like a relapse plan.** When the routine is missed for a legitimate reason, a defined make-up is done. That keeps the identity of "a person who does this" without all-or-nothing collapse. This fits the Lally finding that one miss did not materially derail habit formation, while acknowledging the mixed replication.
- **Anti-perfectionism.** Bukhari 39 with Ibn Hajar and Ibn al-Munayyir ("every extremist breaks off") warns against the start-hard-then-quit cycle that perfectionists show. This matters because MD samples in Iran showed higher socially-prescribed perfectionism (section 8).
- **If-then pairing from the texts themselves.** The Sunnah links practices to fixed anchors: morning, evening and part of the night (Bukhari 39), after obligatory prayers (Ibn Mas'ud's reading of 94:7), and bedtime (Bukhari 247). These anchors make good cues for implementation intentions. An example: "After 'Asr, then 25 minutes of scripting; after 'Isha, then the bedtime routine."

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures Muslims' consistency in worship as habit formation (for example, the automaticity of prayer) or links it to MD.
- The exact effect size of Gollwitzer & Sheeran was not re-verified here; use the sibling notes.
- The Lally replication's results need checking in its full published form before the "a missed day doesn't matter" message is stated strongly.

## 4. Intrusive thoughts and waswas: "forgiven until acted on or spoken", the prophetic responses, and thought-suppression research

### Takeaway
Three things are verified in Bukhari and Muslim:
- Allah has pardoned this ummah for what their souls whisper or tell them, as long as they do not act on it or speak it (Bukhari 5269, 2528, 6664; Muslim 127).
- Companions' horror at thoughts "too grave to speak" was called "clear faith" (Muslim 132–133). al-Nawawi explains that the very distress is the sign of complete faith.
- The prescribed response is brief: seek refuge in Allah, say "I believe in Allah," and *desist* (Bukhari 3276; Muslim 134).

Ibn Hajar explains "desist" as stopping going along with the thought and "striving to repel it by occupying oneself with something else." He relays al-Khattabi that debating the whisper never ends. al-Nawawi, relaying al-Mazari, says the thoughts are to be repelled by turning away from them "without reasoning or examining their refutation." This closely mirrors suppression research, where trying not to think backfires but a specific replacement reduces later preoccupation. It also mirrors ERP's non-engagement stance, with one caution: refuge-seeking repeated for certainty or relief can itself become a compulsion.

### Cited Findings

[Muslim 133] (Ibn Mas'ud), asked about waswasa: «تِلْكَ مَحْضُ الإِيمَانِ» ("that is pure faith"). — [Muslim 132](https://sunnah.com/muslim:132); [Muslim 133](https://sunnah.com/muslim:133)
  - [al-Nawawi] «معناه استعظامكم الكلام به هو صريح الإيمان، فإن استعظام هذا وشدة الخوف منه ومن النطق به فضلا عن اعتقاده إنما يكون لمن استكمل الإيمان استكمالا محققا وانتفت عنه الريبة والشكوك». In English: "its meaning is that your regarding it as too grave to speak of is clear faith, for regarding it as grave and intensely fearing it, let alone believing it, only happens in someone whose faith is truly complete." He also gives the view chosen by al-Qadi 'Iyad: Shaytan resorts to whispering only to one he has despaired of misleading, so the whispering is a sign of pure faith. — [hadithunlocked, Muslim 132 (al-Minhaj)](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:132a)
- [Sahih Muslim 134] «…فَمَنْ وَجَدَ مِنْ ذَلِكَ شَيْئًا فَلْيَقُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللَّهِ» ("whoever finds any of that, let him say: I believe in Allah"); one wording adds "and His messengers." — [Muslim 134](https://sunnah.com/muslim:134)
- [Sahih al-Bukhari 3276] «يَأْتِي الشَّيْطَانُ أَحَدَكُمْ فَيَقُولُ مَنْ خَلَقَ كَذَا… حَتَّى يَقُولَ مَنْ خَلَقَ رَبَّكَ فَإِذَا بَلَغَهُ فَلْيَسْتَعِذْ بِاللَّهِ، وَلْيَنْتَهِ» ("…when it reaches that, let him seek refuge in Allah and desist"). — [Bukhari 3276](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3276)
  - [Ibn Hajar] «(فليستعذ بالله ولينته) أي عن الاسترسال معه في ذلك، بل يلجأ إلى الله في دفعه، ويعلم أنه يريد إفساد دينه وعقله بهذه الوسوسة، فينبغي أن يجتهد في دفعها بالاشتغال بغيرها». In English: "'and desist' means from going along with it; rather he turns to Allah to repel it and knows that [Shaytan] wants to corrupt his religion and mind with this whispering, so he should strive to repel it **by occupying himself with something else**."
  - He relays al-Khattabi: if the person seeks refuge and *stops prolonging engagement* with it, it is repelled. That is unlike a human questioner, who can be answered with proof, because «الشيطان فليس لوسوسته انتهاء، بل كلما ألزم حجة زاغ إلى غيرها إلى أن يفضي بالمرء إلى الحيرة» ("Shaytan's whispering has no end; whenever he is pinned with a proof he swerves to another, until he leads the person into bewilderment"). — [hadithunlocked, Bukhari 3276 (Fath al-Bari)](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:3276)
  - [al-Nawawi on Muslim 134] «فمعناه الإعراض عن هذا الخاطر الباطل والالتجاء إلى الله تعالى في إذهابه» ("it means turning away from this false thought and resorting to Allah to remove it"). He relays al-Mazari: «ظاهر الحديث أنه ﷺ أمرهم أن يدفعوا الخواطر بالإعراض عنها، والرد لها من غير استدلال ولا نظر في إبطالها» ("the apparent meaning is that he ordered them to repel the thoughts by turning away from them … without reasoning or examining their refutation"). — [hadithunlocked, Muslim 132–134 (al-Minhaj)](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:134a)
- [Sunan Abi Dawud 5112: **Sahih** per al-Albani] A man said he had thoughts he would rather be burnt to charcoal than speak. The Prophet said «اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي رَدَّ كَيْدَهُ إِلَى الْوَسْوَسَةِ» ("…praise be to Allah who reduced his [Shaytan's] scheme to mere whispering"). — [Abu Dawud 5112](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5112); [grades in dataset](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/5112.json)
- [Salaf, Companion, Abu Dawud 5110: isnad **Hasan** per al-Albani] Abu Zumayl told Ibn 'Abbas he found something in his breast he could not speak of. Ibn 'Abbas laughed, said no one escapes that, and told him: "If you find something in your heart, say: 'He is the First and the Last, the Evident and the Immanent, and He has full knowledge of all things' (57:3)." — [Abu Dawud 5110](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5110); [grades in dataset](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/5110.json)
- [Sahih Muslim 2203, waswas in prayer] 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As complained that Shaytan came between him and his prayer and recitation, confusing him. The Prophet said: «ذَاكَ شَيْطَانٌ يُقَالُ لَهُ خِنْزِبٌ فَإِذَا أَحْسَسْتَهُ فَتَعَوَّذْ بِاللَّهِ مِنْهُ وَاتْفِلْ عَلَى يَسَارِكَ ثَلاَثًا» ("That is a devil called Khinzab; when you sense him, seek refuge in Allah from him and spit [dryly] to your left three times"). 'Uthman said: "I did that and Allah removed it from me." — [Muslim 2203](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2203)
  - [al-Nawawi] He states that the hadith shows it is recommended to seek refuge from Shaytan at his whispering, with the dry spitting to the left three times. He glosses "came between me and it" as «نكدني فيها، ومنعني لذتها والفراغ للخشوع فيها» ("spoiled it for me and deprived me of its sweetness and of a free mind for khushu'"). — [hadithunlocked, Muslim 2203 (al-Minhaj)](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2203a)
- [Experiment] In the original "white bear" study, people told not to think of a white bear could not suppress it, and afterwards thought about it *more* than controls. Participants given a **specific distracter thought** during suppression were less likely to show later preoccupation. — [Wegner et al. 1987, JPSP](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3612492/)
- [Meta-analysis] Across controlled studies, thought suppression has a small-to-moderate rebound effect. Its size varies with the target thought and the measurement method; clinical participants did not show larger rebound, though few clinical studies existed. — [Abramowitz, Tolin & Street 2001, Clin Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11434226/)
- [Cross-sectional, Turkish Muslims vs Canadian Christians] In both groups the religiosity–obsessionality link was mediated by beliefs about the **importance and control of thoughts**, responsibility/threat beliefs, and generalized guilt. — [Inozu, Karanci & Clark 2012, J Behav Ther Exp Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22484698/)
- [Cross-sectional] Turkish Muslim participants scored higher on OCD symptoms, reported more concern about their thoughts and about controlling them, and used worry to manage unwanted thoughts. — [Yorulmaz, Gençöz & Woody 2009, J Anxiety Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19108983/)

### Inferences
- **The texts directly counter the core OCD belief that thoughts matter as much as acts** (importance of thoughts / thought–action fusion), which Inozu found mediates religiosity's link to obsessionality. The hadith says unacted, unspoken thoughts are not counted; Muslim 130 adds that an intended bad deed left undone is not recorded. A video can use this as a faith-based reappraisal, a cognitive-restructuring step grounded in Bukhari and Muslim.
- **"Clear faith" (Muslim 132, al-Nawawi) is the Islamic equivalent of saying the thought is ego-dystonic.** The horror shows the thought is unwanted and foreign to one's values. This is normalisation, a standard first step in CBT for intrusions.
- **Notice → name → return:**
  - Notice: "whoever *finds* any of that" (Muslim 134).
  - Name: "that is Khinzab" (Muslim 2203); "praise be to Allah who reduced his plot to *waswasa*" (Abu Dawud 5112).
  - Return: "*desist*… by occupying oneself with something else" (Ibn Hajar on Bukhari 3276), and "turn away without reasoning about it" (al-Mazari via al-Nawawi).
  - This sequence matches the evidence. Fighting a thought or arguing it out ("it has no end," al-Khattabi) feeds it. Brief acknowledgement plus *specific* redirection reduces later preoccupation (Wegner 1987, experiment 2).
  - For MD the "something else" should be the work task itself, not another fantasy.
- **Critical caution for someone with possible OCD traits.** The prophetic responses are short, one-off acts. If isti'adha, "amantu billah" or the dry spitting is repeated over and over until the thought "goes" or certainty arrives, it starts to work as a *neutralizing compulsion*, which ERP specifically aims to drop. The texts themselves point toward non-engagement ("desist," "turn away," "without reasoning"), which is consistent with ERP's response prevention. How to apply this in prayer or wudu is a question for a qualified scholar working with a clinician.

### Gaps
- al-Nawawi's discussion of settled resolve ('azm) versus passing thoughts (khawatir) under Muslim 127–130 was not located in the retrieved text. The question of when a dwelt-upon fantasy with haram content becomes sinful is a fiqh question: **ask a qualified scholar**.
- A widely circulated fatwa telling people to ignore waswas completely is attributed to **Ibn Hajar al-Haytami**, a different scholar from Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani. It is excluded under the commentary rule and was not verified here. Writers should not misattribute it to the author of Fath al-Bari.
- On the same hadithunlocked pages, some commentaries were deliberately not used: al-Dihlawi's *Lama'at al-Tanqih*, which contains Sufi-style material; Mulla 'Ali al-Qari's *Mirqat*; and Ibn 'Uthaymin's explanation. None of these is a permitted commentator.

## 5. Religious coping (Pargament): meta-analyses, Muslim samples, and religiosity with depression/anxiety in Muslims

### Takeaway
Across 49 studies, positive religious coping (e.g. benevolent reappraisal, seeking spiritual support) is moderately linked to positive adjustment (r = .33) and weakly to less distress (r = −.12). Negative religious coping (e.g. feeling punished or abandoned by God) is linked to more distress (r = .22) and is unrelated to positive adjustment. Later meta-analyses repeat the asymmetry: the negative side is the more reliable predictor.

In Muslim samples the picture is similar but noisier. Negative coping and religious struggles track distress. Positive coping tracks life satisfaction more than it tracks depression. Higher religiousness relates to happiness partly through gratitude and lower malicious envy.

### Cited Findings
- [Meta-analysis] Ano & Vasconcelles 2005 pooled 49 studies and 105 effect sizes (Table 5):
  - Positive religious coping with positive adjustment: r = .33 (29 studies; 95% CI .30–.35).
  - Positive religious coping with negative adjustment: r = −.12 (38; −.14 to −.10).
  - Negative religious coping with positive adjustment: r = .02, not significant (16; −.02 to .05).
  - Negative religious coping with negative adjustment: r = .22 (22; .19–.24).
  - — [Ano & Vasconcelles 2005, J Clin Psychol (full text PDF)](https://www.uned.es/universidad/inicio/dam/jcr:fd37f522-5523-47c7-9f9f-821180678483/ANOYVA~1.PDF); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15503316/)
- [Meta-analysis] Smith, McCullough & Poll 2003 covered 147 studies (N = 98,975). Religiousness and depressive symptoms correlate at r = −.096, and the link is stronger under recent stressful life events. Extrinsic orientation and **negative religious coping (e.g. "avoiding difficulties through religious activities," blaming God)** were associated with *more* depressive symptoms. — [Smith et al. 2003, Psychol Bull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12848223/)
- [Meta-analysis, first COVID wave] 42 studies, 25,438 participants, 24 countries. Only negative religious coping correlated with psychological symptoms (r = .29). General and positive coping did not (r = .04 and −.02, ns), although positive coping was linked to *more* symptoms in some high-stress subgroups. — [Cheng & Ying 2023, Front Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36741121/)
- [Systematic review/meta-analysis, ages 10–24] In high-quality longitudinal studies, negative religious coping trended toward more later depression (r = 0.09; 95% CI −0.009 to 0.188) and spiritual well-being was protective (r = −0.153). Personal importance of religion was not associated (per the abstract, which is truncated in the retrieved text). — [Aggarwal et al. 2023, BMC Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37817143/)
- [Scale development: Psychological Measure of Islamic Religiousness (PMIR)] The PMIR is a 60-item instrument built in three stages: interviews with 25 Muslims, a pilot with 64 Muslims (US and Israel), and validation in an international online sample of 340 Muslims. It has seven factors, including Islamic Beliefs, Islamic Religious Struggle and Islamic Religious Conversion, and the authors report strong discriminant, convergent, predictive and incremental validity. Reference: Int J Psychol Relig 18(4):291–315. Details are from search summaries of the record and the author-hosted PDF. — [Tel Aviv Univ. record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/a-psychological-measure-of-islamic-religiousness-development-and-/); [author-hosted PDF](https://www.kennethpargament.com/_files/ugd/fb472f_65c353c63a884e4cbab44fa62e827ff7.pdf)
- [Cross-sectional, 706 students in Israel/Palestine, Turkey and Malaysia] Positive religious coping use was high. It correlated with satisfaction with life but **not** with depressive symptoms, and the strength of the life-satisfaction link differed by country. — [Abu-Raiya et al. 2019, Int J Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30338852/)
- [Cross-sectional, same 706] Religious struggles were generally low. They predicted generalized anxiety only among Malaysians, and lower life satisfaction only among Palestinians and Turks. — [Abu-Raiya et al. 2018, Social Work](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30085296/)
- [Cross-sectional, 812 Muslim adults in Türkiye and Jordan] Religiousness was positively associated with happiness, partly via greater gratitude and lower *malicious* envy. Benign envy was unrelated. — [Abu-Raiya, Ayten & Ferhan 2026, BMC Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42226276/)
- [Cross-sectional, 228 mostly Malay Muslim psychiatric patients] Negative religious coping was significantly associated with high distress. Higher religious commitment was associated with lower distress. — [Nurasikin et al. 2013, Int J Soc Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22408116/)
- [Cross-sectional, 622 Malaysian medical students] Negative, not positive, religious coping was significantly associated with depressive and anxiety symptoms. — [Francis et al. 2019, IJERPH](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30658450/)
- [Cross-sectional, 247 young Muslims in Lahore] Self-regulation mediated the links between positive religious coping and lower stress and anxiety, and between negative coping and higher stress. The effects were stronger in men. — [Fatima et al. 2022, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34480690/)
- [Cross-sectional SEM, 340 Israeli Muslims and Jews] Anxious and avoidant attachment to God predicted divine struggles, which mediated their link to depressive symptoms. Positive religious coping was unrelated to the other variables. — [Shoshan, Chaki-Binon & Abu-Raiya 2024, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38753132/)
- [National US sample, N = 2,140] Religious commitment, life sanctification, religious support and religious hope buffered the link between religious/spiritual struggle and lower happiness. Commitment and sanctification also buffered depression. — [Abu-Raiya, Pargament & Krause 2016, Qual Life Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26476837/)
- [Narrative review, Muslims in English-speaking countries] Muslims' mental health is under-researched. Across the wider literature, religiosity is moderately associated with greater well-being and lower depression, anxiety and suicide, but most studies are cross-sectional, so reverse causation is possible. — [Ibrahim & Whitley 2021 (PMC; via search summary)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9341235/)
- [Problem-solving styles; original sample of 197 church members (Christian)] The **deferring** style (passively leaving problems to God) was associated with lower personal control, lower self-esteem, less active planful problem-solving and a greater sense of control by chance. The **collaborative** style (working with God) had more favorable correlates. These findings come from secondary summaries; the original article was not accessed. — [core.ac.uk summary PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/16149322.pdf); [JSTOR catalogue record](https://katalog.dhi-paris.fr/vufind/Record/JST052962970/Description)
  - [Cross-sectional, 91 US students] In this sample both collaborative *and* deferring styles were positively associated with life satisfaction through hope "agency," while self-directing was negatively associated. So the deferring finding is not uniform. — [Parenteau & Wu 2021, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33449263/)
- [Cross-sectional, US online and student samples] Attributing emotional distress to religious or supernatural causes (e.g. punishment or sin) was associated with lower psychological flexibility in the online sample, which partly accounted for higher distress. This did not hold in the student sample. — [Anderson & Chittenden 2026, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41880143/)

### Inferences
- **Honest grading.** The best evidence is cross-sectional and correlational, and the effects are small to moderate. "Religion protects mental health" is too strong a claim. A fair summary is that *how* one copes religiously matters more than *how religious* one is. Negative patterns ("God is punishing me," feeling abandoned, struggles) are the clearest risk signal.
- **"Tie the camel" ≈ collaborative coping.** The Prophetic model (Muslim 2664: strive, seek help, don't be helpless; Tirmidhi 2517; Qur'an 3:159) describes the collaborative style. Using "tawakkul" as a reason not to act is closer to the deferring style, which has poorer correlates in at least the original data.
- **Success fantasies and envy.** In Turkish and Jordanian Muslims, the religiousness–happiness link runs partly through less malicious envy and more gratitude. That fits Qur'an 4:32 ("do not wish for what Allah has favored others with… ask Allah of His bounty"). It is a useful, data-backed angle for a creator who compares himself with other channels.
- **Measurement caution for the writer.** Negative religious coping measures include appraisals of punishment or abandonment. A Muslim with MD who reads his daydreaming as proof that "Allah is displeased with me" would score in the risky range. Islamic texts in section 4 (thoughts forgiven; distress is "clear faith") offer a reappraisal toward the positive-coping side.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis limited to Muslim samples on religiosity and depression/anxiety was retrieved. A Ghannam & Gorey meta-analysis on Islamic religiosity and well-being in the West was only seen as a slide deck, so its findings are unknown.
- PMIR subscale-level outcome findings (e.g. Islamic Religious Struggle with depression) were not retrieved from the full text.
- Brief RCOPE item content was not re-verified here. Whether Muslim framings of "Shaytan's whispering" load onto Western "demonic reappraisal" items is an open measurement question.
- No Muslim-sample data on the deferring versus collaborative problem-solving styles, or on tawakkul as active versus passive coping, was found in this session.

## 6. Interventions: religiously integrated CBT or psychotherapy for Muslims (Qur'an and Sunnah content), effect sizes versus standard care, and framework checks

### Takeaway
Faith-adapted therapies, which are mostly CBT or behavioural activation (BA), beat no-treatment or waitlist controls. They are at least as good as secular therapy for symptoms. In the most rigorous "additive" comparisons the extra symptom benefit is small and not significant (g = 0.13), but spiritual well-being improves (g = 0.34). The Muslim-specific programmes whose content draws on Qur'an, prophets' stories and du'a are promising:
- BA-M: −2.72 PHQ-9 points versus CBT or social intervention, and higher engagement.
- Islamic Trauma Healing: d ≈ 0.67 versus waitlist.

They are small, developer-led and at risk of allegiance bias. A 2026 Muslim-sample meta-analysis pools interventions that the user's rules exclude, so its pooled estimate should not be used.

### Cited Findings
- [Meta-analysis, 97 studies, N = 7,181] R/S-adapted psychotherapy compared with no treatment: g = 0.74 for both psychological and spiritual outcomes. Compared with alternate secular therapies: psychological g = 0.33, spiritual g = 0.43. In rigorous **additive** designs, which add R/S content to the same therapy, adapted therapy was **equally effective** for psychological distress (g = 0.13, p = .258) but gave more spiritual well-being (g = 0.34). — [Captari et al. 2018, J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30221353/)
- [Meta-analysis, 16 RCTs, all CBT-based] Faith-adapted CBT showed statistically significant benefits. Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment found major limitations, with **researcher/therapist allegiance** a particular concern, so firm recommendations await larger rigorous trials. — [Anderson et al. 2015, J Affect Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25723562/)
- [Meta-analysis, 8 RCTs from 2015–2021] Religious-based interventions reduced depressive symptoms and were reported superior to usual therapies. Intervention definitions varied substantially. — [Marques et al. 2022, J Affect Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35500682/)
- [Systematic review, 25 studies] The literature on treating depression in Muslims is generally poor quality, often contradictory, and rarely separates religion from culture. — [Walpole et al. 2013, J Affect Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22854098/)
- [Development and feasibility] Behavioural Activation for Muslims (BA-M) was adapted from a systematic review, 29 key-informant interviews, and a feasibility study with 19 patients and 13 practitioners. Religious teachings could reinforce BA strategies. Patients valued therapist professionalism and empathy more than a shared religious identity, and therapists needed more support than expected. — [Mir et al. 2015, J Affect Disord](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25913804/)
- [Patient-level meta-analysis of 2 RCTs and 1 feasibility trial; N = 266: Turkey 22, Pakistan 103, UK 141] Comparators were CBT and, in the UK, social interventions. BA-M produced greater PHQ-9 reductions: adjusted mean difference −2.72 (95% CI −3.93 to −1.51). Engagement was higher (IRR 1.30; 1.18–1.44). — [Mir et al. 2026, Glob Ment Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42553194/)
- [RCT, N = 101 Somali refugees in the US, mostly women] Islamic Trauma Healing is lay-led and mosque-based. It combines psychoeducation, discussion of **the lives of prophets who underwent trauma**, and informal prayer turning to Allah about the trauma, while building in cognitive restructuring and imaginal exposure. Compared with waitlist after the intervention: PTSD d = −0.67, depression d = −0.66, well-being d = 0.71. — [Zoellner et al. 2024, JAMA Netw Open](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39186273/)
- [Early controlled trial, Malaysia] Religious psychotherapy added to supportive psychotherapy and anxiolytics produced significantly faster improvement in generalized anxiety than supportive therapy plus drugs alone. — [Azhar, Varma & Dharap 1994, Acta Psychiatr Scand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7976440/)
- [Feasibility RCT protocol, UK, young Muslim women aged 18–24] The faith-based IM-Adapted intervention is compared with an NHS psychoeducation group; this is a protocol and no outcomes have been reported. — [Smith et al. 2025, Pilot Feasibility Stud](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40847382/)
- [Systematic review/meta-analysis, 16 RCTs in Muslim samples, up to 1,030 participants] The pooled interventions were heterogeneous and **include approaches excluded under the user's source rules** (not described here), alongside religiously adapted psychotherapy. Its pooled estimates therefore cannot stand as evidence for Qur'an-and-Sunnah-based therapy specifically. — [Doğan et al. 2026 (DergiPark)](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/hid/article/1873679)

### Inferences
- **Calibrated message for the video.** Weaving Islam into therapy is not magic. In the best-controlled studies it works about as well as standard therapy for symptoms, adds spiritual well-being, and may keep Muslims engaged (BA-M IRR 1.30). For a religious viewer, the practical point is that faith-congruent CBT or BA is a legitimate and evidence-based option, not a compromise.
- **Fit with this person.** BA-M's logic (values-based activity scheduling reinforced by Islamic teachings) maps neatly onto MD triggered by discomfort at work: scheduling valued action replaces avoidance. ITH shows that prophets' stories can carry exposure and cognitive work, which is relevant if the writer wants Qur'anic narratives (e.g. patience under hardship) as reframes.
- **Framework screening done here.** BA-M (Islamic teachings, per its developers) and ITH (prophets' lives and du'a) show no Sufi framework in their abstracts. A full manual audit, including the gradings of any hadith inside the manuals, was not done.

### Gaps
- None of these trials targets MD. No faith-integrated intervention for MD was found.
- Hadith content inside the BA-M, ITH and Koenig RCBT (Muslim version) manuals was not audited against the user's grading rules. ([Koenig et al. 2012 RCBT design paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22778932/))
- Iranian programmes were not screened in full and are flagged, not recommended, because their source frameworks (likely Shi'i narrations not graded by Sunni muhaddithun) could not be verified against the user's rules: [Hosseini et al. 2017 RCBT protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29062373/) and [Dehaghi et al. 2024 ACT with religious content for OCD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39309981/).
- Some widely cited "Islamic psychotherapy" models rest on Sufi-derived frameworks (e.g. "Traditional Islamically Integrated Psychotherapy"). Muraqaba-style "Islamic mindfulness" and "heart meditation" programmes also appeared in searches. All are excluded per the user's rule and are not described.
- The pooled effect sizes of Doğan et al. 2026 were not extracted, and should not be used without re-pooling only eligible trials.

## 7. Risks: scrupulosity/waswas in Muslims (prevalence, prayer/wudu presentation, treatment) and worship becoming a compulsion or an escape

### Takeaway
Religious obsessions are among the most common OCD themes in Muslim-majority clinical samples:
- Egypt: religious and contamination obsessions together at 60%.
- Turkey: 42% with religious obsessions.
- Saudi Arabia: religious themes predominated.

The vulnerability lies in beliefs that thoughts are important and must be controlled, and in guilt, not in religiosity as such. The Sunnah contains built-in anti-scrupulosity defaults:
- Build on certainty when unsure in prayer.
- Do not leave prayer over an imagined breaking of wudu.
- A warning against those who "transgress in purification."
- "Ruined are the extremists."

ERP, ideally coordinated with a knowledgeable scholar, is the recommended treatment, but most surveyed imams did not know scrupulosity or ERP. For someone with MD (where about 54% have an OCD-related disorder), the two specific risks are turning dhikr or prayer into neutralizing rituals, and using worship to escape uncomfortable work. Both are visible in the coping and spiritual-bypassing literature.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence and phenomenology**
- [Clinical series, 32 Muslim Saudis with OCD] Compulsive acts (78%) and doubts (66%) were the commonest forms, and "religious themes predominated in both the obsessions and compulsions." — [Mahgoub & Abdel-Hafeiz 1991, Br J Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1873636/)
- [Clinical series, 90 OCD outpatients in Cairo] The most common obsessions were religious and contamination (reported together, 60%) and somatic (49%). The most common compulsions were repeating rituals (68%), cleaning and washing (63%), and checking (58%). — [Okasha et al. 1994, Compr Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8045109/)
- [Clinical series, 45 OCD outpatients in Turkey] 42% had religious obsessions. Religiosity (Religious Practices Index) did **not** differ between those with and without religious obsessions; the only predictor of religious obsessions was having more types of obsession. — [Tek & Ulug 2001, Psychiatry Res](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11711164/)
- [School survey, 1,299 Alexandrian adolescents] 15.5% had obsessive-compulsive symptoms and 2.2% met OCD criteria; religious attitudes were also assessed. The abstract retrieved was truncated before the religious findings. — [Rady et al. 2012, Glob J Health Sci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23121759/)
- [Cross-sectional, Turkish vs Canadian students] Highly religious Turkish students scored higher than highly religious Canadians on Fear of God (PIOS) but not on Fear of Sin. Obsessions, importance/control-of-thought beliefs and guilt uniquely predicted scrupulosity. — [Inozu, Clark & Karanci 2012, Behav Ther](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22304890/)
- [Cross-sectional, 235 undergraduates, Western] Scrupulosity predicted contamination, especially mental contamination, more strongly than religiosity did, which supports separating normative belief from pathological doubt. — [Inozu et al. 2026, J Cogn Psychother](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40639964/)
- [Conceptual] Scrupulosity has been framed cognitively using Islamic sources, a difficult issue for religious authorities because the feared consequence is religious. — [Besiroglu, Karaca & Keskin 2014, J Relig Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22395755/)
- [Practitioner article, IOCDF/NOCD; via search summary] Religious OCD in Muslims shows as intrusive doubts and compulsions aimed at proving sincerity or avoiding punishment: **repeating wudu, restarting prayers, mentally checking for blasphemy**. ERP is the most effective form of CBT for it, and exercises must not violate the person's religious beliefs. — [IOCDF article by T. Surles (NOCD)](https://iocdf.org/?p=66627)

**Sunnah texts that build in anti-scrupulosity defaults** (quoted, not ruled on; for application **ask a qualified scholar**)
- [Sahih al-Bukhari 137] A man was described who "imagines that he feels something in prayer." The Prophet said «لاَ يَنْفَتِلْ ـ أَوْ لاَ يَنْصَرِفْ ـ حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ صَوْتًا أَوْ يَجِدَ رِيحًا» ("he should not turn away until he hears a sound or finds a smell"). Bukhari's chapter heading is «باب لا يتوضأ من الشك حتى يستيقن» ("one does not do wudu because of doubt until certain"). — [Bukhari 137](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:137); [hadithunlocked (Fath al-Bari)](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:137)
- [Sahih Muslim 362] If someone feels something in his abdomen and is unsure whether anything came out, «فَلاَ يَخْرُجَنَّ مِنَ الْمَسْجِدِ حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ صَوْتًا أَوْ يَجِدَ رِيحًا» ("he should not leave the mosque until he hears a sound or finds a smell"). — [Muslim 362](https://sunnah.com/muslim:362)
- [Sahih Muslim 571] «إِذَا شَكَّ أَحَدُكُمْ فِي صَلاَتِهِ… فَلْيَطْرَحِ الشَّكَّ وَلْيَبْنِ عَلَى مَا اسْتَيْقَنَ…» ("if one of you doubts in his prayer… let him cast aside the doubt and build on what he is certain of…"), with two prostrations that, if he had prayed correctly, "are a humiliation for Shaytan." — [Muslim 571](https://sunnah.com/muslim:571)
- [Sunan Abi Dawud 96: **Sahih** per al-Albani] «إِنَّهُ سَيَكُونُ فِي هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ قَوْمٌ يَعْتَدُونَ فِي الطُّهُورِ وَالدُّعَاءِ» ("there will be in this ummah people who transgress the limits in purification and in supplication"). — [Abu Dawud 96](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:96); [grades in dataset](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/fawazahmed0/hadith-api@1/editions/eng-abudawud/96.json)
- [Sahih Muslim 2670] «هَلَكَ الْمُتَنَطِّعُونَ» ("Ruined are the extremists"), said three times. al-Nawawi glosses the extremists as «الغالون المجاوزون الحدود في أقوالهم وأفعالهم» ("those who exaggerate and exceed the limits in their words and deeds"). — [Muslim 2670](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2670); [hadithunlocked (al-Minhaj)](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2670)
- [Sahih al-Bukhari 39 + Ibn Hajar] "No one overburdens himself in religion except that it overwhelms him." See section 3 for Ibn Hajar's gloss and Ibn al-Munayyir's "every extremist breaks off." — [hadithunlocked, Bukhari 39](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:39)

**Treatment and religious leaders**
- [Clinical paper] ERP, the first-line OCD treatment, can be adapted to stay consistent with Ultra-Orthodox Jewish values, which raises motivation and participation. This is the standard model for scrupulosity work with religious authorities. — [Huppert, Siev & Kushner 2007, J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17828763/)
- [Commentary] Storch & Fuselier call for more collaboration with clergy in treating scrupulosity OCD by harnessing patient values. — [Storch & Fuselier 2023, J Cogn Psychother](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36788000/)
- [Qualitative, 9 patients with religious OCD in Türkiye] Therapy helped when the therapist was competent in religious knowledge and applied it meaningfully, combined normalisation and psychoeducation, integrated religious and scientific knowledge, and was non-judgemental and empathic. — [Toprak 2026, Br J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42068076/)
- [Survey, 64 imams: Sunni in Australia, Shi'i in Iran] Most were unfamiliar with scrupulosity as a possible OCD symptom and with ERP. Sunni imams in Australia were more likely to give advice inconsistent with ERP and less likely to refer outside their denomination, and scored higher on thought–action fusion. — [Jones, Harris & Esfahani 2019, Behaviour Change (via search summary)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behaviour-change/article/imams-experience-with-and-response-to-mosquegoers-with-ocd-scrupulosity/93148705F336D0997856B3B1B09B2D6C); [related USyd thesis](https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/12553?show=full)

**MD–OCD overlap, and worship as escape**
- [Structured clinical interviews, 39 people with MD] 53.9% met criteria for an obsessive-compulsive or related disorder. ADHD was 76.9%, anxiety 71.8% and depression 66.7%; 28.2% had attempted suicide. — [Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017, J Nerv Ment Dis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28598955/)
- [Meta-analysis] Negative religious coping, including "**avoiding difficulties through religious activities**," was associated with *higher* depressive symptoms. — [Smith et al. 2003, Psychol Bull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12848223/)
- [Meta-ethnography, 5 studies from 2014–2023, 55 participants, including a Muslim American context] Spiritual bypassing, "the use of spiritual beliefs or practices to avoid unresolved psychological distress," is better understood as a contextually adaptive response to pain than as a fixed deficit. It resolves through *differentiation from*, not *abandonment of*, spirituality, moving from defensive to integrative spirituality. — [Patel & Kumar 2026, Front Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42609545/)
- [Cross-sectional, 435 adults in Spain and Honduras] Spiritual bypass is defined as avoiding emotional problems and trauma instead of healing and learning from them. Its levels and predictors vary by culture. — [Motiño et al. 2021, Front Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34025522/)
- [Cross-sectional, 428 Iranian adults] People with MD showed significantly higher **experiential avoidance**, more self-judgment, isolation and over-identification, lower mindfulness, and higher other-oriented and socially-prescribed perfectionism. — [Pourmoazzen et al. 2026, Iran J Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42051277/)

### Inferences
- **The two concrete risks for this viewer:**
  - *Compulsion risk.* Given the 54% OCD-related comorbidity in MD and the user's course content, a "replace daydreams with dhikr" message could turn into repetitive neutralizing: saying a'udhu billah or dhikr *every* time a fantasy appears until it feels clean, repeating wudu or prayers, or seeking reassurance from shaykhs. Warning signs include doing it to get certainty or relief, needing it to feel "just right," time creep, and distress when it is interrupted. The Sunnah's own defaults (build on certainty; don't leave prayer for imagined hadath; don't transgress in purification; "ruined are the extremists") run in the same direction as ERP response prevention.
  - *Escape risk.* The user's MD starts when work gets uncomfortable, which is experiential avoidance (Pourmoazzen 2026). If he leaves an uncomfortable editing task to do extra voluntary worship and never returns, worship is doing the same job the fantasy did. That matches "avoiding difficulties through religious activities" (Smith 2003) and spiritual bypassing (Patel 2026). The functional test is whether the act *returns* him to the task, as in the collaborative "seek Allah's help and do not be helpless" (Muslim 2664) followed by working, or *replaces* the task.
- **A safe framing** for the video: worship as a *bridge back* to work, not a hiding place. Use a brief, one-time, Sunnah-sized act (one isti'adha, a short du'a, two rak'ahs at a natural break, the bedtime routine), then return to the task. Anything repeated "until it feels right" is a reason to speak with an OCD-informed clinician *and* a qualified scholar together.
- **Choosing helpers.** Many imams do not know ERP (Jones 2019), and patients benefit from therapists who know the religion (Toprak 2026). A sound recommendation is a clinician trained in ERP who will consult a qualified scholar on what exposures are permissible.

### Gaps
- No Muslim-specific RCT of ERP for scrupulosity was found, and no published case of formal scholar–therapist co-treatment of wudu or prayer compulsions was located.
- al-Nawawi's statement of the legal maxim on certainty and doubt under Muslim 362 was not found in the retrieved text. It is not quoted, and the hadith texts and Bukhari's chapter heading stand on their own.
- No study was found that measures "worship as avoidance" or compulsive dhikr in people with MD.
- Full-text prevalence figures for religious obsessions in community (non-clinical) Muslim samples were not retrieved. Okasha's 60% combines religious and contamination obsessions.

## 8. MD in Muslim-majority or Arab samples, including religiosity and prayer

### Takeaway
An Arabic MDS-16 exists (N = 180, multi-country Arab sample; two factors; high sensitivity and specificity), as do Turkish and Persian versions. Student samples in Muslim-majority countries report high rates of self-identified or above-cutoff MD:
- Sudan: 34.3% of 323 medical students.
- Saudi Arabia: 70% using an older 14-item scale, which is likely inflated.

MD in these samples is linked with anxiety and GAD, lower GPA, experiential avoidance, perfectionism, shame, emotion dysregulation and cognitive fusion. No study was found that relates religiosity, prayer or religious coping to MD.

### Cited Findings
- [Validation] The Arabic MDS-16 (MDS-16-AR) was tested in 180 adults from several Arab countries, aged 18–58 (M = 26.28). Two strongly related factors emerged: immersive daydreaming, and distress/impairment. The scale differentiated self-identified MDers from others and had high sensitivity and specificity. Reference: Psychology of Consciousness 6(2):171–183, 2019. Details come from search summaries of the record. — [Abu-Rayya, Somer & Meari-Amir 2019 (Univ. of Haifa record)](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/the-psychometric-properties-of-the-arabic-16-item-maladaptive-day/)
- [Cross-sectional, 323 medical students, University of Khartoum] Using the MDS-16-AR, 34.3% self-identified as maladaptive daydreamers. The mean MDS score was 33.6 (SD 22.3) and rates varied by academic year, from 13.4% in 6th year to 51.7% in 3rd year. The study reports MDS-16-AR sensitivity of 89 and specificity of 87 at a cutoff of 45/100. Details are via search summary of the full text. — [Bashir 2021, Middle East Curr Psychiatry (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8352612)
- [Cross-sectional, Saudi medical students] Using the 14-item MDS, MD prevalence was 70% and GAD 80% (GAD-7) or 55% (GAD-7 plus PSWQ). MDers had significantly lower GPA and higher GAD-7 and PSWQ scores. — [Alenizi et al. 2020, Cureus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33033669/)
- [Validation, 357 Turkish adults: 251 with ADHD, 106 controls] The Turkish MDS-16 had a four-factor structure and α = .93, and the MDS-5 α = .87. Yearning and Music/Kinesthesia items captured MD-specific features, while Impairment overlapped more with ADHD. — [Kandeğer et al. 2026, Br J Clin Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41736174/)
- [Validation] An earlier Turkish validity and reliability study (2022) and a Persian MDS-16 validation in Iranian students reported acceptable psychometrics; both are from search summaries. — [Turkish MDS study (Turkmedline)](https://turkmedline.net/detay/turkish-validity-and-reliability-study-of-maladaptive-daydreaming-scale/9726b2594160342d/tr/464); [Persian MDS-16 (Semnan Univ. journal)](https://cprpi.semnan.ac.ir/article_7219_en.html)
- [Cross-sectional, 428 Iranian adults, MDS-16 ≥ 50 cutoff] The MD group showed higher experiential avoidance, more negative self-compassion components, lower mindfulness, and higher other-oriented and socially-prescribed perfectionism. — [Pourmoazzen et al. 2026, Iran J Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42051277/)
- [Cross-sectional SEM, about 400 Iranian adults] Anxious attachment and emotion dysregulation were significant antecedents of MD, with shame and emotion dysregulation as mediators. — [Pourmoazzen et al. 2025, BMC Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41024174/)
- [Cross-sectional, 366 medical students] Cognitive fusion and psychological distress fully mediated the link between childhood trauma and MD. — [Karaağaç et al. 2026, BMC Psychol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41913230/)
- [Search result] PubMed searches for "maladaptive daydreaming" combined with religiosity, prayer, Muslim, Saudi, Arab or Lebanon returned no study relating religiosity or prayer to MD. — [PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=maladaptive+daydreaming+religiosity)

### Inferences
- **The prevalence figures are not comparable with general-population estimates.** The Saudi 70% used an older 14-item scale and the Sudan 34% was self-identification. Both are student samples and should not be quoted as "Muslims have more MD." What they show is that MD-like absorption is common and impairing (GPA, anxiety) among young people in these countries too.
- **Mechanism overlap with this person's story.** Experiential avoidance, perfectionism and cognitive fusion (Iran and Türkiye samples) match "daydreams start when work gets uncomfortable." The Islamic material in sections 1–4 maps onto exactly these targets:
  - Avoidance → the collaborative "strive and seek help; do not be helpless" (Muslim 2664).
  - Perfectionism → "the most consistent, even if small"; "every extremist breaks off" (Bukhari 6464; Bukhari 39 with Ibn Hajar).
  - Fusion with thoughts → "thoughts are forgiven unless acted on"; "turn away without arguing" (Bukhari 5269, Muslim 132–134 with al-Nawawi).

### Gaps
- No data were found on religiosity, prayer frequency, khushu', dhikr or religious coping in relation to MD in any sample, Muslim or otherwise. This is a genuine gap in the literature, not just in this search.
- No UAE-specific MD study was found. The full texts of the Arabic MDS-16 validation and the Sudan study were not opened directly; their figures come from search summaries.
- It is not known whether MD and religious scrupulosity co-occur specifically, as opposed to MD and OCD in general, in Muslim samples.

# Break the Fantasy Loop, Then Build Skill

Maladaptive daydreaming (MD) is compulsive, vivid, story-like fantasy, often launched on purpose with music and movement, that a person craves, struggles to stop, and lets crowd out work, relationships and sleep; it is not yet in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11, but 27 dissociation experts formally proposed it as a dissociative disorder in 2025, and the only interview-confirmed population study puts it at **about 2.5% of adults**, highest among 18–30-year-olds ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/); [Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)). Your pattern (drifting when work gets uncomfortable, reaching for music to deepen it, success fantasies, flare-ups after exciting news, bedtime and idle time) matches the proposed criteria almost item by item, and the strongest evidence describes it as an avoidance loop: the fantasy repairs your mood and supplies a feeling of competence now, while the cost arrives later as lost hours (MDers report **57% of waking hours** in fantasy against 16% for controls), more negative emotion the next day and more distress a year on. Success fantasies are the costliest kind for someone building a business, because idealized fantasies about the future experimentally lower energy and predict fewer job applications, lower pay and later depression. Music is the loop's main amplifier: it makes imagined scenes **about 2.7 times more vivid**, 36 of 41 people in the only study of music in MD said it deepens their fantasies and makes them harder to leave, and lyrics plus fast, loud beats separately impair the verbal work scripting and ideation need, so the evidence and your values point the same way, toward silence for focused work, with steady noise or nature sounds as a temporary bridge. For learning, producing beats consuming: retrieval and spacing (g ≈ 0.5–0.74) far outperform rereading and tutorials, watching experts raises confidence without raising skill, and students given unrestricted AI help did **17% worse** once it was taken away. The only MD treatment trial found large gains from mindfulness plus daily self-monitoring and none from willpower plus online forums, which is why the closing protocol rests on removing cues, if-then plans, self-monitoring, mental contrasting and output-based practice, each graded for evidence strength.

## Maladaptive daydreaming is compulsive, vivid fantasy that crowds out real life

Almost all MD research comes from one network of researchers: Eli Somer at the University of Haifa, Nirit Soffer-Dudek at Ben-Gurion University, and their collaborators. Most of it recruits self-identified sufferers online, and roughly three-quarters of them are women. That does not make the findings wrong, but it is why so many claims below carry an evidence label.

### Two decades of definitions converged on immersion, compulsion and cost

Somer coined the term in 2002 after treating **six patients in a trauma practice**. He defined MD as "extensive fantasy activity that replaces human interaction and/or interferes with academic, interpersonal, or vocational functioning." He concluded that it grew from "a normal childhood propensity for creative imagination" that hard circumstances turned into a problem ([Somer 2002](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/)). Bigelsen and Schupak's 2011 survey of **90 self-identified "non-normative fantasizers"** sharpened the picture and proposed the name "compulsive fantasy." Participants' distress came from "difficulty in controlling the need or desire to engage in fantasizing," from worry that fantasy "interfered with actual relationships and endeavors," and from "intense shame." **79% moved their bodies while fantasizing** ([Bigelsen & Schupak 2011](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21959201)). Participants estimated that fantasy took up about 56% of their waking hours and described it as "extraordinarily vivid, detailed, and compelling" ([Burrell, Lawson & Christoff 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)).

Formal tools followed. The 14-item Maladaptive Daydreaming Scale, developed with **447 participants in 45 countries**, found three dimensions: Yearning, Kinesthesia (movement) and Impairment. Scores tracked obsessive-compulsive symptoms, dissociative absorption and attention deficit ([Somer et al. 2016](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26707384)). The first controlled comparison, **340 MDers against 107 controls**, found that MD "differs significantly from normative daydreaming in terms of quantity, content, experience, controllability, distress, and interference with life functioning" ([Bigelsen et al. 2016](https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=33452)). In 2017 the group proposed criteria for "Daydreaming Disorder." The core requirement was vivid fantasy with intense absorption. On top of that came further features from a list that reads like your own description: triggered or maintained by music; triggered or maintained by repetitive movement; daydreaming "when distressed or bored"; more daydreaming when alone; annoyance when interrupted; preferring to daydream over "daily chores, social, academic, or professional activities"; and repeated failed attempts to cut back ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). The same paper called MD "a behavioral addiction for vivid fanciful imagery that can last for hours, and be triggered or maintained either by evocative music or repetitive physical movement."

The current proposal, a 2025 *British Journal of Psychiatry* position paper, turns this into a usable bar. It requires persistent or compulsive vivid fantasy with intense absorption, plus at least two associated features: preferring fantasy to reality, repetitive movements, short-term relief from "stress, anxiety, guilt, shame, low self-worth or loneliness," annoyance when interrupted, or failed attempts at control. The fantasy must take **at least 30 minutes per episode and at least 1 hour per day, on most days, for 6 months**, it must cause clinically significant distress or impairment, and it must not be better explained by OCD, ADHD, PTSD, autism or similar conditions ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). Some individual episodes last 4–5 hours. Immersion is "often intensified using evocative music and kinaesthetic stereotypies, including facial gestures, circular pacing, whispering of dialogues." Through every revision the core picture has barely changed: a deliberate, story-based, emotionally rewarding inner world, fed by music, movement and solitude, that pushes out real goals. What changed is that the definition dropped its original trauma-and-loneliness framing for one that makes no claim about cause. A minority think the label itself is wrong. Burrell, Lawson and Christoff argue that these episodes are "not daydreams, because they are more constrained in their mental dynamics," and prefer the term "compulsive fantasy" ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)).

### From the inside, MD feels like living two parallel lives

The richest first-person data come from 21 in-depth interviews. People described MD as "an excessive form of unwanted daydreaming that produces a rewarding experience based on a created fantasy of a parallel reality associated with a profound sense of presence" ([Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/parallel-lives-a-phenomenological-study-of-the-lived-experience-o/)). Their "natural capacity for vivid daydreaming had developed into a time-consuming habit that resulted in serious dysfunction." The fantasies were "laced with emotionally compensatory themes involving **competency, social recognition, and support**." Being alone was necessary, because "social interaction seems to be incompatible with this absorbing mental activity," and music or movement "appeared to be essential features." Typical plots include an idealized version of the self, romance, relationships with celebrities, wish fulfillment, alternate families, and sci-fi or medieval worlds that "continued for years with characters aging appropriately over time" ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). Success-and-performance plots appear explicitly; one interviewee described scenes "when I am someone famous who answers a question in an interview in a very intelligent way" ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). People describe a "double consciousness," experiencing reality and fantasy at the same time, and episodes can include whispered dialogue and acting out the plot ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)).

Three things recur in these accounts: reward, ritual and regret. The **reward** is strong enough that a quarter of Bigelsen and Schupak's sample spontaneously called MD "a compulsive addiction that engenders irritation, anxiety, and even illness when curbed." One said, "I can't turn it off… maybe like a drinker who promised himself 'this is the last bottle'," and another, "I am in control of what drug I want, but not how much I consume it" ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). The **ritual** is deliberate: people set up music, solitude and movement on purpose even while feeling compelled, which Burrell and colleagues call an "agency paradox," a felt sense of control inside the fantasy alongside lost control in real life (a theoretical idea, not yet tested) ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)). The **regret** shows up as shame and secrecy. A grounded-theory study found seven themes, running from "vivid fantasy as an innate talent" through "compelling yearning" and "shame and secrecy" to "failed treatment attempts" ([Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016b](https://doi.org/10.1097/nmd.0000000000000507)). Only **23%** of Bigelsen and Schupak's sample had ever sought therapy, and dismissive responses from clinicians were a frequent complaint ([Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)).

For many people, learning the term "has been utterly life-changing" ([Soffer-Dudek 2022](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/consciousness-and-psychopathology/202205/why-we-should-take-maladaptive-daydreaming-seriously)). An analysis of 20 literary works, from *Frankenstein* to *The Great Gatsby*, found MD-like patterns long before the internet existed ([PsyPost on Somer & Iyer 2026](https://www.psypost.org/classic-literature-shows-maladaptive-daydreaming-is-not-a-new-psychological-trend/)).

### A 16-item scale screens for MD, but neither psychiatric manual lists it

The standard screen is the **MDS-16**. It added two music items to the original scale after "consistent guidance from members of the MD community" ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). It measures four factors (Yearning, Impairment, Kinesthesia and Music), and that structure held across the USA, Italy, Turkey and the UK (N = 1,081). One nuance matters: the Yearning factor did not mean the same thing across cultures. That led the authors to conclude that interference with daily life and long-term goals "may be the central defining factor of MD" ([Soffer-Dudek et al., J. Behavioral Addictions](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/different-cultures-similar-daydream-addiction-an-examination-of-t/)). An Arabic version has been validated with 180 respondents from several Arab countries ([Abu-Rayya, Somer & Meari-Amir 2019](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/the-psychometric-properties-of-the-arabic-16-item-maladaptive-day/)).

In the original validation, a cut-off of **50** separated 31 interview-confirmed MDers from 31 controls with 96.8% sensitivity and 100% specificity. Two blinded interviewers using the Structured Clinical Interview for MD (SCIMD) agreed well (κ = .63–.84) ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). Real-world screening is messier. Population studies using a lower cut-off of **40** confirmed only **60% of people who screened positive** when they were interviewed ([Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)). Among adults with ADHD, the MDS-16 struggled to tell ADHD alone from ADHD plus MD; its specificity fell to **44.7%** ([Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)). Newer tools include a five-item screener tested on 2,512 US adults ([Soffer-Dudek & Oh 2024](https://doaj.org/article/f6e4a128e4294d68a93f1cf34023254e)). There is also an addiction-framed Addictive Daydreaming Scale, whose scores tracked loss of quality of life more closely than older measures did ([Pietkiewicz, Hełka & Tomalski 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1702416/full)).

Officially, "neither DSM-5-TR nor ICD-11 currently includes maladaptive daydreaming as a formal diagnosis" ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025, BJPsych](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/maladaptive-daydreaming-should-be-included-as-a-dissociative-disorder-in-psychiatric-manuals-position-paper/54F944C59CB77D9224E6BB78ADBCDF4A)). The March 2025 position paper was signed by **27 dissociation researchers and clinicians**, including Colin Ross, David Spiegel and Somer. It argues that MD meets four of the five classic tests of whether a psychiatric diagnosis is valid: a clear clinical description, psychological tests, differentiation from other disorders, and early follow-up showing stability. Only laboratory markers and family studies are missing ([Medical Xpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-03-maladaptive-daydreaming-dissociative-disorder-psychiatric.html)). As of October 2026, no response from the American Psychiatric Association or the WHO could be found. In practice this means two things: an online MDS-16 score means "possible MD," not a diagnosis, and many clinicians will not recognize the term.

### Roughly 2.5% of adults have MD, and young adults have the most

The only prevalence study confirmed by clinical interview used four Israeli samples of about 1,000 people in total. **4.2%** of a community sample and **5.5–8.0%** of student samples scored above the screening cut-off; after interviews, the estimated true rate was **about 2.5%** of the general population and about 4.4% of students, with 18–30-year-olds at highest risk and "no clear female predominance" ([Soffer-Dudek & Theodor-Katz 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9091653/)). Research samples, by contrast, are heavily female, with 18,519 women against 5,905 men across the 40 studies in the 2025 meta-analysis ([Somer, Herscu, Samara & Abu-Rayya 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)). That skew reflects who joins online communities and volunteers for surveys more than who has MD, so a young man is not an unusual case.

Self-report studies of Arab and African students report much higher figures: **70%** of Saudi medical students on the 14-item scale, along with a significant drop in GPA among those flagged ([Alenizi et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7532859/)), **53.8%** of UAE undergraduates ([UAEU thesis](https://scholarworks.uaeu.ac.ae/all_theses/1131)), and **34.3%** of Sudanese medical students ([Middle East Current Psychiatry 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s43045-021-00122-8)). None of these studies used interviews, and the Israeli data show that even a stricter screen over-counts by about 40%, so these figures point to heavy immersive daydreaming under academic pressure rather than to the true rate of MD.

MD is common in clinical groups. It affects about **20.5% of adults with ADHD** ([Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)), an estimated 25–40% of people with dissociative identity disorder ([Soffer-Dudek, Aquarone & Somer 2025](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-among-patients-with-dissociative-identity/)), and 49 of 100 inpatients with dissociative disorders ([Ross, Ridgway & George 2020](https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.prcp.20190050)). It usually starts in childhood, where "reports of extensive immersive daydreaming starting at very early ages are the norm" ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). In 814 Italians followed for 13 months, MD was **highly stable** (r = .68) ([Musetti et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2023.00001)). The Reddit MD community alone has more than 121,000 members ([Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)).

### Immersion, compulsion and cost separate MD from ordinary mind-wandering

Everyone's mind wanders. In a study of 2,250 adults sampled about 250,000 times, minds wandered **46.9%** of waking hours, and the timing of the data suggested that wandering caused later unhappiness more than the reverse ([Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010, Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/11/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/)). Ordinary mind-wandering is spontaneous and "usually not particularly fanciful." It helps with planning ahead, creativity, shifting attention and relief from monotony ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). The psychologist Jerome Singer described three daydreaming styles: **positive-constructive** ("playful, wishful imagery, and planful, creative thought"), **guilty-dysphoric** ("obsessive, anguished fantasies") and **poor attentional control** ("inability to concentrate on either the ongoing thought or the external task") ([McMillan, Kaufman & Singer 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00626/full)). MD looks like the second and third styles combined, plus compulsion and impairment. It also differs from "fantasy proneness," which can involve hallucination-like experiences and confusing fantasy with reality. MD "is not characterised by psychotic-spectrum delusions, hallucinations or thought impairments" ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). The clearest numbers come from 156 adults diagnosed by interview: an "immersive daydreaming" factor correlated **r = .71 with MD**, but only r = .23 with ADHD and r = .31 with ordinary mind-wandering ([Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)). Even so, MD and everyday mind-wandering tend to rise and fall together (within-person r = .38 in daily diaries) ([Marcusson-Clavertz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880993/)).

So imagination itself is not what makes MD a problem. The position paper treats "an innate ability for immersive daydreaming, which may not in itself be psychopathological," as a risk factor rather than the disorder ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). Three markers together define the problem: **immersion** (a deliberate, story-based, emotionally vivid world rather than drifting thought), **compulsion** (yearning, irritation when interrupted, failed attempts to cut back) and **cost** (lost work, relationships or sleep). That gives you a simple test for any daydream: does it end in an action (a title, a script beat, a plan), or does it replace the action and keep looping?

### Six lenses each explain a different part of the same experience

Researchers disagree about what MD fundamentally is. Even its leading proponents concede that it "could arguably be categorised as a behavioural addiction, an obsessive–compulsive spectrum disorder or a unique subtype of attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder" ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)).

| Lens | Core claim | Strongest support | Strongest critique |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distinct disorder | MD is its own syndrome and needs its own name and treatment | Reliable interviews (κ = .63–.84); a consistent pattern of co-occurring disorders; stable over 13 months; responds to targeted treatment | Criteria "still somewhat arbitrary"; no brain markers or family studies; mostly online samples |
| Dissociative absorption | MD is a disorder of absorption and detachment and belongs with the dissociative disorders | Dissociation is the strongest specific correlate (r = .447); feeling unreal or detached increases on and after high-MD days | MD is sought out, enjoyed and partly voluntary, unlike classic dissociation |
| Behavioral addiction or compulsion | MD is an addiction to fantasy, or a compulsion on the OCD spectrum | A distinct "yearning" factor; 25% spontaneously call it an addiction; obsessive-compulsive symptoms come before next-day MD | No clear tolerance; the fantasy is enjoyable, unlike obsessions; yearning means different things across cultures |
| Attention / ADHD | MD is, or causes, an attention problem | ADHD in 76.9% of interviewed MDers; MDers say their inattention follows from MD | Only 20.5% of adults with ADHD have MD; immersion is not ordinary mind-wandering |
| Coping / escape | MD is a learned escape from distress, loneliness or unmet needs | Relief from stress, shame and loneliness; linked to difficulty regulating emotions (r = .365) | Link to trauma is small (r = .198) and trauma is not required; MD predicts later distress |
| Skeptical | The label turns imagination into a disease and rests on weak samples | Self-selected online samples; screens over-identify | Sufferers report real impairment; the label reduces shame |

**The distinct-disorder lens** rests on the claim that the whole package hangs together like other recognized disorders. The 2025 meta-analysis combined **40 studies (N = 24,977)** and found MD associated with overall psychopathology (r = .372), psychological distress (.492), shame (.451), dissociation (.447), depression (.432) and problematic internet use (.404), and with lower self-esteem (−.360). The authors concluded that MD "behaves like other DSM disorders" ([Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)). Among 39 MDers diagnosed by interview, **74.4%** met criteria for more than three other disorders: ADHD (76.9%), anxiety (71.8%), depression (66.7%) and OCD-related disorders (53.9%) ([Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)). The strongest single support is that a targeted online program helped while generic approaches did not; that trial is covered in the protocol below. Proponents concede that the criteria "are still somewhat arbitrary" and that adding MD would add to the growing number of diagnoses. Their answer to the "it's just an extreme version of normal" objection is that the same objection applies to most DSM disorders, and they note that major depression reached interviewer agreement of only κ = .28 in its field trials ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)).

**The dissociative lens** is the one behind the official 2025 proposal. It holds that MD involves "disconnection from perception, behaviour and sense of self." Feeling detached from yourself or from your surroundings rises on high-MD days and the day after, but not the day before ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). In a Hungarian sample of 717 people, dissociation explained part of the link between specific childhood traumas and MD ([Sándor et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-021-02223-3)). The weakness of this lens is that classic dissociation happens to people, whereas MDers seek out, enjoy and deliberately set up their fantasies with music, solitude and movement. That "contradict[s] purely automatic" models ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)). Much of the supporting data also comes from dissociation clinics, where MD would be expected to be over-represented.

**The addiction-and-compulsion lens** explains the craving and the relapses. In one video-recorded clinical case, MD "was developed as a strategy to cope with distress but led to uncontrollable absorption in fantasy, social withdrawal, and neglecting aspects of everyday life," alongside excessive internet and pornography use ([Pietkiewicz et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6426361/)). In 14-day diaries kept by 77 MDers, obsessive-compulsive symptoms were "the only consistent temporal antecedent of MD." MD in turn predicted next-day obsessive-compulsive symptoms, which the authors called "a vicious cycle" ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)). The dissociation camp pushes back on two points. First, "MD does not necessarily include gradually increasing elements to reach the same effect," meaning there is no clear tolerance. Second, "indulging in a daydream storyline is satisfying and enjoyable in the short term, unlike the distress caused by intrusive obsessions" ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)).

**The attention lens** matters most for your work. MDers who also met ADHD criteria "viewed the attentional problem as a resulting or secondary factor," and researchers propose that "addictive and immersive daydreaming causes inattention." In a survey of 202 MDers, stimulant medication had "next to no positive effect" on daydreaming and was reported to increase MD symptoms ([Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)). MD cannot simply be folded into ADHD, though. Only 20.5% of 83 adults with ADHD met MD criteria, and that subgroup had more depression and loneliness and lower self-esteem. Soffer-Dudek argues that some people diagnosed with ADHD "would benefit more from a diagnosis of MD" ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/04/220412141107.htm)). No study has followed MD and ADHD over time, so which drives which is still open.

**The coping lens** explains why MD started and what it does for you. Somer's original cases came from a trauma practice. He identified the fantasy's functions as "Disengagement from Stress and Pain by Mood Enhancement and Wish Fulfillment Fantasies" and "Companionship, Intimacy, and Soothing" ([Somer 2002](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-a-qualitative-inquiry/)). A 2025 scoping review of 89 studies calls MD "a dysfunctional coping mechanism" ([Mansuklal et al. 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40665643)). When trauma is present, it shapes the content: emotional neglect goes with idealized-family imagery, and neglect goes with revenge daydreams ([Somer, Abu-Rayya & Brenner 2021](https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=11619)). But the pooled correlation between MD and trauma is only r = .198 ([Somer et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858500/)), and MD appears in many people without difficult childhoods ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). Crucially, the 13-month data show that **MD predicts later distress**; it is not only a result of distress ([Musetti et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2023.00001)).

**The skeptical lens** targets the label, not the suffering. Psychologist Jonathan Raskin asks whether people who eat badly are "afflicted with 'dietary self-sabotage disorder'?" He warns against "conflating difference with dysfunction," and adds pointedly: "If she daydreams because she's bored, maybe the things she's doing bore her" ([Raskin 2022](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/making-meaning/202205/is-maladaptive-daydreaming-a-mental-disorder)). Burrell and colleagues call adding MD to the manuals now "detrimental," given the field's "heavy reliance on Reddit self-reports from potentially unrepresentative samples" ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)). No peer-reviewed paper argues that MD isn't real. Soffer-Dudek's reply is that "human behavior exists on a spectrum. If sadness or worry become too extensive… we diagnose depression or anxiety" ([Soffer-Dudek 2022](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/consciousness-and-psychopathology/202205/why-we-should-take-maladaptive-daydreaming-seriously)).

These lenses are best read as views of different parts of one experience, not as rival theories. **The addiction lens** explains the craving and the "one more scene" relapse, and why removing the cue (music) matters so much. **The coping lens** explains why uncomfortable work is the trigger and what the fantasy is doing for you in that moment. **The dissociation lens** explains the absorbed, detached feeling during an episode and the foggy aftermath. **The attention lens** explains the cost to focus, and warns that an ADHD label or stimulant medication on its own would probably miss the target. **The skeptics** contribute a genuinely useful question: if a task reliably sends you into fantasy, how much of that comes from the way the task is set up, and what need is the fantasy meeting that real life is not?

## One avoidance loop turns uncomfortable work into lost sessions

MD research rarely runs experiments, so the cause-and-effect claims in this section carry labels. **Experimental** evidence (people randomly assigned to conditions) supports causal claims, but nearly all of it comes from general-population studies of procrastination, mood and fantasy. **Longitudinal** data (one 13-month study) and **daily diaries** (two studies) show what follows what, which is stronger than a correlation but not proof. **Cross-sectional** correlations and **qualitative** interviews describe associations and lived experience. Most MD samples are self-selected online volunteers, 75–85% of them female.

### Music, solitude, distress, boredom and strong emotion start episodes

The proposed criteria list the best-supported triggers directly: music, repetitive movement, distress or boredom, and being alone ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). **Movement** accompanied fantasy in 79% of Bigelsen and Schupak's sample. One diary participant noted that "running, walking, and driving are more effective at invoking daydreams than sitting or lying still" ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)). **Music** has the most detailed evidence. It is covered in its own section below, but one finding belongs here: in the scale's validation, the item about needing music separated MDers from controls less sharply than any other item. So music is common in MD but not universal. It works best understood as an amplifier rather than the root cause ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). **Solitude and idle time** are close to a precondition. During Italy's COVID-19 lockdown, **17.2% of 6,277 adults** screened as probable MDers ([Musetti et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631979/full)), and existing MDers reported more time in fantasy and stronger urges (N = 1,565, 70+ countries) ([Somer et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667241/)).

**Distress and boredom** work through what Burrell and colleagues call "affective salience": strong emotion "can exert a pull on thought," and "the pleasure of emotional gratification also commands cognitive resources in an automatic way." A forum quote they cite captures the habit: "my brain has sort of been trained to escape into daydreams whenever I'm stressed, bored, or overwhelmed." Even when fantasy is not sought compulsively at first, "over time and repeated experiences, the process of fantasizing to emotionally regulate can become habitual" ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)).

The timing data add an important wrinkle. In the 14-day diary study, low mood, anxiety and other emotions on one day did not predict more MD the next day. Only obsessive-compulsive symptoms did (B = 1.30) ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)). Over 13 months, distress likewise did not predict later MD ([Musetti et al. 2023](https://air.unipr.it/retrieve/ee690cdb-2925-44d9-844e-fe5ae6f644c1/2023_Musetti%20et%20al.%20JBAdd.pdf)). Both sets of authors suggest that distress acts within minutes or hours, which day-level designs cannot detect. Nobody has yet measured the minute-by-minute sequence of a hard task, then discomfort, then an MD episode. Poor sleep did not predict next-day MD either, although it did predict more ordinary mind-wandering the next day (B = 0.20) ([Marcusson-Clavertz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880993/)). A 2025 clinical model ties these together. It lists stressors, emotional states and loneliness as *starters*, and music as an "emotional soundtrack," pacing and rocking as "hypnotic behaviors," and "difficulty sustaining real-world attention" as *maintainers* ([Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)).

### The fantasy pays out instantly in relief, competence and recognition

MD is rewarding in two ways at once. It adds something pleasant ("immensely gratifying fantasies involving such themes as romance, relationships with celebrities, **wish fulfillments, and idealized versions of self**"), and it removes something painful, "when emotionally distressed individuals soothe themselves with absorbing compensatory fantasizing" ([Somer et al. 2017](https://delphicentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Somer_et_-al_Maladaptive-_Daydreaming.pdf)). Personality shapes what the fantasy supplies. In 539 probable MDers, people high in grandiosity used fantasies for "wish-fulfillment of power and dominance" ([Somer et al., Univ. of Haifa](https://sdg.haifa.ac.il/?p=5722)). Among 242 probable MDers, sensitivity to reward was the most prominent motivational trait, and MD went with traits linked to "avoidance… and motivational tendencies towards reward seeking," as well as lower conscientiousness ([Wall & Clotworthy 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/02762366251352852)).

One finding fits your pattern especially closely. In a study of 317 people, MDers had **higher frustration intolerance** and more avoidance-based coping than non-MDers. The authors concluded that "the role of discomfort intolerance and its links to avoidance should be of significant focus in MD therapy" ([Nowacki & Pyszkowska 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-024-06382-x)). MDers also show more avoidance of unpleasant inner experiences and more harsh self-judgment ([Pourmoazzen et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111966/)).

The relief is real in the moment. Within one group of 252 MDers, more MD went with *less* depression and anxiety, which the authors read as MD managing negative emotion ([Current Psychology 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-06487-3)). The meta-analysis authors' overall verdict is that MD can serve as "self-entertainment, distraction from boredom, and compensatory wish fulfillment," but "cannot be considered an effective emotion regulation strategy" ([Kingston eprint](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/56752)). The next section shows why.

### Relief now, distress later: the loop link by link

No single MD study has tested the whole loop. Each link has its own evidence, though, and the pieces fit together:

| Link | What happens | Strongest evidence | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Work turns hard, boring or uncertain and feels unpleasant | How unpleasant a task feels is among the strongest predictors of procrastination (691 correlations) | Meta-analysis, general population |
| 2 | The unpleasant feeling becomes an urge to escape into fantasy | Criterion "daydreams when distressed or bored"; induced low mood increases off-task thought; MDers have higher discomfort intolerance | Criteria; experiment (general); cross-sectional |
| 3 | Music goes on and immersion deepens | 36 of 41 MDers say music strengthens MD and makes stopping harder | Qualitative |
| 4 | Immediate relief, pleasure and a felt sense of competence | Fantasy rewards by adding pleasure and removing pain; MD linked to less distress in the moment | Qualitative; cross-sectional |
| 5 | Relief strengthens the habit, so the urge comes faster next time | Stronger urge to return after being interrupted; theory of habit-forming fantasy | Cross-sectional; theory |
| 6 | Hours are lost and the work goes undone | 57% vs 16% of waking hours; 4.5 hours/day | Cross-sectional; diary |
| 7 | Guilt, shame and a widening gap between fantasy and reality | The gap is "often eased with further daydreaming" | Expert review; qualitative |
| 8 | More negative emotion the next day, and more distress over a year | Diary B = 0.37; 13-month cross-lagged paths | Diary; longitudinal |
| 9 | Lower mood feeds the next urge | Not supported at the day level; probably acts within hours, but untested | Gap |

The general-population links are the most solid. Steel's meta-analysis of 691 correlations found that "strong and consistent predictors of procrastination were **task aversiveness**, task delay, self-efficacy, and impulsiveness" ([Steel 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201571/)). Sirois and Pychyl describe procrastination as "short-term mood repair," with the costs "borne by the future self" ([Sirois & Pychyl 2013](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91793/)).

The causal test is striking. When people were led to believe their bad mood was "frozen" and could not be changed, their tendency to procrastinate disappeared. People put off work *because* they expect the escape to make them feel better ([Tice, Bratslavsky & Baumeister 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11195891/)). A second experiment showed that inducing a low mood made people lapse more, report more "task irrelevant thoughts," and become "less inclined to reengage" after a lapse ([Smallwood et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19348539/)).

The MD-specific links add the back half of the loop. In the diaries, a day of more MD was followed the next day by more negative emotion (B = 0.37), more obsessive-compulsive symptoms (B = 1.04) and more dissociation (B = 0.78). The authors concluded that "in the long term… MD does not promote pleasurable sensations but rather results in psychological distress" ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)). Over 13 months, MD predicted later stress, anxiety and depression, but not the reverse. The authors concluded that "MD may be used as a means to escape from psychological distress in the short term that results in long-term impairment" ([Musetti et al. 2023](https://air.unipr.it/retrieve/ee690cdb-2925-44d9-844e-fe5ae6f644c1/2023_Musetti%20et%20al.%20JBAdd.pdf)).

Researchers describe how the loop closes. MD "creates further distress associated with the **time wasted** and the **widening gap between the idealized fantasy and reality**, a disillusionment that is often **eased with further daydreaming**" ([Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)). Burrell and colleagues add an "agency" version: replacing real pursuits with fantasy "may, in turn, increase feelings of loneliness and helplessness, generating negative affect, which can trigger more powerful and frequent urges to fantasize" ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)). One published case, a 25-year-old man, showed the online version of this loop. Escapist activities caused impairment, which led to more MD and more internet use to relieve the distress ([Pezzi et al. 2024](https://art.torvergata.it/retrieve/0ffe267e-94d2-434c-b9e0-9f4f848cffc0/Pezzi%20et%20al.%2c%202024.pdf)).

The Tice finding gives you a practical lever. The escape is driven by the *belief* that it will repair your mood, and the diary data show that MD is followed by a *worse* mood the next day.

### The bill arrives as lost hours, weaker focus and worse mood a year on

**Time** is the most visible cost. MDers report spending **57% of their waking hours** in daydreams on average, compared with 16% for people without MD ([Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)). Diary participants reported **4.5 hours** of active daydreaming on the first day measured ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)). All of these figures are self-estimates.

**Focus** pays next. MDers report "a diminished ability to sustain attention externally due to their tendency to give in to the lure of their inner fantasy world" ([Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)). One diary participant said, "I often feel as if I just cannot turn off my mind, whether because I need to concentrate in class, go to sleep, or just find some peace" ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)). Somer stresses that during MD "the mind is not wandering off-task but instead **focused on creating fanciful scenarios**" ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). In other words, MD competes directly with work for the same deliberate attention. Across 88 samples in the general population, more mind-wandering meant worse task performance, and the cost was "**more pronounced for more complex tasks**" ([Randall, Oswald & Beier 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25089941/)). Scripting, editing decisions and business strategy are exactly those complex tasks.

**Work and income** suffer in clinical samples. In Somer's music study, **46% were unemployed** and fewer than 15% were in a relationship ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). During lockdown, MD was linked to impaired "social, family, and work-related activities" ([Musetti et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631979/full)). But no study has objectively measured MDers' work output, such as words written or hours of focused work. In one sample of medical students, MD correlated with procrastination only weakly (r = .18), and GPA was unaffected ([Karaağaç & Korkmaz 2025](https://www.turkpsikiyatri.com/Yayin/27th-National-Clinical-Education-Symposium-Presentation-Abstracts/maladaptive-daydreaming-and-academic-procrastination-the-relationship-with-psychological-symptoms-in-medical-students)).

**Mood** carries the strongest evidence on direction. Over 13 months, MD predicted later stress, anxiety and depression after accounting for where people started, but the effects were small (standardized β ≈ 0.07–0.11) ([Musetti et al. 2023](https://air.unipr.it/retrieve/ee690cdb-2925-44d9-844e-fe5ae6f644c1/2023_Musetti%20et%20al.%20JBAdd.pdf)). At the severe end of the spectrum, **28.2% of 39 MDers diagnosed by interview had attempted suicide** ([Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)). In a US panel of 2,512 adults, people flagged for suspected MD were more than twice as likely to have recently attempted suicide, even after accounting for distress (OR = 2.44) ([Soffer-Dudek & Oh 2024](https://doaj.org/article/f6e4a128e4294d68a93f1cf34023254e)). These are signals to take seriously, not predictions about any one person.

**Sleep** is the surprise. MDers widely report that daydreaming interferes with sleep. But in a pre-registered study of 126 people keeping daily diaries, MD did **not** predict worse sleep the following night (B = −0.00). Sleep loss did predict more ordinary mind-wandering the next day ([Marcusson-Clavertz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880993/)).

**Shame and isolation** reinforce each other. "Daydreaming and social isolation" are "a two-way street" ([Somer, Somer & Jopp 2016b](https://doi.org/10.1097/nmd.0000000000000507)). During lockdown, probable MDers even had *fewer* online social relationships ([Musetti et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631979/full)).

**Social media** is the most relevant correlate for a creator. MD's strongest link among online behaviors was with problematic social media use (partial r = 0.25). One proposed reason is that social media lets people manage their identity "in accordance with an **idealized self**" ([Pezzi et al. 2024](https://art.torvergata.it/retrieve/0ffe267e-94d2-434c-b9e0-9f4f848cffc0/Pezzi%20et%20al.%2c%202024.pdf)). In 437 Hungarians, mostly male, celebrity worship went together with problematic internet use, MD and **desire for fame** ([Zsila, McCutcheon & Demetrovics 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6426373/)). All of this is cross-sectional, and no study has looked at short-form video and MD specifically.

### Success fantasies drain the energy needed to succeed

Gabriele Oettingen's research separates two things that feel alike: **positive expectations**, meaning a judged likelihood of success, and **positive fantasies**, meaning vivid, pleasant images of the desired future. "Positive expectations predicted high effort and successful performance, but the reverse was true for positive fantasies" ([Oettingen & Mayer 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1198)). In one study, 83 German students rated their fantasies about graduating and landing a job. Two years later, those with the most positive fantasies had "put in **fewer job applications**, received **fewer job offers**, and ultimately earned **lower salaries**" ([NYU Stern summary](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/news/new-yorker-positive-thinking)). The same pattern held for romantic crushes, recovery from hip surgery and weight loss ([Kappes & Oettingen 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284)).

Experiments show how this works. Positive fantasy lowered systolic blood pressure, a marker of physical energization: it fell by 1.73 mmHg compared with a 0.23 mmHg rise in a questioning-fantasy condition. Students who fantasized about an ideal upcoming week felt less energized right away and **reported accomplishing less a week later**, and lower energy explained the effect. The de-energizing effect was larger when the need was more pressing. In the authors' words, positive fantasies "allow people to **mentally consummate a desired future**," so effort feels unnecessary ([Kappes & Oettingen 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284)).

The mood effect also reverses over time. Positive fantasies "related to **decreased symptoms of depression when measured concurrently**; however, positive fantasies predicted **more depressive symptoms when measured longitudinally**," up to 7 months later. In college students this was partly explained by lower grades ([Oettingen, Mayer & Portnow 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26825106/)). Positive fantasies also bias people toward seeking out information about a goal's upsides over its downsides, which leads to worse decisions ([Kappes & Oettingen 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22649113/)).

Oettingen's participants were not MDers, so applying her findings to MD is an inference, but a close one. MD success plots are exactly the idealized, obstacle-free fantasies she studied, run longer and more vividly. The remedy has evidence behind it. **Mental contrasting with implementation intentions** (MCII, known as WOOP) pairs the wish with the inner obstacle and an if-then plan. Across 21 studies and 15,907 participants it improved goal attainment with **g = 0.336**, and the effect was larger when delivered with another person (g = 0.465). Publication bias means the true effect may be smaller ([Wang, Wang & Gai 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)).

### Excitement is a plausible trigger that no study has tested

No study has examined positive events as triggers of MD, so your "something exciting happens, then I'm gone" pattern rests on indirect evidence. In its favor, the affective-salience theory covers emotional arousal of either kind, good or bad ([Burrell et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12562237/)); MDers score high on sensitivity to reward ([Wall & Clotworthy 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/02762366251352852)); and a 2026 preprint found that "positive valence and pleasure" in music drove daydreaming ([Ferreri et al. 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/a5cjf_v1)). Against it, in the general population *negative* mood, not positive mood, increased off-task thought ([Smallwood et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19348539/)), and in MD diaries positive emotion on one day did not predict MD the next ([Soffer-Dudek & Somer 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5962718/)).

The most plausible chain runs like this. Good news (a view spike, a lead, a new idea) brings excitement and an anticipated reward. The fantasy then elaborates the reward, music raises the excitement further, and you "consume" the success in your head. Energy for the actual next step drops, and that drop is the one link with experimental support. Treat this pattern as consistent with theory but unproven.

Two cautions apply. A close match to the criteria is not a diagnosis, because self-screening over-identifies MD by around 40% in population studies. And the co-occurring conditions that most often travel with MD (inattentive ADHD, anxiety, depression and obsessive-compulsive symptoms) are worth checking with a professional if any of them sound familiar.

## Your playlist is tuned for fantasy, not focus

No published study tests phonk, hardstyle, jumpstyle, slowed + reverb edits, sped-up songs or TikTok audios as background for work, or as fuel for daydreaming. Everything below that is specific to a genre is inferred from research on lyrics, tempo, loudness, arousal and emotion, and it is labeled that way.

### Lyrics and fast, loud beats cut performance on verbal work

The headline average is misleading. Across studies, background music versus silence shows essentially no overall effect (r = .03). That is because harm to reading and memory cancels out benefits to mood and sports performance ([Kämpfe, Sedlmeier & Renkewitz 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0305735610376261)). Look at the task type and the picture sharpens. A meta-analysis of 65 studies found **lyrical music harmed reading comprehension by d ≈ −0.35**, about as much as intelligible background speech, while instrumental music was roughly neutral ([Vasilev, Kirkby & Angele 2018](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/29995/)). A well-powered 2023 study found that lyrics, compared with silence, cut verbal recall (d = −.32), visual recall (d = −.33) and reading (d = −.19), while instrumental effects were negligible ([Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273)).

Liking the music does not protect you. In one study, people's own favorite lyrical songs were **as disruptive as music they disliked**, and they rated their favorites 75 out of 100 for distraction, against 10 for quiet ([Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)). The same paper describes two separate mechanisms. First, any sound that changes moment to moment, like beats, syllables and melodic jumps, disrupts tasks that require holding things in order in your head. Second, intelligible words compete with tasks that involve meaning. Rap, with dense, fast, understandable lyrics in your own language, hits both mechanisms at once, which makes scriptwriting its worst-case task. Introverts are more impaired by background music than extraverts ([Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)).

Tempo and loudness matter even without lyrics. When instrumental music was varied by speed and volume, **only the fast-and-loud combination pushed reading comprehension significantly below baseline** ([Thompson, Schellenberg & Letnic 2012](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ982853)). Your genres sit squarely in that corner. Hardstyle is typically produced around **150 BPM** with "overdriven and hard-sounding kick drums" ([Wikipedia: Hardstyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardstyle)); jumpstyle runs **140–150 BPM** ([MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/jumpstyle-music-guide)); drift and gym phonk run about **140–170 BPM** with distorted 808 bass and cowbell rolls, and gym phonk is built for "maximum aggression for workout motivation" ([Futureproof Music School](https://futureproofmusicschool.com/blog/what-is-phonk)); and sped-up edits play songs **about 35% faster** ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/103077116)).

Creative work is not an exception. On insight problems, the kind where you find *the* right answer, which is like choosing the one right hook, music with familiar lyrics, foreign lyrics or no lyrics all impaired performance compared with quiet. This held even for people who habitually study with music ([Threadgold et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)).

There are two narrow counterpoints. Upbeat instrumental music during loose, open-ended brainstorming raised creativity in one study ([Ritter & Ferguson 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0182210)). And preferred music *reduced* mind-wandering on a dull, low-demand monitoring task by raising arousal, though only in people who habitually use background music, and the authors caution that it has not been tested on complex tasks ([Kiss, Linnell & Szikora 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11281972/)). A 2023 meta-analysis that found a small benefit of music for learning (d = 0.314) drew mainly on music heard *before* the task and on classical music ([de la Mora Velasco et al. 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/03057356231153070)). That reflects a pre-task mood boost, not high-tempo lyrical music playing during verbal work.

### Music makes imagined scenes 2.7 times more vivid and harder to leave

Music does more than distract. It drives imagination. In a directed-imagery task, every music condition made imagined journeys **on average 2.7 times more vivid** than silence, and changed their emotional tone and content ([Herff et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-95260-8)). In a study of 62 people, heroic music produced mind-wandering that was far more positive (d = 0.96) and more exciting (d = 1.21) than sad music. Only about **2% of the thoughts reported involved logical reasoning** ([Koelsch et al. 2019](https://stefan-koelsch.de/papers/koelsch_bashevkin_kristensen_tvedt_jentschke_2019_heroic_music_stimulates_empowering_thoughts_during_mind-wandering_scientific_reports_91_10317.pdf)). Sad music, by contrast, produced *more* mind-wandering than happy music, of an inward, self-focused, emotional kind ([Taruffi et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-14849-0)).

The only dedicated study of music in MD interviewed 41 people with probable MD. Eleven were men, the mean age was 31, and the mean MDS-16 score was 61.4. The dominant theme, present in **36 of 41**, was that "music enhances the MD experience" ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). Participants described exactly the work-session problem: "**I can keep myself from daydreaming so long as I maintain concentration, but in the presence of music, that becomes difficult**"; "It's harder to come out of daydreaming if I'm listening to music. I can continue for hours"; music "may cause me to daydream even when I am trying to maintain focus." It sets the plot's pace: "intense music may induce a daydream about two characters having a brawl… characters may hit each other to the beats of the song." It drives movement: "If I listen to music on any occasion, I immediately feel a big urge to pace around." And it works as a mood dial: "Music is like a mood controller."

Somer concludes that music does four jobs. It distances the person from external reality, triggers complex visual storylines, deepens absorption, and intensifies the fantasy through emotion. He calls the relationship potentially "addictive or dependent" and recommends **stimulus control**, an addiction-treatment technique that means controlling exposure to the cue itself. A community artwork in the paper shows a woman trying to work with music on, frozen by fantasy, under the title "When the Escape Became the Prison." A 2026 preprint adds that music was "reported as the most effective tool for triggering and maintaining daydreaming, especially among MDers," and that musical absorption predicted MD severity ([Ferreri et al. 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/a5cjf_v1)).

### Each part of the playlist feeds fantasy by a different route

| Audio | Key features | Predicted effect on focused work | Predicted effect on daydreaming | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rap | Dense, fast, intelligible lyrics | Worst for scripting, reading and prompting: lyrics compete with meaning, and every word is a change in sound | Lyrics supply narrative and identity material | Lyric studies (strong); genre untested |
| Phonk / gym phonk | 140–170 BPM, distorted 808s, cowbell rolls, chopped vocal samples; tied to gym, drift and "edit" imagery of power | Fast + loud + constantly changing; less word interference than rap | Closest real-world match to "heroic" music, so it cues empowering self-as-hero fantasies and the urge to pace | Tempo/loudness and heroic-music studies; culture sources |
| Hardstyle / jumpstyle | ~140–160 BPM, overdriven kicks, repetitive leads | Fast + loud, the combination that pushed reading below baseline | High arousal and rhythmic drive invite movement and action fantasies | Tempo/loudness studies; genre untested |
| Sped-up edits | Familiar songs about 35% faster, lyrics still intelligible | Adds speed to every lyric problem | Familiar songs carry their own memories and associations | Inference only |
| Slowed + reverb | Slow, spacious, sad-leaning; described as "false nostalgia" | Less arousing, so it can feel "calm," but sad music increases inward mind-wandering | Plausibly the most fantasy-inducing part of the library | Sad-music studies; journalism |
| TikTok audios | Short, catchy, endlessly repeated loops | Repetition makes earworms that intrude on later silent work | Earworms carry the linked fantasy cues into silence | Earworm studies; inference |

Slowed + reverb deserves a specific note, because it can pass for "study music." Producers describe it as **"false nostalgia"**. Listeners report mourning "parties they never attended or slow drives through cities they never visited," and one creator explains its appeal this way: "People might be very bored or sad… They find their escape in slowed songs" ([Clash Magazine 2021](https://clashmusic.com/features/how-slowed-reverb-became-the-soundtrack-to-gen-z-lockdown)). That is close to a description of daydream fuel. Combined with the lab finding that sad music increases mind-wandering, this means the two ends of your playlist feed MD by different routes. The fast end supplies power fantasies and the urge to move; the slow end supplies nostalgic, emotional fantasy. Sped-up edits were engineered for capture. One social-media manager put it this way: "you get immediately sucked in… don't have the time to think about it." The hashtags #spedupsongs and #speedupsongs have drawn more than 11 billion and 12 billion views respectively ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/viral/tiktok-sped-up-fast-songs-sounds-rcna79256); [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/103077116)).

### Earworms and withdrawal keep the music running after it stops

About **90% of people get earworms at least weekly**, and about a third get them daily. Heavy listeners get them more often ([Liikkanen 2012](https://philpapers.org/rec/LIIMAP)). Recent and repeated exposure is the main trigger, and mind-wandering itself is another, so MD and earworms feed each other ([Williamson et al. 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224774285_How_do_earworms_start_Classifying_the_everyday_circumstances_of_Involuntary_Musical_Imagery)). Songs that become earworms tend to be **faster**, with simple, generic melodies ([Jakubowski et al. 2017](https://www.gold.ac.uk/news/scientists-find-key-to-writing-catchy-pop-hits/)). In one experiment, hearing a chorus up to four times on day one produced earworms in **68%** of people on day two. More exposure also meant more interference with a working-memory task on day two, both during the music and after it had stopped. Repetition raised the urge to sing along without raising enjoyment ([Killingly & Lacherez 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10585939/)). Chewing gum, which occupies the inner voice, reduced earworms in a lab test ([Beaman et al. 2015](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/40114/1/Gummed%20up%20earworms3.pdf)). Listening to music near bedtime is linked to sleep-disrupting earworms ([Scullin et al. 2021, Baylor](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2021/music-listening-near-bedtime-disruptive-sleep-baylor-study-finds)).

Expect withdrawal when you switch to silence. Across 11 studies, many people found just 6–15 minutes alone with their thoughts unpleasant. In one study, **12 of 18 men gave themselves an electric shock** rather than sit quietly, and in an at-home version 32% admitted "cheating" with music or their phone. Critics dispute whether thinking itself is aversive ([Wilson et al. 2014](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1250830)). MDers describe the absence of music as "like having an itch that you cannot scratch" ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). No study has measured how long it takes for silence to start feeling normal. The early discomfort is a predictable transition, not evidence that silence works worse. Your own sense of what helps is unreliable here, because people consistently believe their liked music helps even while it measurably impairs them ([Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)).

### Silence wins for thinking work, and the evidence agrees with your values

Across the main studies, **quiet matches or beats** lyrical music for reading, verbal memory and creativity ([Souza & Barbosa 2023](https://journalofcognition.org/articles/10.5334/joc.273); [Threadgold et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)). Steady noise is a weaker option. **White and pink noise** helped young people with ADHD or high inattention slightly (g = +0.249) but *hurt* people without ADHD slightly (g = −0.212), and the researchers flag a hearing risk if headphone volume isn't monitored ([Nigg et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890856724000741); [OHSU](https://news.ohsu.edu/2024/08/09/white-pink-noise-improve-focus-for-children-with-adhd-ohsu-study-shows)). **Brown noise** has no controlled studies. **Natural soundscapes** beat urban soundscapes on a directed-attention test, which suits recovery between work blocks ([Van Hedger et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-018-1539-1)). Steady sound lacks the melody, lyrics and emotional imagery that make music an MD trigger, so it makes a reasonable bridge while silence still feels uncomfortable. That MD-specific point is an inference.

| Task | Best | Acceptable bridge | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scriptwriting, outlining, research reading | Silence (earplugs or noise-cancelling headphones with nothing playing) | Low-volume pink noise, rain or stream sounds | Any lyrics; fast, loud beats; emotionally evocative audio |
| Choosing the hook or angle, AI prompting | Silence | Steady noise | Same as above |
| Loose brainstorming | Silence or a walk | Nature sounds | Audio that steers your imagery |
| Mechanical editing (cuts, syncing, export) | Silence or steady noise | Nature sounds | Music: the arousal benefit seen in general populations is outweighed by its role as an MD trigger |
| Breaks between blocks | A walk without audio | Nature sounds | Scrolling short audios, which reload earworms and fantasy cues |

The research has one finding you can use without music. A short burst of arousal *before* a task helps performance ([Thompson, Schellenberg & Husain 2001](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Arousal,-Mood,-and-The-Mozart-Effect-Thompson-Schellenberg/bae215c23ade46b72bc944257cbdc144e1c23b2e)). A brisk walk delivers arousal too: short bouts of exercise improve memory, with pooled effect sizes of 0.26 for short-term memory and 0.52 for long-term memory ([Loprinzi et al. 2019, citing Roig et al. 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6523402/)). No study tests whether vocal audio without instruments behaves like music in MD. Researchers locate music's pull in pleasure and emotional absorption ([Ferreri et al. 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/a5cjf_v1)), which argues for treating any emotionally evocative audio with the same caution during focused work. On every point where the evidence is clear, it points where your values already point: silence for focused work.

## Producing, not consuming, builds the skills your business needs

### Retrieval and spacing top a clear evidence ranking

A landmark review rated ten study techniques. It rated practice testing and spaced practice as high-utility, and rated rereading, highlighting and summarizing as low-utility, even though those are what most learners do ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/)). Later meta-analyses put numbers on the ranking:

| Rank | Technique | Best estimate | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spaced retrieval: testing yourself at intervals | g ≈ 0.74 vs. cramming the same practice into one session | [Latimier, Peyre & Ramus 2021](https://tipsforteachers.substack.com/p/research-bite-53-a-meta-analytic) |
| 2 | Retrieval practice: self-testing, producing from memory | g ≈ 0.50 in the lab and in 222 classroom studies | [Rowland 2014](https://courseware.epfl.ch/assets/courseware/v1/fdde2f0aa590bf3b1324077a6bf1540c/asset-v1%3AEPFL%2BDEMO%2B2020%2Btype%40asset%2Bblock/Rowland2014-meta-analysis.pdf); [Yang et al. 2021](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/testing-quizzing-boosts-classroom-learning-a-systematic-and-meta-/) |
| 3 | Spacing | Best gap ≈ 20–40% of how long you need to remember it for a 1-week horizon, ≈ 5–10% for 1 year | [Cepeda et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/) |
| 4 | Self-explanation | g ≈ 0.55, even after controlling for time on task | [Bisra et al. 2018](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/self-explanation-powerful-learning-technique-according-meta-analysis-64-studies) |
| 5 | Interleaving: mixing types instead of blocking them | g ≈ 0.42, highly variable; best for telling similar categories apart | [Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf) |
| 6 | Generation: producing the answer rather than reading it | d ≈ 0.40 | [Bertsch et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17645161/) |
| 7 | Worked examples for novices | Large early benefit that fades and can reverse as expertise grows | [Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/136) |
| Low | Rereading, highlighting, summarizing | Feel productive, weak results | [Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/) |

Matching instruction to "learning styles" has no support. The few properly designed tests contradicted it ([Pashler et al. 2008](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/)). For skills rather than facts, retrieval means producing from memory. That might be writing a hook without looking at the reference, or rebuilding a transition without the tutorial open, then comparing your version with the original. The worked-example finding sets the order: study complete expert scripts and edits early, then shift toward producing your own as you improve.

### Tutorials, polish and unrestricted AI manufacture an illusion of skill

How smoothly learning goes is a poor guide to how much you learn. Bjork's distinction explains why. Conditions that boost what you can recall *right now* (cramming, blocking, rereading) differ from those that build durable learning (spacing, interleaving, testing, generation). People who confuse the two come to prefer the worse conditions ([Bjork & Bjork 2011](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf)). The evidence is consistent. Learners rated cramming as more effective than interleaving even after their own test scores showed the opposite ([Kornell & Bjork 2008](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Kornell_Bjork_2008_PsychScience.pdf)). A fluent, polished lecturer raised students' confidence but not their scores ([Carpenter et al. 2013](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-instructor-fluency-on-students-perceptions-of-instr/)). Physics students learned more from active instruction but *felt* they learned less ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/gamelab/files/2023/01/Deslauriersa-et-al-2019.pdf)), and across 225 studies active learning raised exam scores by about 6%, with students in traditional lectures **1.5 times more likely to fail** ([Freeman et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4060683)). Most directly relevant to you: across 6 experiments with 2,225 people, repeatedly watching someone else perform a skill raised people's belief that they could do it **without improving their actual ability**, and only a brief hands-on attempt reduced the illusion. The press release was titled "watching how-to videos on YouTube gives people false sense of expertise" ([Kardas & O'Brien 2018](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/media-relations-and-communications/press-releases/easier-seen-than-done-watching-how-to-videos-on-youtube-gives-people-false-sense-of-expertise)).

AI has the same trap built in. In a field experiment with about 1,000 high-school math students, unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice performance by 48%. But once access was removed, those students scored **17% worse** than students who never had it. A tutor version that gave hints rather than answers largely removed the harm ([Bastani et al. 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)). In Anthropic's own trial with 52 engineers learning an unfamiliar library, the AI-assisted group averaged **50% on a follow-up quiz against 67%** for those who coded by hand. The engineers who kept their skills asked conceptual questions and fixed errors themselves rather than delegating the work ([Anthropic 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)). A survey of 319 knowledge workers found that more confidence in AI went with less critical thinking ([Lee et al. 2025](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=1135061)).

Learning design also controls mind-wandering, which matters most for you. In a 21-minute video lecture, brief tests after each segment cut mind-wandering from **about 40% to 19%** and raised final scores from 68% to 90% ([Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/)). Across 73 correlations, *momentary* mind-wandering during reading predicted poorer comprehension (r = −0.21), while people's general tendency to daydream barely predicted anything ([Bonifacci et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9971160/)). That means the way you set up a session can outweigh your daydreaming tendency.

Heavy short-form video use correlates with poorer attention (r = −0.38) and impulse control (r = −0.41) across 71 studies, though which causes which is not established ([Nguyen et al. 2025, via PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/)). Switching away from an *unfinished* task leaves "attention residue" that drags on the next one, and finishing creates closure ([Leroy 2009](https://strategy-business.com/article/re00088)).

The common thread is that watching, polish, fluency and AI-generated answers all deliver the *feeling* of progress, which is the same thing a success fantasy delivers.

### Practice hours explain only about 12% of skill differences

Deliberate practice, as Ericsson defined it, means focused work on specific weaknesses with feedback, designed by a skilled teacher. It is necessary, but it explains less than the "10,000 hours" story suggests. Across 88 studies, deliberate practice explained about **12%** of differences in performance:

| Domain | Share of performance differences explained by practice |
|---|---|
| Games | 26% |
| Music | 21% |
| Sports | 18% |
| Education | 4% |
| Professions | under 1% |

Source: [Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/becoming-an-expert-takes-more-than-practice.html). Among elite athletes, practice explained just 1% ([Macnamara, Moreau & Hambrick 2016](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/macnamara-et-al-2016-the-relationship-between-deliberate-practice-and-performance-in-sports-a-meta-analysis.pdf)). A double-blind repeat of the famous violinist study with 39 violinists did not confirm the original finding: the "good" group had logged *more* practice than the "best" group ([Macnamara & Maitra 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745)). Ericsson's side replies that the critics measured generic "structured practice." Their reanalysis of studies meeting the strict definition estimates 29%, rising to 61% after statistical correction ([Ericsson & Harwell 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02396/text)). Either way, starting knowledge, working memory, the quality of feedback and how predictable the domain is all matter alongside volume ([Hambrick et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4101876)). Your skills are profession-like, so better feedback loops and better mental models are your levers more than raw hours.

### Feedback helps on average but backfires in over a third of cases

Across 607 effect sizes, feedback improved performance by d = 0.41 on average, but **more than a third of feedback interventions made performance worse**. Feedback that pulls attention toward the self rather than the task tends to hurt ([Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Marking%20and%20Feedback/The%20effects%20of%20feedback%20interventions.pdf)). Good feedback answers three questions: "Where am I going? How am I going? Where to next?" Feedback about the person is the least effective kind ([Hattie & Timperley 2007](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/Hattie_Timperly_2007.pdf)). A 2020 meta-analysis of 435 studies found an average effect of d = 0.48, and the more information the feedback contained, the bigger the effect ([Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456)). For your work, retention graphs, click-through rates and a viewer's "here's where I lost interest" are information-rich task feedback. Comments praising or attacking *you* are weak or harmful as learning signals.

### Your skills range from kind to wicked, and each needs different practice

Gut instinct becomes reliable only where the environment is regular and gives fast, clear feedback ([Kahneman & Klein 2009](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/strategic-decisions-when-can-you-trust-your-gut)). Researchers call these "kind" environments. In "wicked" ones, feedback is delayed, incomplete or biased ([Hogarth, Lejarraga & Soyer 2015](https://portalinvestigacio.uib.es/documentos/67fbe1662308cf3c390f5aa7?lang=es)), and people "gain confidence without gaining skill" ([Epstein](https://davidepstein.substack.com/p/how-to-get-better-at-learning-from-experience)). No study classifies creator work this way, so the table below is reasoned inference:

| Skill | Environment | Core practice | Fastest feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical editing (cuts, audio, software) | Kind | Recreate expert edits shot for shot; spaced practice of shortcuts | Immediate visual and audio check |
| Creative editing (pacing, story) | Semi-kind | Deconstruct, then rebuild 60–90-second sequences; compare three editors side by side | Retention spikes and dips; critique from an experienced editor |
| AI prompting | Mostly kind | Prompt library with success criteria; test two or three variants on the same input | Instant output judged against your criteria |
| Scriptwriting and hooks | Semi-kind | Daily writing; rewrite top intros from memory; small reps on hooks | 30-second intro retention; watching readers react |
| Ideation and packaging | Semi-wicked | High-volume title and thumbnail reps; study outliers; log predictions | Test & Compare; peers ranking your ideas |
| Brand building | Wicked | Distinctive-asset audits; case studies of other brands | Long-horizon signals (inbound mentions, recall) |
| Business | Wicked | Written hypotheses and cheap tests; interviewing experts | Experiment log reviewed with a mentor |

Experts outperform others not because they have bigger working memories but because they have libraries of patterns. Chess masters recall real board positions far better than novices but not random ones ([Chase & Simon 1973, notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Chase_and_Simon_-_Perception_in_chess)). For you, those patterns are hook types, story structures, title formulas and transition styles. Build the library by labeling examples and practicing from the labels, not by watching passively. Experts also leave out roughly **70% of their decisions** when they explain how they work, so ask mentors "walk me through the last time you…" rather than "what's your advice?" ([Clark & Feldon, cognitive task analysis](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/itls_facpub/454)). Practice transfers mainly to closely similar tasks. Working-memory training transferred to similar tasks at g = 0.46 but to different ones at only g = 0.12 ([Sala & Gobet 2017](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3006842/)). So your practice should look like the real work: real scripts, real edits, real offers.

### Skill by skill: what the evidence says to practice

**Ideation.** Hit rates stay roughly constant per attempt, so more genuine attempts produce more hits. This is Simonton's "equal-odds rule," and in one study the number of responses correlated r = 0.73 with judged creativity ([Jung et al. 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4479710/)). Generate alone first. Groups brainstorming together produce fewer ideas than the same individuals working separately and then pooling ([Diehl & Stroebe 1987](https://homepages.se.edu/cvonbergen/files/2013/01/Productivity-Loss-In-Brainstorming_Toward-the-Solution-of-a-Riddle.pdf)). A "hybrid" process, solo first and then together, produced more and better ideas and was better at telling them apart ([Girotra, Terwiesch & Ulrich 2010](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/2010/idea-generation-and-the-quality-of-the-best-idea/)). Do not trust your own excitement about an idea. Among 339 circus professionals, creators predicted the success of **other people's** new acts better than managers did, but not their own ([Berg 2016](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/managers-are-not-best-judge-creative-ideas)). That is a direct warning for anyone prone to success fantasies: outsource selection to peers and to data. AI raises the floor but narrows the range. Writers given AI story ideas wrote better individually, but their stories became more alike collectively ([Doshi & Hauser 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)). Asking the model to reason step by step restored much of the diversity ([Meincke, Mollick & Terwiesch 2024](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4708466)). Creativity can be trained (g = 0.53 across 332 effect sizes), and training in *convergent* thinking, meaning selecting and evaluating, worked better than training in divergent brainstorming ([Haase, Hanel & Gronau 2023](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/34344/1/CreativityEnhancementMethods_accepted.pdf)). Moderate constraints help, and too many or too few hurt ([Acar, Tarakci & van Knippenberg 2019](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/20459/)).

**Scriptwriting.** Writing expertise develops in stages. It moves from "knowledge-telling" (writing down what you know) to "knowledge-crafting," where the writer holds the reader's moment-by-moment interpretation in mind. Professionals "compose for just a few hours per day at most, but on a highly consistent daily schedule," because binge writing breeds writer's block ([Kellogg 2008](https://jowr.org/jowr/article/download/759/823/694)). Instruction research favors explicit strategy instruction (effect size 0.82), help from peers (0.75) and specific goals for the finished product (0.70). Studying models helps, and watching real readers react to your own draft produced especially large gains ([Kellogg 2008](https://jowr.org/jowr/article/download/759/823/694); [Graham & Perin 2007 summary](https://www.adlit.org/topics/writing/summary-writing-next)). Benjamin Franklin's method still works as a drill. He reduced *Spectator* essays to short hints, waited days, rewrote them from the hints without looking, then compared his version with the original ([Franklin, Autobiography](https://www.ushistory.org/FRANKLIN/autobiography/page06.htm)). That combines spacing, retrieval, generation and feedback in one exercise. Hooks rest on solid theory: curiosity arises "when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge" ([Loewenstein 1994](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)). Specific retention rules, such as MrBeast's leaked memo saying "we freak out so much about the first minute," are practitioner heuristics ([MrBeast memo transcript](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). YouTube's own "Intro" metric, the share of viewers still watching at 30 seconds, gives you an objective check ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)).

**AI prompting.** Official guidance agrees on the basics: be explicit, explain *why*, give 3–5 diverse examples, structure the input, and define "clear… success criteria" with "ways to empirically test against those criteria" before tuning a prompt ([Anthropic prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices); [OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-engineering)). The field evidence shows that prompting skill rests on domain skill. On tasks within AI's capabilities, 758 BCG consultants completed 12.2% more tasks, 25.1% faster and at more than 40% higher quality, but on a task deliberately chosen to be beyond those capabilities, AI users were **19 percentage points less likely** to get it right ([Dell'Acqua et al. 2023](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf)). ChatGPT cut professional writing time by 40% and raised quality by 18% ([Noy & Zhang 2023](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1_0.pdf)), and novice customer-support agents gained 34% ([Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond](https://nber.org/papers/w31161)). Among 640 Kenyan entrepreneurs with an AI business mentor, however, high performers gained about 15% while low performers did **8% worse**, partly because they asked it harder questions ([Otis et al.](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/centers/cid/publications/uneven-impact-generative-ai-entrepreneurial-performance)). The rule that follows: when the goal is learning, attempt first and then use AI to critique and explain; when the goal is output, delegate only what you could judge yourself.

**Creative editing.** Good continuity editing works by managing viewer attention. Viewers asked to spot edits missed about a quarter of cuts, and a third when the cut coincided with sudden motion ([Smith & Henderson 2008](https://doaj.org/article/f74db40d8a69447fabf983ae63e788a3); [Smith 2012](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/)). Shot lengths in Hollywood films have converged toward a rhythm that resembles fluctuations in human attention, so pacing should vary in waves rather than run at a uniform jump-cut cadence ([Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer 2010](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/02/100223121435.htm)). Taste is partly a matter of learning to tell styles apart. Mixing examples of different artists' styles taught those styles better than studying them one at a time ([Kornell & Bjork 2008](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Kornell_Bjork_2008_PsychScience.pdf)). So study three editors side by side. Walter Murch's "Rule of Six," which ranks emotion, then story, then rhythm, and Ira Glass's "taste gap," which only "a volume of work" closes, are practitioner wisdom rather than measured findings ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2016/11/6-rules-good-cutting-according-oscar-winning-editor-walter-murch); [Writers' HQ](https://writershq.co.uk/the-taste-gap-by-ira-glass/)).

**Brand building.** Marketing science says brands grow mainly by reaching more buyers and being easy to notice, recall and buy ("mental and physical availability") ([Ehrenberg-Bass Institute](https://marketingscience.info/how-do-you-measure-how-brands-grow/)). Distinctive assets, such as consistent colors, phrases and visual motifs, need both fame and uniqueness ([Romaniuk, via Marketing Architects](https://www.marketingarchitects.com/blog/v41-n3-your-brand-assets-may-be-working-against-you)). On how to split effort, analysis of 996 advertising case studies suggests roughly 60:40 between long-term brand building and short-term activation ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it)), or about 46:54 for selling to businesses ([The CMA](https://thecma.ca/topic/articles/2023/08/04/the-long-the-short-and-the-reality-of-b2b-marketing-part-1)). Byron Sharp calls the 60:40 ratio "terrible, very misleading" because it comes from award submissions ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/prof-byron-sharp-skewers-binet-tells-marketers-to-sack-agencies-preaching-share-of-voice)). For a personal brand, three research findings matter most: repeated exposure increases liking (r ≈ 0.26 across 208 experiments) ([Mere-exposure effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mere-exposure_effect)), similarity drives attraction ([Montoya & Horton 2013](https://researchportal.murdoch.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-meta-analytic-investigation-of-the-processes/991005635870007891)), and for influencers, credibility, trust, perceived similarity and parasocial connection best predict whether audiences act ([Han & Balabanis 2023](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/101563)). No study tests any of these specifically for solo creators, so treat the brand ratios and laws as starting hypotheses to test.

**Business.** In a randomized trial with Italian startups, founders taught to state theories, form hypotheses and run rigorous tests earned more revenue and pivoted more ([Camuffo et al. 2020](https://www.unibocconi.it/en/news/entrepreneurs-who-think-scientists-get-better-results)). Generic business training shows "relatively modest" effects ([McKenzie & Woodruff 2014](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wbk/wbrwps/6202.html)). So keep a written experiment log rather than collecting courses, and use human mentors for hard strategic calls.

### Imagination becomes an asset only inside a container

Your imagination is not the enemy. Singer's "positive-constructive" daydreaming supports planning and creative incubation ([McMillan, Kaufman & Singer 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00626/full)), and about **20% of the most important ideas** of 45 physicists and 53 professional writers arrived while they were thinking about something else ([Gable, Hopper & Schooler 2019](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/schooler/jonathan/node/1576)). But incubation has conditions. In a 12-minute incubation study, only the group that did an undemanding task improved, and **only on problems they had already worked on** ([Baird et al. 2012](https://www.cmhp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2018-12/Baird%20et%20al.%20%282012%29%20Inspired%20by%20distraction.pdf)). Across 117 studies, incubation effects were modest (d = 0.29) and grew with longer preparation ([Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19210055/)). Moderately engaging activities like showering or walking worked better than boring ones ([Irving et al. 2022](https://as.virginia.edu/science-based-shower-routine-could-boost-creativity)). Students who spent five minutes a day imagining the *process* of studying studied more and scored higher, and later research favors process simulation over imagining the outcome ([Pham & Taylor 1999](https://wissensdialoge.de/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/wissensblitz_59_planung_und_mentalesimulation.pdf)). And vividness is not originality: immersive daydreaming was *negatively* associated with originality in divergent thinking ([West, Somer & Eigsti 2023](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/immersive-and-maladaptive-daydreaming-and-divergent-thinking-in-a-2/)).

Taken together, the line between incubation and escapism has five features: **a loaded problem first** (you have already worked on something specific), **a bounded, low-demand activity** (a walk, not hours in bed), **capture** by voice memo or notebook, **a return to the work** the same day, and **content about process and obstacles** rather than idealized outcomes. A useful redirect is to imagine scenes from the *next video* (the cold open, a thumbnail composition, the obstacle you'll hit in the edit) rather than the life after success. No study has tested whether channeling MD into creative work reduces MD. The risk is that an "ideation slot" becomes a sanctioned gateway into immersion, so cap it, write rather than just imagine, and track whether your total fantasy time goes down.

## A graded protocol for the seven moments that decide your day

Every recommendation below carries a grade. **A** means a randomized trial in MD, or robust meta-analytic evidence on the exact mechanism. **B** means randomized trials or meta-analyses in related problems (procrastination, addiction, insomnia, goal pursuit), applied by analogy. **C** means qualitative MD studies, case reports, expert models, or small or secondary studies. **D** means reasoned inference that nobody has tested.

The backbone comes from the only MD treatment trial. It randomized **557 people** to an eight-session online program. The full version combined psychoeducation, motivational work, mindfulness and **daily self-monitoring**; a partial version omitted the self-monitoring; and a wait-list group used support forums while trying to cut back on their own. Both program groups improved with large effects on MD severity, frequency and functioning (η² = .24, .22 and .15). The wait-list group did not improve. In the full program, **24% improved to a clinically significant degree and 39% reliably**, and the gains held at six months. Self-monitoring sped up improvement in the short term ([Herscu, Somer, Federman & Soffer-Dudek 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/); [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05235243)). About 37% of those randomized did not finish, the trial was self-guided, and there was no placebo control. Willpower plus a forum is the one approach this trial shows does not work.

### (a) When the work gets uncomfortable and the urge hits

**Expect the moment, and reframe it.** The discomfort that triggers the urge is the same discomfort that signals learning, because effortful practice feels worse and works better ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/gamelab/files/2023/01/Deslauriersa-et-al-2019.pdf)). (Grade B for the learning claim; D for using it as a reframe against MD.)

**Write one if-then plan for this exact moment and rehearse it aloud daily for two weeks.** For example: "If I notice the urge to drift when the script or edit gets hard, then I say 'urge,' take three breaths, and do the next two-minute step: one rough sentence or one cut." Implementation intentions improved goal attainment by d = 0.65 across 94 tests ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)), and across 642 tests the effects were larger with a strict if-then format, high motivation and rehearsal ([Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer 2024](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905)). (Grade B.)

**Respond in three steps: notice, name, return.** This non-judgmental awareness skill is the core of the MD trial (grade A), and a separate two-week mindfulness course reduced distracting thoughts and improved test performance ([Mrazek et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23538911/)) (grade B). Don't try to force the thought away, because suppressed thoughts rebound across 31 studies ([Wang, Hagger & Chatzisarantis 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286932/)) (grade B). Don't berate yourself after a lapse either; MDers already show high harsh self-judgment ([Pourmoazzen et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111966/)) (grade C).

**Log every episode** with its start time, trigger, minutes lost and what you did. This is the component that sped up gains in the trial. (Grade A.)

**Make the task less aversive.** Break it into micro-steps and finish one unit before switching, which reduces attention residue ([Leroy 2009](https://strategy-business.com/article/re00088)) (grade C). Take breaks when you need them rather than on a rigid timer, since a fixed 25/5 Pomodoro schedule raised fatigue faster without improving output ([Smits et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12292963/)) (grade B). Decide in advance on a break activity that is not a trigger: no music, no lying down (grade D).

**If pacing is part of your ritual, use a competing response.** Notice the first sign (reaching for earbuds, standing up to pace), then hold an incompatible posture for about a minute: seated, feet flat, hands on the keyboard. This borrows from habit reversal training, which reduced repetitive behaviors with d = 0.80 ([Bate et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21549664/)). (Grade B by analogy.)

### (b) When your hand reaches for music

**Make music unavailable, not just forbidden.** Somer recommends stimulus control for music cues ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)). In practice that means no music during work blocks, streaming apps blocked during those hours, earbuds out of reach and the phone in another room. Somer's six-month treatment of a 25-year-old man included confining recreational internet to one scheduled late-evening hour; his internet time fell by more than 70%, his daydreaming time by more than 50%, and his work and social functioning improved by more than 70% ([Somer 2018](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/maladaptive-daydreaming-ontological-analysis-treatment-rationale-/)). (Grade C.)

**Default to silence**, using noise-cancelling headphones with nothing playing if the room is loud (grade B for verbal work). **If silence feels unbearable, use low-volume pink noise or rain sounds as a bridge and phase them out**, because noise slightly hurts people without ADHD ([Nigg et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890856724000741)) (grade B for the general effect; D as an MD bridge). **Expect the "itch," and earworms, for days to weeks**, since no study gives a timeline (grade C), and **chew gum during writing sessions to quiet earworms** ([Beaman et al. 2015](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/40114/1/Gummed%20up%20earworms3.pdf)) (grade C, from a single experiment).

**Replace the pre-work "hype" track with a five-to-ten-minute brisk walk without audio**, which gives you arousal without the cue (grade B for memory benefits; D for MD). **Run your own two-week test**: compare words written or minutes on task against your old routine, because your sense of what helps is unreliable ([Perham & Currie 2014](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/music/distraction/2014-perham.pdf)) (grade D, but cheap). Finally, **use the fact that your values and the evidence agree**. Motivational interviewing, which the trial and Somer's case both used, works by tying change to what the person already cares about (grade C).

### (c) After something exciting happens

**Pre-commit to an if-then for good news.** For example: "If I get exciting news, then I write one line about it, add a short dhikr or word of thanks if that fits me, name the next concrete action and either do it or schedule it, then walk five minutes without audio." Excitement as a trigger is unproven (grade D), but the if-then format is well supported (grade B).

**Convert the excitement into a process step at once.** Positive fantasies sap more energy when the need is more pressing ([Kappes & Oettingen 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284)), and imagining the *process* of doing the work improved effort and results ([Pham & Taylor 1999](https://wissensdialoge.de/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/wissensblitz_59_planung_und_mentalesimulation.pdf)). (Grade B.)

**Never celebrate with music plus fantasy.** That combination is the most amplified state the evidence describes, because heroic music makes thought empowering ([Koelsch et al. 2019](https://stefan-koelsch.de/papers/koelsch_bashevkin_kristensen_tvedt_jentschke_2019_heroic_music_stimulates_empowering_thoughts_during_mind-wandering_scientific_reports_91_10317.pdf)) and pleasure in music drives daydreaming ([Ferreri et al. 2026, preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/a5cjf_v1)). (Grade C.)

### (d) When a success fantasy starts

**Give the fantasy a daily, five-minute, written home: WOOP.** Write the **Wish** (for example, a channel or business milestone), imagine the best **Outcome** for one minute only, name the *inner* **Obstacle** ("when editing gets boring I escape into fantasy"), and finish with an if-then **Plan**. MCII improved goal attainment with g = 0.336, and with g = 0.465 when done with another person, so reviewing it weekly with an accountability partner or coach adds value ([Wang, Wang & Gai 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)). (Grade B.)

**Stop using visualization as motivation.** Success visualization and "manifesting" do not work as a productivity ritual: positive fantasies predict fewer applications, lower pay and later depression ([Oettingen, Mayer & Portnow 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26825106/)). (Grade B.)

**Read the fantasy's theme as data.** Fame, recognition and competence fantasies point to needs that real life is not meeting yet, and clinicians use these themes to "tend to needs within real life" ([Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)). The real-world dose is small, shipped work that gets real feedback from peers and viewers. (Grade C.)

**When a fantasy starts outside the WOOP slot, close it deliberately.** Write one line on where the episode stops, and leave nothing "to be continued" (grade D). Somer's patient intercepted plots early and gave them aversive endings, but that was one case, and paired with self-shaming statements it risks backfiring in people already prone to self-judgment. Neutral interruption fits the trial's non-judgmental approach better (grade C).

### (e) Before sleep and in idle time

**Keep the bed for sleep only.** Phones and earbuds stay out of the bedroom, and you get up if you're still awake after roughly 20 minutes. Stimulus control carries a conditional recommendation from sleep medicine, and multicomponent CBT for insomnia carries a strong one if insomnia develops ([AASM guideline, Edinger et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/)). (Grade B.)

**Write tomorrow's to-do list one to two hours before bed.** People who wrote a specific to-do list fell asleep about nine minutes faster in a small lab study ([TIME on Scullin](https://time.com/5097840/how-to-fall-asleep-faster)), and the habit mirrors the next-day schedule in Somer's case. (Grade C.)

**In bed, use a 15-minute body scan.** It shortened time to fall asleep for slow sleepers in a trial of 232 adolescents ([Bartel et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29441644/)) (grade B). The "cognitive shuffle," picturing random neutral images, has only weak, secondary support ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-innovator-next-door/202504/a-creatively-random-idea-for-falling-asleep)) (grade C). **Keep music away from bedtime**, which is linked to sleep-disrupting earworms ([Scullin et al. 2021](https://news.web.baylor.edu/news/story/2021/music-listening-near-bedtime-disruptive-sleep-baylor-study-finds)) (grade C).

**Protect sleep for the sake of learning.** Bedtime MD did not predict worse sleep the next night in diary data, but one night of sleep deprivation cut the ability to form new memories by about **40%** ([Yoo et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17293859/)), and poor sleep raised next-day mind-wandering ([Marcusson-Clavertz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6880993/)).

**Pre-plan defaults for idle time**: a walk without audio, dhikr, a pre-chosen two-minute task such as reviewing your idea inbox, or talking to someone. Unstructured isolation raised MD urges during lockdown ([Somer et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667241/)). (Grade C.) **Be careful with a scheduled "daydream window."** No study has tested one; if you try it, cap it and check your log to see whether total fantasy time falls. (Grade D.)

### (f) A daily and weekly learning-and-practice system

The system turns the learning evidence into a fixed routine. Make output, not hours or felt progress, the unit you track. Plan on a two-to-three-month ramp, because forming a new daily habit took a median of **66 days** (range 18–254) ([Lally et al. 2010](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit)). Set specific, hard goals rather than "do your best" ([Locke & Latham 2002](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20%282002%29.pdf)). Keep progress visible, because making progress was the single strongest feature of people's best workdays ([Amabile & Kramer](https://hbswk.hbs.edu/item/6685.html)).

| When | Block | What you do | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily, morning | 10 minutes mindfulness, then read the day's plan | Mindfulness practice; plan reviewed several times a day as in Somer's case | A (MD trial), C |
| Daily, same time | 60–120-minute creation block in silence | Script or edit with one defined deliverable (for example, a finished hook set); phone in another room | B (spaced practice, Kellogg), C |
| Daily | 20–50 raw ideas or 20 titles, generated alone and fast; selection in a separate session | Quantity first, then judging against explicit criteria | B |
| Daily, when learning | Input with built-in retrieval | No tutorial segment over 5–10 minutes without stopping to reproduce it; AI as tutor, not answer machine | B |
| Daily, evening | MD log plus tomorrow's plan | Minutes lost, triggers, if-then plan refined | A |
| Weekly | Copywork in Franklin's style | Reduce one top intro to hints, wait 2–3 days, rewrite it, compare | B/C |
| Weekly | Edit deconstruction | Log every cut in a 60–90-second segment, rebuild it, compare three editors side by side | B/C |
| Weekly | Peer exchange | Swap title and hook lists with 1–3 creators and rank each other's | B |
| Weekly | Prediction log and Test & Compare | Predict the click-through and retention range before publishing, then compare | C |
| Weekly | One rep with no AI | One script section written unaided, to check the skill isn't fading | C |
| 2–3 times a week | Incubation walk | Load a specific problem, walk 10–20 minutes without audio, capture ideas, use one that day | B |
| Weekly | WOOP with a partner | Review the week's inner obstacles | B |
| Monthly | Business experiment review and brand-asset audit | Hypothesis, test, metric, decision; check that distinctive assets are consistent | B/C |

### (g) When to see a professional, and what to ask for

**Seek help urgently** if you have any thoughts of suicide or self-harm. MD carries elevated risk at the severe end ([Soffer-Dudek & Oh 2024](https://doaj.org/article/f6e4a128e4294d68a93f1cf34023254e)).

**Seek a professional assessment** if you have low mood or loss of interest lasting two weeks or more, intrusive thoughts or rituals that suggest OCD, lifelong inattention or disorganization that predates the daydreaming (possible ADHD), episodes of losing track of reality or confusing fantasy with real memories, or a history of trauma. Do the same if MD is still costing you deadlines or income after four to eight weeks of the structured self-help above, a window that matches the trial's eight weeks. These red flags come from the comorbidity data. In interviewed MDers, inattentive ADHD, anxiety, depression and OCD-related disorders each ran above 50% ([Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28598955)). One documented case confused vivid daydreams with real memories ([Somer 2024 case](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022530/)).

**Who to look for.** A clinical psychologist or CBT therapist, ideally one with experience in dissociation or behavioral addictions. Add a psychiatrist if medication for a co-occurring condition is being considered.

**What to ask for.** Ask for an **assessment** with the MDS-16 and, if available, the SCIMD interview, and for **screening** for depression, anxiety, OCD, ADHD and dissociation that explicitly checks MD against ADHD, because the MDS-16 is poor at telling them apart (specificity 44.7%) ([Theodor-Katz & Soffer-Dudek 2025](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-international-journal-clinical-health-psychology-355-articulo-differential-diagnosis-between-maladaptive-daydreaming-S1697260025000730)). Ask for **targeted treatment**, meaning CBT with mindfulness, self-monitoring and motivational work rather than generic supportive talk; the position paper's summary is that "non-specific therapy is ineffective, targeted treatment seems promising" ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12038384/)). **Bring the papers**, because many clinicians don't know the term: the trial, the position paper and the CBT formulation model ([Lucas & Bone 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-cognitive-behaviour-therapist/article/introducing-a-psychological-formulation-model-of-maladaptive-daydreaming/883AC5B2DFE48DD252A2FA2EB6B82EE9)). If it matters to you, look for **a clinician comfortable with faith-consistent goals** such as cutting music; motivational, values-based work fits this naturally, though no MD-specific data exist on it.

**What targeted treatment has looked like.** In a 24-year-old man, a tailored cognitive program covered coping skills, behavior change, restructuring of beliefs and relapse prevention, and his MDS-16 score fell from 66 to 32, below the cut-off ([Shanbhag & Pothiyil 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39545109/)).

**What medication can and cannot do.** No medication has trial evidence for MD itself. Most sufferers report little effect from medication, and survey authors tentatively favor antidepressants and advise against cannabis ([Ross, West & Somer 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31688494/)). Stimulants were reported to do little for MD and sometimes to worsen it ([Theodor-Katz et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9790222/)). Medication matters mainly for co-occurring depression, OCD or ADHD.

## Conclusion

Every trap in this report sells the same product: the feeling of progress without the progress. A success fantasy "mentally consummates" the goal and drains the energy to pursue it. A tutorial makes you feel able without making you able. A polished lecture feels like learning while teaching less. Unrestricted AI raises practice scores while independent skill falls. A soundtrack makes the fantasy richer and the exit harder. That is why the fixes converge: measure progress in shipped output (a hook written, an edit rebuilt, a test run), never in how motivated, inspired or knowledgeable you feel. It is also why the moment work gets uncomfortable deserves so much attention. That is when MD fires, and it is also when effortful practice is building skill. Learning to stay in that moment for two more minutes protects your focus and builds your craft at the same time.

The evidence also changes what "fixing" MD should mean for you. Total abstinence from imagination is neither realistic nor supported; suppression rebounds, and vivid imagination is a risk factor, not the disease. The realistic goal is to cut the rituals that turn imagination into compulsion (music, solitude, the bed) and to give the imagination a container: written, time-boxed, obstacle-aware and ending in action. Your religious conviction about music happens to remove the single strongest amplifier the research identifies. Change grounded in your own values tends to last longer than change grounded in willpower. The biggest open question is not whether this approach is sensible but how fast it works for a young man whose MD is tied to work rather than to trauma or loneliness, a group the research has barely studied. Your daily log is the best data you will get on that question.

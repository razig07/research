# Pray to Return, Not to Escape

The Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah treat a wandering mind as normal and prescribe a precise response: remove the distracting cue, settle the body, do not hold unbidden thoughts against yourself, meet the pull with a brief act of remembrance, and go back to the task. Nearly every step matches psychology he already uses, from stimulus control and notice–name–return to if-then plans, WOOP and habit formation. Science has not tested any of it for focus, though. There is **no controlled trial of salah and attention**, and the dhikr studies are small, unblinded anxiety studies in hospital patients. In head-to-head trials Qur'an listening and music calmed patients **about equally**, and **no study links prayer, dhikr or religiosity to maladaptive daydreaming (MD)**. Realistically, these practices can give his day five if-then anchors, a ready replacement act for urges and idle time, a set script for bedtime (his worst window) and a guilt-free way to read his own drifting. They cannot cure MD, guarantee focus, or replace mindfulness-style attention training plus daily self-monitoring, the only combination with randomised support in MD. Two risks need guarding. OCD-related disorders affected **more than half of MDers diagnosed by interview**, so dhikr or wudu repeated "until it feels right" can become a compulsion. And worship used to flee uncomfortable work does the fantasy's job. The test for every act is whether it returns him to the task or replaces it. Recitation and nasheeds without instruments drop the beat and status themes that made his music an amplifier. But they are untested substitutes that can inherit the old ritual, and any speech he understands makes scripting harder, so silence stays the default for verbal work.

## Science has measured calm in patients, never focus or MD

Every scientific claim below carries the grade from his existing course: **A** for a randomised trial in MD or robust meta-analytic evidence on the exact mechanism, **B** for trials or meta-analyses in related problems or general samples applied by analogy, **C** for small, uncontrolled, correlational or qualitative evidence, and **D** for reasoned inference nobody has tested. Texts are graded by source instead: the Qur'an, Bukhari or Muslim, or another collection with its named grader's verdict.

The Qur'an presents focus in prayer as both a goal and a struggle. The successful believers are "during their prayer humbly intent" ([Qur'an 23:1–2](https://quran.com/23/1-2)), and prayer "is difficult except for the humbly submissive," which Ibn Kathir reads as prayer being "heavy and burdensome" for others ([Ibn Kathir on 2:45](https://quran.com/2:45/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). This report's searches found no randomised trial, controlled trial or meta-analysis of salah and attention, anxiety or mind-wandering, and no study measuring attention with a standard task before and after prayer. The evidence is correlational: in 174 Pakistani students, regular, attentive prayer went with better self-reported mental health, with religious education, prayer education and attentive prayer jointly explaining **13% of the variance** on scales the authors built themselves ([Ijaz et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28502025/)) (C). The direction is unknown, since attention problems may make focused prayer harder, so struggling in salah is no evidence of weak faith. A 2023 khushu' scale found four factors that correlate with well-being: humility, focused attention, contemplation and control of how one prays ([Aldahadha 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36943556/)) (C). Nobody has measured mind-wandering during salah or what predicts it.

Dhikr shows the same gap. The Qur'an says "by the remembrance of Allāh hearts are assured," which Ibn Kathir explains as hearts finding comfort and tranquillity when He is remembered ([Ibn Kathir on 13:28](https://api.quran.com/api/v4/tafsirs/169/by_ayah/13:28)). Science has tested only short-term anxiety in patients, in three studies. One was a non-randomised study of 40 cancer patients that did not name the phrases used ([Sulistyawati et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31572762/)), and another an uncontrolled study of 34 cardiac patients ([Wilandika et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42240339/)). The third, a randomised trial in haemodialysis patients, bundled dhikr with salawat, so dhikr's own effect cannot be separated ([Wijayanti et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40632388/)). None of 45 PubMed records on dhikr measured rumination, attention, mind-wandering or daydreaming ([PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=dhikr+OR+zikr+OR+zikir+OR+%22remembrance+of+God%22+OR+%22remembrance+of+Allah%22+OR+istighfar+OR+tasbih)). So "dhikr eases acute anxiety" rates C at best, and "dhikr improves focus" has no evidence either way.

Qur'an listening has the biggest literature and the same limits. The texts link recitation with tranquillity, as when sakina descended on a man reciting Surat al-Kahf ([Bukhari 5011](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5011)). The trials, though, measured only momentary anxiety in patients awaiting surgery, chemotherapy or scans. The main systematic review found that all 28 of its studies were Iranian and none reported blinding ([Ghiasi & Keramat 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6178573/)). The one meta-analysis reports "d = −8.893," almost certainly a raw score difference mislabelled as a standardised effect ([Zarea Gavgani et al. 2022](https://relbib.de/Record/1815281626/Details)). In randomised head-to-head trials, recitation and music cut anxiety **equally** in 238 cancer patients ([Al-Jubouri et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587831/)), and murattal (measured recitation) and classical music cut post-surgical pain equally ([Prihatno et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41229901/)). Much of the short-term calm therefore looks like ordinary relaxation and expectation. Whether devotion adds more has not been tested, and no study has measured attention during listening or reciting (C for short-term calm; nothing on focus).

Biology adds little. Small lab studies without proper controls report changes in brain waves (EEG) and heart-rate variability during salah ([Doufesh et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24827587/)). One brain-imaging (fMRI) study of 23 adults found that silently repeating a single word quietened the brain network tied to self-focused thought and reduced spontaneous thinking. That is the only hint of how a repeated phrase might compete with a daydream ([Berkovich-Ohana et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26221571/)) (C).

The only hifz study with matched controls found that huffaz learned new material no better than controls: memorising built a specific skill, not a bigger memory ([Black et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32870071/)) (C). In a lab study of eight men, waking for Fajr and going back to sleep did no harm as long as total sleep was kept ([BaHammam et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22347349/)) (C). PubMed holds no study relating religiosity or prayer to MD ([PubMed search](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=maladaptive+daydreaming+religiosity)). The only tested MD treatment is an online programme of mindfulness training and daily self-monitoring, which beat a wait-list with large effects in 557 randomised people ([Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)) (A).

## Seven texts match the psychology he already uses

The Islamic material follows this report's source rules. The Qur'an is quoted in Sahih International with Tafsir Ibn Kathir, and Bukhari and Muslim as they are. Other hadith appear only with a named grading that the major graders do not notably dispute, which removes several narrations popular on these themes. Salaf sayings appear only with their source ("as recorded by Ibn Kathir" for those in his tafsir), and commentary comes only from Ibn Kathir, al-Nawawi and Ibn Hajar. Sunnah.com blocked automated access, so texts and gradings were checked against an open mirror of its data; spot-check the links before publication. A parallel shows that a text and a finding agree, not that science proves the text, and it does not reduce the unseen, such as Shaytan's whispering, to psychology.

### Wishing that feels like progress drains the effort it imitates

The Qur'an calls empty wishing *amani*. "It is not [obtained] by your wishful thinking," says 4:123. Ibn Kathir reports from Qatadah that the verse was revealed when Muslims and the People of the Book were boasting about their prophets. His own conclusion is that "salvation is not reached by mere wishing; what counts is obeying Allah" ([Ibn Kathir on 4:123](https://quran.com/4:123/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir)). On Shaytan's vow in 4:119, he spells out what those *amani* contain: making it attractive to abandon repentance, promising wishes, and ordering **taswif, procrastination and delay** ([Ibn Kathir on 4:119](https://quran.com/4:119/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir)).

These verses concern salvation, not careers. So the link to success daydreams lies in the structure, a wish standing in for a deed with delay to follow. It is not a claim that every daydream comes from Shaytan. Oettingen found that structure in experiments: positive fantasies predict lower effort and poorer results, while positive expectations predict more ([Oettingen & Mayer 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1198)). Fantasising lowers physical energy and lets people "mentally consummate" the goal ([Kappes & Oettingen 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284)) (B).

Wanting good things is not the problem. Qur'an 4:32 says "ask Allah of His bounty." Ibn Kathir reconciles the verse's ban on coveting another person's blessing with the permitted envy of Bukhari 7232, which is worded as a commitment to act: "if I were given the like of what he was given, I would do as he does" ([Ibn Kathir on 4:32](https://quran.com/4:32/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir); [Bukhari 7232](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:7232)). "I wish I had his channel" is a fantasy. "If I had his skill I would post weekly, so I practise hooks today" is a plan.

### "If only" opens a door that "do not be helpless" closes

Muslim 2664 is the Sunnah's most compact text on self-regulation: "Be keen on what benefits you, seek Allah's help, and do not be helpless. If something befalls you, do not say 'if only I had done such-and-such…' but say 'Allah decreed, and what He willed He did,' for 'if' opens [the door to] the work of Shaytan" ([Muslim 2664](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2664)). The Prophet ﷺ himself used "if" about the past, so al-Nawawi concludes that the prohibition covers saying it "where there is no benefit" ([al-Nawawi on Muslim 2664](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2664)).

Psychology draws the same line: "if only" thoughts correlate with depressive symptoms (r = .26 across 42 effect sizes; [Broomhall et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28501706/)), yet help when they yield a specific intention that changes behaviour ([Epstude & Roese 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18453477/)) (C). The hadith also reads as WOOP clause by clause: "be keen on what benefits you" is the wish, "do not be helpless" names the inner obstacle (the moment discomfort makes escape attractive), and "seek Allah's help" followed by action is the plan. Its word for helplessness, *'ajz*, is the one the Prophet ﷺ sought refuge from alongside worry, grief and laziness ([Bukhari 6363](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6363)). Read with the *amani* verses, the texts close both exits of the wandering mind, fantasy forward and regret backward, and leave the present task.

### Free time is where "many people are cheated"

"There are two blessings that many people are deceived into losing: health and free time" ([Bukhari 6412](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6412)). Ibn Hajar notes that "many people" shows "those granted success in this are few" ([Ibn Hajar on Bukhari 6412](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:6412)). That makes his struggle with idle time the common human failure, not a personal defect.

Another text names both postures of his worst windows: whoever "sits at a place where he does not remember Allah," or "lies at a place where he does not remember Allah," has deprivation descend on him from Allah ([Abu Dawud 4856](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4856); al-Albani: hasan sahih; Zubair 'Ali Za'i: hasan). Qur'an 94:7, "So when you have finished [your duties], then stand up [for worship]," has the very shape of an if-then plan. Ibn Kathir reads it as finishing worldly tasks, then standing for worship "energetically, with an unoccupied mind" ([Ibn Kathir on 94:7](https://quran.com/94:7/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir)).

The science agrees on the danger zone. Unstructured isolation in lockdown raised MDers' fantasy time and urges ([Somer et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667241/)) (C), and if-then plans improved goal attainment with d = .65 across 94 tests ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601%2806%2938002-1)) (B). Ibn 'Umar's saying in Sahih al-Bukhari turns him back to the present: "When evening comes, do not wait for the morning… take from your health for your sickness" ([Bukhari 6416](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6416)).

### A little that lasts beats a lot that stops

"The most beloved of deeds to Allah is the most consistent, even if small" ([Bukhari 6464](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6464)). Ibn Hajar reads the companion command, "do what you are able to," as "occupy yourselves with deeds you are able to keep up consistently," which forbids taking on what one cannot bear ([Ibn Hajar on Bukhari 43](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:43)). Al-Nawawi gives the payoff: "the continuous little bears fruit until it exceeds the interrupted much many times over" ([al-Nawawi on Muslim 782](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:782a)). When pain made the Prophet ﷺ miss his night prayer, he prayed twelve rak'ahs by day ([Muslim 746](https://sunnah.com/muslim:746)), a defined make-up of the kind a relapse plan provides.

Habit research agrees. In 96 volunteers, a daily behaviour done in a fixed context took 18–254 days to become automatic, and one missed day "did not materially affect" the process ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)) (C). A registered replication, though, appears to find that misses matter ([replication preprint](https://rr.peercommunityin.org/download/t_recommendations.reply_pdf.8382af6b73bdd0f7.6d616e757363726970745f7072657072696e742e706466.pdf)). The reverse chain runs from an ambitious start to aversion to quitting. Ibn Hajar says it overtakes whoever goes to extremes in religious deeds and abandons gentleness ([Ibn Hajar on Bukhari 39](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:39)). Ibn Kathir records 'Ikrimah and Sa'id ibn Jubayr likening the one who "gave a little and then refrained" to well-diggers who hit rock and say "we are finished" ([Ibn Kathir on 53:33ff](https://quran.com/53:39/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). MDers score higher on socially prescribed perfectionism ([Pourmoazzen et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42051277/)) (C), which makes that start-hard-then-quit cycle a live risk.

### Seek refuge, then occupy yourself with something else

The *waswas* (whispering) narrations say plainly that thoughts are not deeds: "Allah has pardoned my ummah for what their souls tell them, as long as they do not act or speak" ([Bukhari 5269](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5269); [Muslim 127](https://sunnah.com/muslim:127)). Some Companions were horrified by thoughts "too grave to speak of," and the Prophet ﷺ called that "clear faith" ([Muslim 132](https://sunnah.com/muslim:132)). Al-Nawawi explains that fearing such thoughts "only happens in someone whose faith is truly complete" ([al-Nawawi on Muslim 132](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:132a)). The response is brief: "seek refuge in Allah and desist" ([Bukhari 3276](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3276)). Ibn Hajar glosses this as stopping "going along with it" and repelling the thought "by occupying himself with something else" ([Ibn Hajar on Bukhari 3276](https://hadithunlocked.com/bukhari:3276)). Ibn 'Abbas, told of such a thought, laughed, said no one escapes it, and gave a verse to recite instead ([Abu Dawud 5110](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5110); al-Albani: isnad hasan).

Suppression research supports this design: people told not to think of a white bear thought of it more afterwards, but those given **a specific distracter** were less preoccupied later ([Wegner et al. 1987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3612492/)), and meta-analyses keep finding the rebound ([Wang et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32286932/)) (B). The texts run the same three steps as notice–name–return: notice ("whoever finds any of that"; [Muslim 134](https://sunnah.com/muslim:134)), name it mere whispering, as the Prophet ﷺ did ([Abu Dawud 5112](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:5112); al-Albani: sahih), and return by occupying oneself with something else. Two cautions keep the parallel honest. *Waswas* is unwanted while his fantasies are pleasant and sought, so the texts lift guilt about the pull without licensing the scene; and for MD the "something else" must be **the task itself**, not another fantasy or an open-ended spell of worship. Seeking refuge has never been tested as thought control.

### The Prophet ﷺ removed distractions instead of out-willing them

The most practical focus texts concern surroundings, not willpower. The Prophet ﷺ sent away a marked garment because it "has diverted my attention from the prayer" ([Bukhari 373](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:373)) and had a curtain removed because "its pictures are still displayed in front of me during my prayer" ([Bukhari 374](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:374)). He said there is no prayer when food is served or when one is "prompted by the call of nature" ([Muslim 560a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:560a)), told the drowsy to sleep "till he understands what he is saying" ([Bukhari 213](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:213)), warned against looking up or around in prayer ([Bukhari 750](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:750); [Bukhari 751](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:751)), told those praying toward a sutrah to keep close to it ([Abu Dawud 695](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:695); al-Albani: sahih), and stopped returning greetings because "prayer demands whole attention" ([Muslim 538a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:538a)). This is stimulus control, the method Somer recommends against music cues in MD ([Somer 2024](https://www.somer.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/somer-2024-calling-the-tune-in-maladaptive-daydreaming-the-impact-of-music-on-the-experience-of-compulsive-fantasizing.pdf)) (C).

The mechanisms have some support: an extreme urge to urinate cut attention and working memory by more than 0.8 standard deviations in eight people ([Lewis et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21058363/)) (C), and lack of sleep hits simple attention hardest (g = −0.776 across 70 articles; [Lim & Dinges 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438143/)) (A). The claim that a phone's mere presence drains capacity failed a pre-registered replication ([Ruiz Pardo & Minda 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36007374/)) (B), so the phone leaves because of its notifications and cues. If the Prophet ﷺ changed his surroundings rather than relying on resolve, moving the earbuds to another room follows the same logic and admits no weakness.

### Unbidden thoughts are forgiven; following them is the choice

The promise for one who prays two rak'ahs "during which he does not think of anything else" ([Bukhari 159](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:159)) can sound impossible with MD. Ibn Hajar narrows it to self-talk "the mind runs along with" and that "a person can cut off." He adds that "thoughts and whispers that rush in and are hard to repel are forgiven." He also reports al-Nawawi's view that the reward is still earned despite passing, unsettled thoughts ([Ibn Hajar, Fath al-Bari](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/52/123/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%88%D8%B6%D9%88%D8%A1-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7-%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D8%A7?idfrom=312&idto=313&start=1)). The Prophet ﷺ described Shaytan making the worshipper "remember things which he does not recall to his mind before the prayer" ([Bukhari 608](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:608)). He himself remembered, during 'Asr, some gold left at home, finished the prayer, then hurried to have it given away ([Bukhari 1221](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1221)). Ibn Kathir records 'Ata' b. Dinar's observation that 107:5 condemns those heedless *of* their prayer, not those absent-minded *in* it ([Ibn Kathir on 107](https://quran.com/107:1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)).

Psychology makes the same distinction. Minds wander in **46.9%** of sampled waking moments ([Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21071660/)), and more often during well-practised routines ([Mason et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17234951/)). Intentional and unintentional mind-wandering are distinct ([Seli et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27318437/)), and outcomes depend on noticing that the mind has drifted ([Smallwood & Schooler 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25293689/)) (B). For MD the chain is: a thought arrives unbidden (forgiven, and normal) → he notices it → he either builds it into a scene, the deliberate step MD is made of, or he returns. The decision point is therefore **noticing**, not the thought's arrival, and that is exactly the moment notice–name–return trains. Whether a particular distracted prayer is valid: ask a qualified scholar.

## Worship can turn into a compulsion or a hiding place

Religious obsessions are among the commonest OCD themes in clinics in Muslim-majority countries. Religious themes "predominated" among 32 Saudi patients ([Mahgoub & Abdel-Hafeiz 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1873636/)). And 42% of 45 Turkish patients had religious obsessions, though they were no more religious than the others ([Tek & Ulug 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11711164/)) (C). The weak point is guilt and the belief that thoughts matter and must be controlled, which explained the link between religiosity and obsessive symptoms in Turkish Muslims and Canadian Christians alike ([Inozu et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22484698/)) (C). The overlap with MD is large: **53.9% of 39 interview-diagnosed MDers had an obsessive-compulsive or related disorder** ([Somer, Soffer-Dudek & Ross 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28598955/)) (C). So "replace every daydream with dhikr" can slide into neutralising rituals, acts repeated to cancel out an unwanted thought. In Muslims these show up as repeated wudu, restarted prayers and mental checking ([IOCDF](https://iocdf.org/?p=66627)), and the warning signs are doing it for certainty or relief, needing it to feel "just right," time creeping up and distress when interrupted.

The Sunnah carries its own brakes. The worshipper who "imagines that he feels something" should not leave prayer "until he hears a sound or finds a smell" ([Bukhari 137](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:137)); one unsure whether he prayed three or four rak'ahs should "cast aside his doubt and base his prayer on what he is sure of" ([Muslim 571a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:571a)); the Prophet ﷺ foretold people "who transgress the limits in purification and in supplication" ([Abu Dawud 96](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:96); al-Albani: sahih); and he said "ruined are the extremists," whom al-Nawawi glosses as those who "exceed the limits in their words and deeds" ([Muslim 2670](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2670); [al-Nawawi](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2670)). These brief, one-off responses point the same way as exposure and response prevention (ERP), the first-line treatment for OCD. ERP has been adapted to religious values with gains in motivation and participation ([Huppert et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17828763/)) (B). In a survey of 64 imams, most did not recognise scrupulosity or know ERP ([Jones et al. 2019](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behaviour-change/article/imams-experience-with-and-response-to-mosquegoers-with-ocd-scrupulosity/93148705F336D0997856B3B1B09B2D6C)). The safest help is therefore an ERP-trained clinician who consults a qualified scholar. On how these texts apply to his own wudu or prayer: ask a qualified scholar.

The second risk is quieter. His daydreams start when work gets uncomfortable, a form of experiential avoidance (escaping unpleasant inner states), and MDers score high on this ([Pourmoazzen et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42051277/)) (C). If he leaves a hard edit for extra voluntary worship and does not come back, worship is doing the fantasy's job: discomfort → escape to something rewarding → relief → avoidance reinforced → the task still undone. Across 147 studies, negative religious coping, which includes "avoiding difficulties through religious activities," went with *more* depressive symptoms ([Smith et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12848223/)) (C). Comfort itself is not the issue, since the Prophet ﷺ said "O Bilal, call iqamah for prayer: give us comfort by it" ([Abu Dawud 4985](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4985); al-Albani: sahih). The test is **whether the act returns him to the task or replaces it**. One isti'adha and then the next sentence returns him. A third voluntary session that happens to outlast the edit replaces it. Muslim 2664's sequence ends in effort, not withdrawal.

How he reads his lapses matters too. Across 49 studies, positive religious coping went with better adjustment (r = .33), and negative coping, such as feeling punished or abandoned by God, with more distress (r = .22) ([Ano & Vasconcelles 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15503316/)) (C). Reading each episode as proof that "Allah is displeased with me" puts him on the risky side; the texts on forgiven thoughts offer the better reading. If the daydreaming is severe or comes with low mood, professional help comes first. People with suspected MD were 2.44 times as likely to have recently attempted suicide, even after accounting for distress ([Soffer-Dudek et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38061294/)) (C). Faith-adapted therapy matches standard therapy on symptoms while adding spiritual well-being ([Captari et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30221353/)) (B).

## Recitation can replace music's calm but not its ritual

No study has tested whether Qur'an recitation or nasheeds without instruments trigger imagery the way music does, so this section is inference throughout (D). In interviews, people with MD describe music and movement as near-essential, with fantasies "laced with emotionally compensatory themes involving competency, social recognition, and support" ([Somer et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26943233/)) (C). In general samples, a steady drum pulse made imagery more vivid ([Ayyildiz et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40846722/)), and heroic music produced "positive, exciting, constructive, and motivating" mind-wandering ([Koelsch et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31311967/)). Repeated pairing also turns tracks into learned cues ([Juslin 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23769678/)) (B).

Voice-only murattal has no drum pulse and none of the status and swagger that music genres carry. Where he understands its content, it points to Allah and accountability rather than fame. It keeps an emotional human voice, though, and nasheeds with "epic" delivery or vocal beat effects sit closer to music. Which nasheeds are permissible, and the ruling on music itself: ask a qualified scholar. Either way, removing the amplifier leaves the triggers in place: solitude, pacing and discomfort at work.

So recitation can take over music's calming role, but on its own it will not break the ritual: played through headphones while pacing, as phonk was, it keeps the ritual alive with a new soundtrack and may become a new cue. The better swap is behavioural: counted dhikr said while standing still or back at the desk, recitation from memory, which uses voice, breath and retrieval and is probably harder to daydream through than listening, and listening kept as its own attentive act. The texts model listening that way. The Prophet ﷺ asked Ibn Mas'ud to recite to him, and his eyes overflowed as he listened ([Bukhari 5050](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5050)). Ibn Kathir describes the Companions' hearts softening as "they would sit quietly" ([Ibn Kathir on 39:23](https://api.quran.com/api/v4/tafsirs/169/by_ayah/39:23)).

Background recitation while he writes is a separate, better-studied problem. Across 65 studies, noise, speech and music all had "a small but reliably detrimental effect on reading performance," with "intelligible speech and lyrical music" the most distracting ([Vasilev et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29958067/)) (A for the mechanism). Foreign speech barely disrupts sentence reading ([Hyönä et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27003410/)). But any irrelevant speech, even in an incomprehensible language, makes it harder to hold a sequence of words in mind ([Ueda et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31255145/)) (B). If he understands the Arabic, recitation behind a script should cost about what song lyrics cost (d ≈ −0.3; [Souza et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37152835/)). If he does not, it still makes it harder to memorise lines and hold a sentence in mind while drafting (D for recitation). Silence stays the default for scripting.

Silence also sidesteps a question of adab (proper conduct). Qur'an 7:204 commands, "when the Qur'ān is recited, then listen to it and pay attention." Ibn Kathir explains this as listening "in respect and honor of the Qur'an," emphasised in the obligatory prayer, before turning to the fiqh debate ([Ibn Kathir on 7:204](https://api.quran.com/api/v4/tafsirs/169/by_ayah/7:204); [Arabic](https://api.quran.com/api/v4/tafsirs/14/by_ayah/7:204)). Whether background recitation while working, scrolling or falling asleep fits that adab: ask a qualified scholar.

## A graded protocol built on five daily anchors

Everything here sits on top of the two components with randomised support in MD: notice–name–return, and an evening log of each episode's start time, trigger, minutes lost and response ([Herscu et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36634023/)) (A). The log gains one column: "worship act: returned me, or replaced the task?" One rule runs through all of it: a Sunnah-sized act, done once, then back to the task.

### Prayer times become if-then anchors

The most beloved deed is "prayer at its time" ([Bukhari 527](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:527)). Ibn Kathir quotes Qatadah on 23:9: "At the fixed times, with the proper bowing and prostration" ([Ibn Kathir on 23](https://quran.com/23:1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). That gives him five external cues a day. He plans work blocks between prayers and scripts their edges as if-then plans: "When the adhan goes, I save, write the next ten-minute step on paper, and make wudu," and "When I finish the salam and its adhkar, I start that step before touching my phone." The hard stretch of an edit now ends at a visible point, which bounds the discomfort that triggers him, and wudu becomes a reset (D). If-then plans are B, and pairing them with salah is D. A meta-analysis of breaks says to expect less fatigue rather than more output ([Albulescu et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36044424/)) (B). On preferred prayer times, late Isha or combining prayers: ask a qualified scholar.

### The urge moment gets a sixty-second script

When work turns uncomfortable and the pull starts, he notices it, names it ("the pull"), and says one short dhikr or one isti'adha, "I seek refuge with Allah from Satan," the words the Prophet ﷺ said would make an angry man's anger go ([Bukhari 3282](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3282)). Then he does the next two-minute step of the task and logs the episode that evening. Qur'an 7:201 frames the sequence: when "an impulse touches them from Satan, they remember [Him] and at once they have insight." Ibn Kathir applies this to an evil thought or anger crossing the mind ([Ibn Kathir on 7:201](https://api.quran.com/api/v4/tafsirs/169/by_ayah/7:201)). Noticing and logging are A, not suppressing is B, and dhikr as the act is D. He says it once per urge, not until the pull fades, and the "something else" is the task. If a success fantasy is running, he parks it for a five-minute written WOOP slot. He phrases the wish as an action, as in Bukhari 7232, and at the obstacle step adds Bukhari 6363's du'a seeking refuge from helplessness and laziness. WOOP itself rates B (g = 0.336; [Wang, Wang & Gai 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149892/)); the du'a's added effect is D. After a lapse, "Allah decreed, and what He willed He did" replaces "if only" (C). Saying dhikr aloud while drafting would compete with the inner voice that writing uses, so it belongs at transitions (D). Whether adhkar must be said aloud: ask a qualified scholar.

### Idle time gets its default before it arrives

Renders, queues and the minutes after finishing a task are where fantasy wins by default, so he picks the occupant in advance. The first choice is counted dhikr: "Subhan Allah wa bihamdihi" said 100 times a day brings forgiveness of sins "even if they were as much as the foam of the sea" ([Bukhari 6405](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6405)), and a man who found the laws of Islam too many was told, "Let not your tongue cease to be moist with the remembrance of Allah" ([Tirmidhi 3375](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:3375); al-Albani and Ahmad Shakir: sahih; Bashar 'Awwad Ma'ruf: hasan). The alternatives are a walk without audio, muraja'ah of what he has memorised, or a two-minute task from his idea list. He does these seated or standing still, because pacing with earbuds was the old ritual. Idle time as MD territory is C, pre-planned if-then defaults are B, and dhikr reducing idle-time MD is D.

### Bedtime runs on a script that ends with the last words

His worst window gets the Sunnah's most detailed routine, all from Bukhari, in a practical order of which the text fixes only the last item. After Isha there is no idle talk and no screen, since the Prophet ﷺ disliked talk after it ([Bukhari 568](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:568)); phone and earbuds stay out of the bedroom, and he makes wudu. In bed he recites Ayat al-Kursi ([Bukhari 2311](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2311)) and the last two verses of al-Baqarah ([Bukhari 5009](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5009)), blows the three Quls into cupped hands and wipes them over his body ([Bukhari 5017](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5017)), and says Subhan Allah 33 times, al-hamdu lillah 33 and Allahu akbar 34 ([Bukhari 3705](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3705)). Last, on his right side, comes "Allahumma aslamtu wajhi ilaika…", with the instruction "let these words be the last you say" ([Bukhari 6311](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6311)). If he wakes in the night, the remembrance in Bukhari 1154 replaces picking up a scene ([Bukhari 1154](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1154)). Counted, spoken items may occupy the inner voice that narrative fantasy relies on. That idea has only uncontrolled support in insomnia ([Levey et al. 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2012592/)), and techniques for managing thoughts before sleep show mixed results generally ([Lemyre et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31918338/)) (C/D). Some imagery while falling asleep is normal; the aim is to stop building scenes on purpose. He says the script once, as worship, not over and over until it feels right. Protecting sleep protects attention (A). On Isha or Fajr timing at high latitudes: ask a qualified scholar.

### Focus in prayer is trained through body, eyes, mind and meaning

The khushu' scale's four factors suggest a frame of body, eyes, mind and meaning (D). For the **body**, he deals with the toilet and hunger first ([Muslim 560a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:560a)), protects sleep, and settles fully into each posture, as the man told "go back and pray, for you have not prayed" was taught ([Bukhari 757](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:757)). For the **eyes**, a plain space, a sutrah, and the phone silenced and out of sight. For the **mind**, the first drift is forgiven; he catches the moment it becomes a scene and returns to the next phrase. Persistent whispers get the taught response of seeking refuge and dry-spitting to the left three times ([Muslim 2203a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2203a)), whispers that al-Nawawi says rob the worshipper of "a free mind for khushu'" ([al-Nawawi on Muslim 2203](https://hadithunlocked.com/muslim:2203a)). Doubts over three or four rak'ahs get Muslim 571's rule, and a worldly task that surfaces is noted straight after the salam, as in Bukhari 1221. For **meaning**, he learns what al-Fatiha and his regular surahs say, since Allah answers each verse of al-Fatiha ([Muslim 395a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:395a)) and the Prophet ﷺ glorified, asked and sought refuge as each verse called for ([Muslim 772](https://sunnah.com/muslim:772)); he varies surahs because practised routines invite drift. Sleep is A, bodily needs C, returning rather than suppressing B, and the visual field and meanings D; with no khushu'-training trial in existence, he counts returns, not perfect prayers. On the validity of a distracted prayer, sutrah details, the spitting gesture in congregation, sujud al-sahw and drowsiness in obligatory prayers: ask a qualified scholar.

### Learn like a hafiz: new, recent, old, out loud

The hifz tradition runs three tracks: **sabaq**, the new lesson recited until fluent without the text; **sabqi**, recent portions revised daily; and **manzil**, older portions in rotation, such as a seventh a day ([Indonesian boarding-school study](https://ejournal.iainpalopo.ac.id/index.php/jiis/article/view/12297); [Quran Sheikh](https://www.quransheikh.com/quran-hifz-revision-schedule/)). That matches the best tools in learning science (A for learning). Recall beats rereading ([Rowland 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150680/)), and spaced review beats cramming, with gaps that grow as material ages ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/)). Corrective feedback strengthens testing (g = 0.499; [Yang et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33683913/)). And drilling to fluency fades within weeks without review ([Rohrer et al. 2005](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1772/)).

The texts describe the forgetting: memorised Qur'an runs off like camels set loose ([Bukhari 5031](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5031)). They describe the long review: Jibril recited the Qur'an with the Prophet ﷺ yearly, and twice in his final year ([Bukhari 4998](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4998)). And they promise a double reward to the one who "recites it with great difficulty" ([Bukhari 4937](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4937)), which reframes the very discomfort that triggers him.

He can use the model twice. Muraja'ah is an effortful, spoken default for idle time and for the minutes before the bedtime adhkar (D). The three tracks also fit his craft: one new hook technique a day, a daily review of last week's, and a rotation of older skills, each tested by doing rather than rewatched. He should expect no bigger memory, since huffaz show none, only the skill he rehearses ([Black et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32870071/)).

## Conclusion

The most useful thing the texts add is not a technique science lacks. It is a stance that MD treatment struggles to hold: the unbidden thought carries no guilt, while following it is a choice he can still reverse at the moment of noticing. Mercy about the pull and accountability for the scene are the balance between self-compassion and change that his course asks for, here in words he already trusts. The second addition is structural. No secular protocol can install five external, lifelong, non-negotiable cues a day. The prayer timetable does, and the bedtime adhkar give his worst window a script with a fixed last line.

Nobody knows whether any of this changes MD. Until someone runs that trial, his own log is the only evidence there will be, comparing minutes lost on days with and without the anchors. For the video, the honest line is "consistent with the science," never "proven by it." The Sunnah's guidance on distraction works mainly through surroundings and responses, the levers that the MD trial and the suppression research favour over willpower. But no one has yet tested salah, dhikr or recitation against a wandering mind.
